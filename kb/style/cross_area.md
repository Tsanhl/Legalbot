# Questions that span several areas of law ({areas})

- Identify each area at the start and say how they connect (e.g. the same facts give a claim in contract and in tort).
- Deal with each area under its own heading, applying that area's own tests and authorities; never borrow a test from
  one area into another (e.g. remoteness in contract is not the tort test).
- Where the areas overlap, say which route is better for the client and why (limitation, remedies, burden, defences).
- Where one area's rules above conflict with another's on presentation, follow the area the question mainly concerns.
- Finish with one combined conclusion or advice, not separate disconnected ones.

## Learned from testing
- Check whether a further area of law also governs the same term or issue, even if the question does not mention it (e.g. consumer protection alongside jurisdiction), and deal with it under its own heading. [2026-11-22 · ref 2026-11-22-1855 (see that run's log and grades)]
- Before the analysis that depends on it, resolve any threshold issue that links the parts of a question (e.g. whether a contract or a disputed term exists at all), and state how its answer affects each part. [2026-11-24 · ref 2026-11-22-1301/q21,q29]
- For each obligation, identify who owes it and to whom, and direct each remedy at that party; never treat a duty arising in one relationship as binding a non-party in another. [2026-11-24 · ref 2026-11-22-1301/q21,q29]
