# Free movement of workers - Article 45 TFEU, the concept of "worker" and retention of status

## Overview
Article 45 TFEU secures free movement for workers and prohibits nationality discrimination in employment. Who counts as a "worker" is decisive: workers have an unconditional right of residence (no sufficient resources or sickness insurance requirement) and full equal treatment, including social and tax advantages, whereas economically inactive citizens must satisfy Directive 2004/38's conditions and face limits on social assistance (*Dano*). The Court defines "worker" as an autonomous EU concept, broadly, including part-timers and low earners, provided the work is "genuine and effective". After the citizenship retreat, the work/inactivity boundary has become even more important (O'Brien). Jurisdiction: EU law.

## The law

### 1. Article 45 TFEU and secondary law
- Art 45(1): "Freedom of movement for workers shall be secured within the Union."
- Art 45(2): abolition of any discrimination based on nationality as regards employment, remuneration and other conditions of work and employment.
- Art 45(3): subject to public policy, public security and public health, the right (a) to accept offers of employment actually made; (b) to move freely within Member States for this purpose; (c) to stay in a Member State for employment; (d) to remain after employment.
- Art 45(4): does not apply to employment in the public service.
- Secondary law: **Regulation 492/2011** (consolidating Reg 1612/68) and **Directive 2004/38**.

### 2. "Worker" is an EU concept, interpreted broadly
- *Hoekstra* (75/63): "worker" has a Community meaning; otherwise States could narrow it to exclude other nationals. (The ruling concerned "wage-earner or assimilated worker" in social security Regulation 3, which, like "workers" in the Treaty, has a Community meaning.)
- *Levin* (53/81): otherwise Art 45's scope would vary between States; as a fundamental freedom the concept cannot be interpreted restrictively.
- Distinguish from nationality, which is a national concept.

### 3. The definition: Lawrie-Blum
- *Lawrie-Blum* (66/85) para 17 (quoted as para [7]): "The essential feature of an employment relationship ... is that for a certain period of time a person performs services for and under the direction of another person in return for which he receives remuneration."
- Three elements: (i) a period of time; (ii) services under the direction of another (subordination); (iii) remuneration.
- Held: a trainee teacher giving lessons under the direction and supervision of school authorities, receiving remuneration, is a worker irrespective of the legal nature of the relationship; and the preparatory service is not "public service" under Art 45(4).
- **Without subordination**, the person is self-employed: Art 49 (establishment) or Art 56 (services) (see *Jany* C-268/99 in `freedom-of-establishment.md`).

### 4. Genuine and effective, not marginal and ancillary
1. **Part-time work**: *Levin* (53/81) (a part-time chambermaid earning less than the subsistence minimum): EU law covers someone whose income is below the minimum required for subsistence, whether or not supplemented by other income, provided the activity is **effective and genuine**. The motives for seeking work elsewhere are irrelevant. The discussion quotes the reasoning: part-time work is for many an effective means of improving living conditions, so limiting the freedom to full-timers earning at least the minimum wage would impair its effectiveness. The activity must not be on "such a small scale as to be purely marginal and ancillary" (also *Ninni-Orasche* C-413/01).
2. **Supplementing income from public funds** does not matter: *Kempf* (139/85) (part-time music teacher claiming social assistance).
3. **Few hours / on-call**: *Raulin* (C-357/89) (waitress on an on-call contract with no guaranteed hours).
4. **Amount and form of remuneration immaterial**: *Lawrie-Blum* (trainee teacher paid below a qualified teacher's starting salary); *Steymann* (196/87): work within a religious community's commercial activities, where the community provides members' material needs, is economic activity because the services provided may be regarded as an indirect quid pro quo for genuine and effective work.
5. **Normal labour market test**: *Trojani* (C-456/02): a French national did about 30 hours a week of jobs for the Salvation Army in Brussels, under its direction, as part of a personal socio-occupational reintegration programme, in return for board, lodging and pocket money. Held: he could claim residence as a worker only if the activity is "real and genuine", for the national court; the activity must be capable of being regarded as forming part of the **normal labour market**, taking account of the hostel's status and practices, the content of the reintegration programme and the nature of the services. (Further: he was not self-employed or a service provider; but as a citizen lawfully resident with a residence permit he could rely on Art 12 EC (now 18 TFEU) to obtain the minimex: see `citizenship-equal-treatment-and-social-assistance.md`.)
6. **Purpose of the work** may show it is marginal and ancillary: *Bettray* (344/87).
7. **Overall assessment** of the employment relationship: *Genc* (C-14/09, para 26).

### 5. Retaining worker status: Article 7(3) Directive 2004/38
A citizen who is no longer working keeps worker status if:
1. temporarily unable to work because of **illness or accident**;
2. in duly recorded **involuntary unemployment after more than one year** of employment and registered as a job-seeker;
3. in duly recorded involuntary unemployment after a **fixed-term contract of less than a year**, or after becoming involuntarily unemployed **during the first 12 months**, and registered as a job-seeker: status retained for **no less than six months** (Art 7(3)(c), as explained in *Alimanovic* para 53);
4. embarking on **vocational training**, which must be related to the previous employment unless the person is involuntarily unemployed.
- Why it matters: retained workers need not satisfy sufficient resources/sickness insurance, and keep full equal treatment. *Vatsouras* (para 32, cited in *Alimanovic* para 54): those who retain status under Art 7(3)(c) are entitled to social assistance during that period. In *Alimanovic*, once the six months had expired the claimants were no longer workers (para 55).
- **Beyond Art 7(3)**: *Saint Prix* (C-507/12): a woman who gives up work, or seeking work, because of the physical constraints of late pregnancy and the aftermath of childbirth retains worker status under **Art 45 TFEU**, provided she returns to work or finds another job within a reasonable period after the birth. The Court relied on Art 45 itself, so the list in Art 7(3) is not exhaustive (the discussion asks why the case is significant).

### 6. Other persons protected by Article 45
- **Job-seekers** (first-time): within Art 45 but with a "semi-status" (see `jobseekers-and-access-to-benefits.md`).
- **Employers**: *Clean Car* (C-350/96): an employer can rely on Art 45 to employ workers from other Member States; a rule that the owner of an undertaking may not appoint a non-resident as manager breaches Art 45. Otherwise States could exclude foreign workers by regulating employers.
- **Frontier workers**: work in one State, live in another.
- **Returning workers**: own nationals returning after exercising Treaty rights, who may bring family members as a migrant would: *Singh* (C-370/90). But purely internal situations are excluded: *Morson and Jhanjan* (35 and 36/82).
- **Family members** of workers: derived rights (see `workers-rights-and-obstacles.md`).

### 7. Rights flowing from worker status (summary)
- Leave, enter, stay and move (Directive 2004/38); residence conditional only on proof of employment; permanent residence possibly before five years (Art 17).
- Equal treatment (Art 45(2), Art 18 TFEU, Art 24(1) Dir 2004/38, Reg 492/2011 incl. Art 7(2) social and tax advantages).
- Protection against non-discriminatory barriers (*Bosman*).
- Details in `workers-rights-and-obstacles.md`.

## Debate and critique
- **Teleological breadth**: The conclusion: Art 45 is interpreted teleologically and broadly, covering part-timers and job-seekers. Including part-timers on low wages "has real implications" because it opens national welfare systems to claims by EU nationals.
- **Weak guidance and non-compliance**: The discussion notes there is not much guidance on the EU concepts ("genuine and effective", "marginal and ancillary"), and Member States do not seem very compliant, applying their own thresholds.
- **O'Brien, "Civis Capitalist Sum" (2016) 53 CML Rev 937**: as the last traces of citizenship disappear (after *Dano*), the work/inactivity boundary becomes critical; the Court's growing tolerance of nationality discrimination creates a "moral vacuum" which States exploit by imposing their own definitions of work (e.g. minimum earnings thresholds). Low-income and insecure workers, especially with zero-hours contracts, risk exclusion; free movement's benefits are reserved for the better resourced; class becomes the new guiding principle. Domestic distortions then feed back into EU law.
- **Saint Prix as a gender-sensitive extension**: the Court used Art 45 to protect women's mobility, reading Art 7(3) as non-exhaustive; contrast with the formalism of *Alimanovic* on job-seekers.
- **Questions**: Should free movement of workers apply only to those already offered a job? (Art 45(3)(a) refers to offers "actually made", but *Royer*/*Antonissen* extend it to job-seekers.) Do workers have more extensive rights than citizens relying on Art 21? (Yes: unconditional residence and full equal treatment.)

## Applying it to a problem question
1. **Is the person a worker?** Apply *Lawrie-Blum*: period, subordination, remuneration. Then "genuine and effective" (*Levin*): hours, regularity, duration, pay (even low or in kind: *Steymann*), whether part of the normal labour market (*Trojani*), overall assessment (*Genc*). Low income and social assistance top-ups do not matter (*Kempf*).
2. **If not currently working**, does status survive? Art 7(3) categories (illness, involuntary unemployment over/under a year with registration, vocational training) and *Saint Prix* (pregnancy, reasonable period).
3. **If never worked**, is the person a job-seeker (Art 14(4)(b); *Antonissen*)? If neither, they are economically inactive: Art 7(1)(b) conditions.
4. **Self-employed?** If no subordination: Art 49/56.
5. **Other claimants**: employer challenging rules on hiring (*Clean Car*); frontier worker; returning national (*Singh*), but not a purely internal case (*Morson*).
6. **Consequences**: residence and equal treatment rights; then go to `workers-rights-and-obstacles.md`.
Typical traps: treating low pay as fatal; ignoring retained status; forgetting the six-month limit for short-term workers under Art 7(3)(c) (*Alimanovic*).
