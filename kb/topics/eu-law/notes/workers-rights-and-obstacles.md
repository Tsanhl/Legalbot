# Workers' rights - non-discrimination, Regulation 492/2011, social advantages, non-discriminatory obstacles, public service and family

## Overview
Once someone is a worker (see `workers-concept-and-status.md`), Article 45 TFEU and Regulation 492/2011 give them: rights to leave, enter, stay and move; a right not to be discriminated against on grounds of nationality (directly or indirectly) in everything connected with employment, including social and tax advantages; protection against genuinely non-discriminatory obstacles to access to the employment market (*Bosman*); and derived rights for family members, notably children's education. Limits are the Treaty derogations (Art 45(3)), the public service exception (Art 45(4)) and objective justification for indirect discrimination and obstacles. Jurisdiction: EU law.

## The law

### 1. Right to leave, enter, stay and move
- As under Directive 2004/38 (see `citizens-rights-directive-residence-and-family.md`). For workers, residence is conditional only on proof of employment; permanent residence may be gained before five years in the cases in Art 17 (Handout 3; the materials).
- Restrictions on entry and expulsion must be justified by a Treaty derogation, "the very negation of Article 45" otherwise; procedural guarantees and fundamental rights must be respected; no economic aims (Handout 3).

### 2. Direct discrimination
- Discrimination in law: the rule distinguishes by nationality (e.g. only Italians may teach Italian literature). Always forbidden unless (i) a Treaty derogation applies, or (ii) the public service exception covers the post (Handout 3).
- **Treaty derogations** (Art 45(3); Dir 2004/38 Arts 27-33): public policy, public security, public health; narrowly construed and unlikely to succeed: *Commission v Spain* (C-114/97) (requirement that private security staff, e.g. private investigators, watchmen, bodyguards, be Spanish). Even if a derogation applies, proportionality is required (the materials). Derogations cannot serve economic aims (whereas imperative requirements can be invoked when a rule also, but not only, pursues an economic aim) (Handout 3).

### 3. Public service exception: Article 45(4)
- "Public service" is an EU concept, not dependent on national labels or on the post being governed by public law. The Court takes a **functional** rather than institutional approach: *Commission v Belgium* (149/79): posts involving (1) direct or indirect participation in the exercise of powers conferred by public law and (2) duties designed to safeguard the general interests of the State or other public authorities. These presume a special relationship of allegiance and reciprocity of rights and duties forming the foundation of the bond of nationality. Assessed case by case (the materials; Handout 3; second judgment in *Commission v Belgium* [1982] ECR 1845 for examples).
- *Lawrie-Blum* (66/85): a trainee teacher's preparatory service is not public service under Art 45(4).

### 4. Indirect discrimination
- Discrimination in fact: neutral on its face but affecting foreigners or migrants more than nationals. Example: requiring an Italian degree to teach Italian literature.
- Broad definition in *O'Flynn* (C-237/94, para 18): conditions are indirectly discriminatory where, although applicable irrespective of nationality, "they affect essentially migrant workers ... or the great majority of those affected are migrant workers ..., where they are indistinctly applicable but can more easily be satisfied by national workers than by migrant workers ... or where there is a risk that they may operate to the particular detriment of migrant workers".
- **Justification**: compatible only if objectively justified, i.e. pursuing an imperative requirement of public interest, and necessary (least restrictive means) and proportionate (Handout 3; the materials).
- **Examples** (Handout 3):
  - rules connected with nationality: advantages for those who did military service in the host State: *Ugliola* (15/69);
  - territorial elements: residence requirements (*Sotgiu* 152/73); requiring a benefit to be used in the territory (*O'Flynn*); qualification requirements, analysed as indirect discrimination or a double burden but requiring justification (*Vlassopoulou* C-340/89); counting periods of work in the domestic but not another State's civil service (*Schöning* C-15/96);
  - habitual residence for jobseeker's allowance (*Collins* para 65);
  - residence of managers (*Clean Car* C-350/96);
  - **language requirements**: *Groener* (379/87): a Dutch teacher refused a permanent post in Ireland for not speaking Irish; the requirement could be justified as part of a policy promoting Irish as an expression of national culture and identity, provided it is applied proportionately; see also Art 3 Reg 492/2011 (the materials).
  - A fluency requirement for teaching in the language of instruction is necessary and proportionate (Handout 3 example).

### 5. Scope of equal treatment: Regulation 492/2011
Non-discrimination applies to everything connected, even remotely, with the economic activity (Handout 3), including:
- access to employment (Arts 3, 4, 6);
- assistance from employment offices (Art 5);
- conditions of employment (Art 7(1));
- **social and tax advantages (Art 7(2))**;
- vocational training (Art 7(3));
- nullity of discriminatory clauses in collective or individual agreements (Art 7(4));
- trade union membership (Art 8);
- housing (Art 9);
- children's education (Art 10).

### 6. Social and tax advantages: Article 7(2)
- Text: a worker who is a national of a Member State "shall enjoy the same social and tax advantages as national workers".
- **Broad interpretation**: covers advantages not attached to the employment contract, if generally granted to national workers primarily because of their objective status as workers, or by virtue of residence in the territory, or if they encourage mobility: *Reina* (65/81) (interest-free childbirth loan).
- **Family members**: advantages directly benefiting family members (only those within the Directive's definition: *Lebon* 316/85) but indirectly benefiting the worker are covered: *Cristini* (32/75) (rail fare reduction card for large families, even when claimed after the worker's death by the family remaining in the host State); *Inzirillo* (63/76) (disability allowance for a worker's son); *Reed* (59/85) (if nationals' unmarried stable partners may reside, the same must be allowed to migrant workers as a social advantage).
- **Not covered**: benefits linked to the special bond of nationality: *Even* (207/78) (Belgian arrangements for pensioners with a Second World War service invalidity pension; their essential objective was to reward hardship suffered for the country); *de Vos* (C-315/94) (employer's obligation to keep paying contributions during military service).
- **Work-seekers** cannot rely on Art 7(2): *Lebon*; and after *Alimanovic* little social assistance is available to them (see `jobseekers-and-access-to-benefits.md`).
- *Jobcenter Krefeld v JD* (C-181/19): the Court examined how Art 7(2) interacts with Dir 2004/38 and Art 24(2) for someone whose residence is based on Art 10 Reg 492/2011; Art 10 includes a right of residence (including for the primary carer), even after loss of worker status, and opens access to Art 7(2) equal treatment for benefits (Handout 3; the materials).
- Tax advantages: there is no equivalent to Art 7(2) for the self-employed, but the Court applies Art 49 similarly (see `freedom-of-establishment.md`).

### 7. Non-discriminatory obstacles (market access)
- Rules that are neither directly nor indirectly discriminatory but **affect access to the employment market** or the worker's ability to move are caught by Art 45 unless justified by an imperative requirement and proportionate (Handout 3; the materials).
- *Bosman* (C-415/93): football transfer fee rules for out-of-contract players "directly affect players' access to the employment market in other Member States and are thus capable of impeding" free movement.
- *Lehtonen* (C-176/96): basketball rules barring clubs from fielding players transferred after a deadline: that the rules concern fielding rather than employment is irrelevant, since restricting participation restricts employment chances.
- **Not caught**:
  - rules **inherent in the organisation** of the activity: *Deliège* (C-51/96 and C-191/97) (selection rules for international sporting competitions);
  - rules whose effect is **too remote and indirect**: *Graf* (C-190/98): compensation on dismissal depended on a future and hypothetical event (termination not at the worker's initiative), so it did not deter a worker from leaving to work elsewhere.

### 8. Against whom? (summary)
- Vertically: host State and home State (exit and return restrictions; *Singh*); not purely internal situations (*Morson and Jhanjan*).
- Semi-horizontally: collective regulation (*Walrave and Koch* 36/74; *Bosman*).
- Horizontally: private employers may not discriminate: *Angonese* (C-281/98) (bilingualism certificate obtainable only in Bolzano); cf Art 7(4) Reg 492/2011 (discriminatory clauses in collective or individual agreements "null and void").
- See `horizontal-direct-effect-of-free-movement.md`.

### 9. The worker's family
- Family members (as defined in Dir 2004/38, EU or third-country nationals) derive rights to reside and work.
- **Children's education: Art 10 Reg 492/2011**: "The children of a national of a Member State who is or has been employed in the territory of another Member State shall be admitted to that State's general educational, apprenticeship and vocational training courses under the same conditions as the nationals of that State, if such children are residing in its territory."
  - Generous reading: includes non-dependent children over 21 (*Gaal* C-7/94); a broad range of courses, including rehabilitation for disabled children (*Michel S* 76/72); funding and conditions of education (*Casagrande* 9/74).
  - Continues when the worker has left or stopped working, or the family has broken down (*Echternach and Moritz* 389 and 390/87); the parent-carer derives a right to stay from the child's right to education (*Baumbast and R* C-413/99); *Jobcenter Krefeld* (C-181/19).
- Handout 3 conclusion: spouse and children under 21 or dependent have a right to reside and work; children have a right to education; other family members only a right to reside.

## Debate and critique
- **From discrimination to market access**: *Bosman* extended Art 45 to non-discriminatory barriers, paralleling *Gebhard* for establishment and services. Snell (2010) cites *Bosman* and *Graf* as market access cases, and argues the notion conceals the choice between anti-discrimination and economic freedom. Spaventa ("From Gebhard to Carpenter", 2004) notes that while Keck curtailed goods, the persons provisions moved to a non-discrimination-free assessment, which raises questions about the reach of EU law into national regulation; limits like *Deliège* (inherent rules) and *Graf* (too remote) are the persons-equivalent of exclusionary rules.
- **Bosman's human story**: the materials notes that Bosman's own life after the ruling was an unhappy one, a reminder that landmark litigation has personal costs.
- **Breadth of social advantages**: the "objective status as worker or residence" test makes Art 7(2) a broad social integration tool, but it is unavailable to work-seekers; combined with *Alimanovic*, the line between workers and non-workers becomes decisive (O'Brien; see `workers-concept-and-status.md`).
- **National identity and language** (*Groener*): an example of accommodating cultural policy within free movement, subject to proportionality.
- **Public service exception**: the functional approach means a post is not excluded merely because the State labels it public or governs it by public law (Handout 3); *Lawrie-Blum* shows teaching training posts fall outside.

## Applying it to a problem question
the materials problem 1 (a Dutch designer applying for a part-time design post in an Austrian government department; refused because (a) only a limited number of foreign nationals are recruited, (b) his Italian is not good enough, (c) some work needs access to military installations; he and his French wife are told to leave):
1. **Status**: is he a job-seeker (Art 14(4)(b), *Antonissen*)? Part-time work counts if genuine (*Levin*). His wife: family member (spouse, Art 2(2) Dir 2004/38), and also a Union citizen in her own right.
2. **(a) Quota on foreign nationals**: direct discrimination; only Art 45(3) derogations (unlikely, *Commission v Spain*) or Art 45(4).
3. **(b) Language**: indirect discrimination (*O'Flynn*); justified only if the requirement pursues a legitimate aim and is proportionate (*Groener*; see Art 3 Reg 492/2011); and why Italian in Austria? Probe suitability and necessity.
4. **(c) Military installations**: Art 45(4) public service exception, functional test (*Commission v Belgium* 149/79): does the post involve exercise of public law powers and safeguarding State interests? If only some tasks do, a blanket exclusion is disproportionate. Alternatively public security derogation (personal conduct needed).
5. **Expulsion**: job-seekers cannot be removed while they show continuing search and genuine chances (Art 14(4)(b)); public policy requires personal conduct (Art 27). Wife's right depends on his status or her own (Art 7).
General checklist for other problems: identify the rule; classify as direct, indirect or obstacle; check exclusions (*Deliège*, *Graf*); justification and proportionality; who is the defendant (State, collective body, private employer); social advantages under Art 7(2) (status as worker, family member eligibility, nationality-linked exceptions); children's education rights under Art 10.
