# Freedom of establishment - Articles 49-55 TFEU, discrimination, Gebhard restrictions, CaixaBank and social dumping

## Overview
Article 49 TFEU gives nationals of a Member State (and, via Article 54, companies) the right to set up and pursue self-employed activity or manage undertakings in another Member State on a stable and continuous basis. It sits between workers (Art 45: subordination) and services (Art 56: temporary activity). The law has moved from prohibiting discrimination to catching any national measure "liable to hinder or make less attractive" establishment (*Gebhard*), which raises the core essay question in the materials: was it right for the Court to "step beyond discrimination" in establishment and services? This note covers natural persons and general principles; company mobility is in `company-mobility.md`. Jurisdiction: EU law.

## The law

### 1. Treaty provisions
- **Art 49 TFEU**: restrictions on establishment of nationals of a Member State in another are prohibited, including restrictions on setting up **agencies, branches or subsidiaries**. Establishment includes taking up and pursuing activities as self-employed persons and setting up and managing undertakings, "under the conditions laid down for its own nationals by the law of the country where such establishment is effected".
- **Primary establishment** (setting up and managing undertakings) and **secondary establishment** (agencies, branches, subsidiaries) (the materials).
- **Art 51 TFEU**: no application to activities connected, even occasionally, with the exercise of **official authority**.
- **Art 52 TFEU**: special treatment for foreign nationals on grounds of public policy, public security or public health.
- **Art 54 TFEU**: companies formed under a Member State's law with registered office, central administration or principal place of business in the EU are treated like natural persons.
- **Art 62 TFEU** applies Arts 51-52 to services.

### 2. Personal scope
1. **Self-employed, not workers**: Art 45 covers activity under subordination; Art 49 covers activity outside subordination: *Jany* (C-268/99) (Handout 4).
2. **Establishment, not services**: *Gebhard* (C-55/94): establishment is "a very broad" concept allowing a national "to participate, on a stable and continuous basis, in the economic life of a Member State other than his State of origin and to profit therefrom" (para 25), whereas a service provider pursues activity in the host State on a **temporary** basis (para 26). Temporariness depends on **duration, regularity, periodicity and continuity**; a service provider may have some infrastructure (office, chambers) if needed for the services (para 27). Mr Gebhard, a German lawyer practising on a stable and continuous basis in Milan from an established professional base, holding himself out to Italian nationals, came under establishment (para 28); being established does not require membership of the host professional body (paras 29-30).
3. **Natural persons** must be nationals of a Member State; **legal persons** depend on the connecting factor set by national law (incorporation vs real seat; *Cartesio* C-210/06) (the materials; Handout 4). See `company-mobility.md`.
4. *Trojani* (C-456/02): a person doing reintegration work in a hostel is not within Arts 43 or 49 EC (now 49, 56 TFEU).

### 3. Scope and direct effect
- **No purely internal situations**: need an intra-Union element; but returning nationals are covered: *Kraus* (C-19/92) (German national with a UK LLM; German rules requiring authorisation to use the title) (the materials).
- **Vertical, semi-horizontal and horizontal**: same principles as Art 45: Art 49 can be relied on against States, professional associations, discriminatory private rules, and trade union action to stop a business relocating: *Viking* (C-438/05) (Handout 4; the materials).

### 4. Rights: leave, enter, stay; family; retention of status
- As in Directive 2004/38 for natural persons; for companies the case law is more complex (Handout 4).
- Family members: Directive 2004/38.
- Retention of self-employed status: Art 7(3) Dir 2004/38, as for workers.
- Derogations: Art 52 and Dir 2004/38 Arts 27-33.

### 5. Direct discrimination
- Prohibited; justifiable only by Treaty derogations (Art 52; public policy, security, health), narrowly interpreted, and must be proportionate and compatible with fundamental rights; or by Art 51 official authority (the materials; Handout 4).
- **Official authority (Art 51)**: Union meaning, narrow interpretation: *Reyners* (2/74): exclusion of non-nationals is limited to activities which, taken on their own, have "a direct and specific connection with the exercise of official authority". Not covered: traffic-accident experts (*Commission v Greece* C-306/89); commissioners of insurance companies (*Thijssen* C-42/92).

### 6. Indirect discrimination
- Broadly construed, as for workers: neutral rules likely to affect foreigners more, unless justified by an imperative requirement (or a Treaty derogation) and proportionate.
- Examples: prior residence requirements (*Commission v Luxembourg* C-111/91: childbirth and maternity allowances); qualification requirements (*Vlassopoulou* C-340/89) (the materials; Handout 4).

### 7. Tax and social advantages
- No equivalent of Art 7(2) Reg 492/2011 for the self-employed, but Art 49's non-discrimination principle covers tax and social advantages: *Steinhauser* (197/84) (nationality condition for renting business premises from a public authority); *Commission v Italy* (C-63/86) (preferential access to social housing and reduced-rate mortgage loans). Now also Art 24(1) Dir 2004/38 (the materials; Handout 4).

### 8. More than one establishment
- Art 49 covers being established in more than one Member State, e.g. working part of the week in each: *Klopp* (107/83) (a German national wanting to practise as an avocat in Paris while keeping chambers in Germany). For companies, secondary establishment through agencies, branches or subsidiaries (the materials; Handout 4).

### 9. Non-discriminatory barriers: the Gebhard formula
- *Gebhard* (C-55/94) para 37: "national measures liable to hinder or make less attractive the exercise of fundamental freedoms guaranteed by the Treaty must fulfil four conditions: they must be applied in a non-discriminatory manner; they must be justified by imperative requirements in the general interest; they must be suitable for securing the attainment of the objective which they pursue; and they must not go beyond what is necessary in order to attain it" (citing *Kraus*). Para 38: States must take account of knowledge and qualifications already acquired in another State. Facts (the materials notes): Mr Gebhard faced disciplinary action for using the title "avvocato" without being enrolled in the Italian bar.
- Examples of rules regulating a profession or activity caught: *Mac Quen* (C-108/96), *Gräbner* (C-294/00), *Payroll Data Services* (C-79/01) (Handout 4).
- *Centros* para 34 applies the same four conditions to company formation rules.
- **CaixaBank France** (C-442/02): French rules prohibited paying remuneration on "sight" current accounts in euros. CaixaBank France, a French subsidiary of a Spanish bank, argued this obstructed effective and profitable banking. The Court: the rate paid on sight accounts is a method of competing with domestic banks; the prohibition "hinders credit institutions which are subsidiaries of foreign companies in raising capital from the public, by depriving them of the possibility of competing more effectively, by paying remuneration on sight accounts, with the credit institutions traditionally established in the Member State of establishment, which have an extensive network of branches" (the materials).
- **AG Tizzano in CaixaBank**: warned that reading "hinder or make less attractive" to cover any measure that narrows profit margins would let operators challenge any regulation of economic activity (para 62), "bending the Treaty" to create "a market without rules" in which rules are prohibited in principle unless justified (para 63). He proposed that, where non-discrimination is respected in law and fact, a measure is a restriction only if, in light of its purpose and effects, it **directly affects market access** (para 66), drawing on *Alpine Investments* and *Graf* (para 67) and the Keck rationale combining market access and discrimination (paras 70-73). His proposed answer: such rules are restrictions if they place the operator in less favourable conditions of law and fact than an established operator or otherwise directly affect market access; the sight-account ban would be one if it deprived foreign banks' subsidiaries of the possibility of competing effectively with banks with extensive networks, for the national court; and on the facts he found it not justified by consumer protection or encouragement of saving.
- **Not caught if too uncertain and indirect**: *Casa Uno* (C-418/93): Italian rule requiring shops to close on Sundays and public holidays; any restrictive effects were "too uncertain and indirect" (the materials).
- the materials asks: what are the problems with a non-discrimination-based approach? (And with abandoning it?)

### 10. Services Directive 2006/123
- Applies to establishment as well as services; covered in a separate lecture/handout which is not among these sources (Handouts 4-5 refer to it). Klamert notes its Art 15(7) notification duty for "suspect" requirements under establishment (see `internal-market-foundations-and-harmonisation.md`).

### 11. Social dumping: Viking and Laval
- Risk (Handout 4; the materials): businesses relocate to States with lower labour standards (regulatory arbitrage) or use cheaper home-State labour to provide services elsewhere (posting). The risk grew after the 2004 and 2007 enlargements given wage differences. Directive 96/71 (Posted Workers) lets host States apply some of their labour law (including minimum wages) to posted workers.
- **Viking** (C-438/05): a Finnish ferry operator on the Finland-Estonia route wanted to reflag a vessel (the Rosella) to Estonia to benefit from lower wages; the Finnish seamen's union, supported by the international transport workers' federation, threatened strikes. Art 49 could be relied on against the collective action. The Court held such collective action is a restriction on establishment; even if it could reasonably be seen as falling within the objective of protecting workers, that view "would no longer be tenable if it were established that the jobs or conditions of employment at issue were not jeopardised or under serious threat" (the materials).
- **Laval** (C-341/05): a Latvian company won a contract to refurbish a Swedish school using Latvian workers paid about 40% less than Swedish workers, refused to apply the Swedish collective agreement, and faced industrial action: was the action contrary to Art 56 and the Posted Workers Directive? (the materials; see `free-movement-of-services.md`.)

## Debate and critique
- **Stepping beyond discrimination (the materials essay)**:
  - *For*: protects professionals and companies from unnecessary barriers even if equally applied; serves the single market (Art 26 TFEU); aligns persons with goods (*Cassis*) and gives coherence across freedoms; protects individual economic freedom.
  - *Against*: threatens national regulatory autonomy (AG Tizzano's "market without rules"); legal uncertainty about what counts as a restriction; shifts decision-making to proportionality review by courts (van Leeuwen); risks the "Sunday trading trap" in persons (Spaventa 2009). The *Casa Uno* exclusion is vague.
- **Spaventa, "From Gebhard to Carpenter: Towards a (Non-)Economic European Constitution" (2004) 41 CML Rev 743**: the *Gebhard*/*Gourmet* line is a "qualitative leap". By allowing scrutiny of non-discriminatory rules even of the State of establishment, the Court stepped beyond a movement rationale; neither discrimination/double burden nor "market access" fits, because market access must then be read so widely that almost any regulation is caught. She prefers to describe it as an **individual right to exercise an economic activity free of disproportionate regulation**, linked to the Court's broader role as protector of individuals against rules it perceives as unjust (*Carpenter*), signalling a change in the Community's conception beyond free movement of factors of production. This raises legitimacy concerns.
- **Snell (2010)**: market access as used by AGs (Tizzano in *CaixaBank*) is a slogan that hides the choice between anti-discrimination and economic freedom.
- **Tizzano's Keck-style test**: an attempt to import the goods settlement into persons; critics say *Keck* itself proved unstable (see `keck-selling-arrangements-and-market-access.md`).
- **Viking/Laval**: Handout 1 links them to the economic challenges of enlargement; they expose tensions between market freedoms and social rights (the right to strike), with the Court placing collective action within free movement and requiring proportionality. Snell cites reactions to *Laval* ("Europe loses when it legitimises low wages") as evidence of the legitimacy problem.

## Applying it to a problem question
1. **Classify**: subordination (Art 45) or self-employed? Stable and continuous presence (Art 49) or temporary (Art 56; duration, regularity, periodicity, continuity; infrastructure allowed: *Gebhard* paras 26-27)?
2. **Scope**: cross-border element; returning national (*Kraus*)?
3. **Defendant**: State, professional body, union (*Viking*), private party.
4. **Restriction**:
   - nationality rule: direct discrimination; only Art 52 or Art 51 (official authority narrow: *Reyners*);
   - residence, qualification, language: indirect discrimination; justify and proportion; recognise foreign qualifications (*Gebhard* para 38; *Vlassopoulou*);
   - neutral rule regulating the activity: *Gebhard* four conditions; does it hinder or make less attractive (*CaixaBank*), or is it too uncertain and indirect (*Casa Uno*)?
5. **Justification**: imperative requirements (consumer protection, creditor protection, proper administration of justice, worker protection in *Viking*) and proportionality (suitability, necessity).
6. **Benefits**: tax and social advantages via Art 49 (*Steinhauser*, *Commission v Italy*) or Art 24 Dir 2004/38.
7. **Companies**: go to `company-mobility.md`.
