# Brexit and the free movement of goods - Withdrawal Agreement, Northern Ireland, TCA and the UK Internal Market Act 2020

## Overview
The UK left the EU on 31 January 2020 (Withdrawal Agreement in force 1 February 2020; transition period to 31 December 2020). For goods, three instruments matter: the **Withdrawal Agreement (WA)** and its **Protocol on Ireland/Northern Ireland** (now as amended by the **Windsor Framework**), which keeps Northern Ireland inside the EU's customs union and internal market rules for goods; the **Trade and Cooperation Agreement (TCA)**, a free trade agreement giving zero tariffs and quotas but no free movement; and the **UK Internal Market Act 2020 (UKIMA)**, which builds a domestic internal market modelled partly on EU case law (the materials). Jurisdiction: EU law, international law (WA, TCA) and UK law (UKIMA).

## The law

### 1. The options (the materials)
- **"Norway" model**: EEA membership; remain in the internal market.
- **"Turkey" model**: leave the internal market but stay in a customs union.
- **"Canada+" model**: a free trade agreement. This is broadly what was chosen.
- The free movement issues in the Art 50 TEU process were citizens' rights (see `brexit-and-citizens-rights.md`) and the Good Friday Agreement / Irish border.

### 2. The Irish border problem
- The Good Friday Agreement requires an open border between Northern Ireland and Ireland; both sides committed to protecting it.
- **Theresa May's "backstop"**: the whole UK would stay in the EU customs union if no other solution was found. This would keep the border frictionless, but the common external tariff would stop the UK making its own trade deals, undermining Brexit's sovereignty aims.
- **Boris Johnson's solution** (renegotiated October-November 2019): Northern Ireland stays in the EU customs union for goods; checks move to the border between Great Britain and Northern Ireland.

### 3. The Protocol on Ireland/Northern Ireland
- Aims to reconcile UK territorial integrity and EU internal market integrity without a hard border (Handout 7, 2023-24 EU Law module).
- **Art 4**: Northern Ireland is part of the UK customs territory for international trade (including UK FTAs).
- **Art 5**: goods moving from Great Britain or third countries into Northern Ireland pay no EU duties unless "at risk" of moving into the Union. Under Art 5(2) a good is at risk unless it is established that it (a) will not undergo commercial processing in Northern Ireland and (b) meets criteria set by the Joint Committee. Duties collected are kept by the UK (Art 5(6)). Personal property exempted.
- **Art 5(5)** (as described in the materials): the UK must comply with **Arts 30 and 110 TFEU** for Northern Ireland, and quantitative restrictions on imports and exports between the EU and Northern Ireland are prohibited (**Art 34-36** principles apply to goods coming from the EU: Handout 1).
- **Arts 7-10**: EU product regulation, VAT and excise, the single electricity market and State aid rules continue to apply to Northern Ireland.
- **EU institutions and CJEU**: EU representatives may attend UK implementation activities (Art 12(2)); EU institutions keep their powers, including CJEU jurisdiction and preliminary references under Art 267 TFEU (Art 12(4)); their acts have the same effects in the UK as in the EU (Art 12(5)); references to EU law are dynamic, to EU law "as amended and replaced" (Art 13(3)).
- **Safeguard clause (Art 16)**: unilateral safeguard measures for "serious economic, societal or environmental difficulties that are liable to persist, or to diversion of trade", met by proportionate rebalancing measures.
- **Consequence**: an internal border in the Irish Sea, with checks on goods from Great Britain to Northern Ireland.

### 4. The Windsor Framework (2023)
- Background: UK proposals (July 2021) to remove GB-NI checks and EU supervision were rejected by the EU; the UK's Northern Ireland Protocol Bill (June 2022) would have overridden parts of the Protocol, and the Commission started infringement proceedings; the Sunak government withdrew the Bill.
- Agreed politically in the Joint Committee (the materials dates it February 2023; Handouts say adopted in March 2023).
- **Green lanes** for goods staying in Northern Ireland (no further checks) and **red lanes** for goods destined for Ireland/the EU (full checks) (Handout 1).
- A **"Stormont brake"**: the UK government, with Northern Ireland Assembly consent, may block changes to EU law with significant impact specific to everyday life in Northern Ireland; widely thought hard to use in practice. Changes on VAT, labelling and technical matters; no substantive change to the CJEU's role (Handout 7).

### 5. The Withdrawal Agreement: legal effects (Art 4 WA)
- Same legal effects in the UK as EU law in Member States: primacy over conflicting UK law; direct effect where EU conditions are met (Art 4(1)).
- EU law concepts interpreted in line with pre-transition CJEU case law (Art 4(4)); "due regard" to later case law (Art 4(5)).
- Joint Committee (Art 164) and specialised committees (Art 165); dispute settlement by arbitration with mandatory CJEU rulings on EU law questions (Arts 168-181, Art 174).

### 6. The Trade and Cooperation Agreement (TCA)
- Concluded 24 December 2020; on the EU side an association agreement under **Art 217 TFEU**, not a mixed agreement (Handout 7, citing Van Elsuwege).
- **No free movement of goods**, but **zero tariffs and zero quotas** for goods meeting the TCA's **rules of origin** (Arts 15-122 TCA) (the materials; Handout 7).
- National treatment in line with **Art III GATT** (Art 19 TCA); WTO case law relevant (e.g. Art 516). Because GATT is incorporated, TCA provisions are not interpreted in line with EU law.
- **Customs formalities and checks** on UK goods entering the EU, including health (SPS) inspections. Barnard and Leinarte note that the EU introduced full SPS controls on UK imports on 1 January 2021 while the UK phased in its controls.
- A purely international treaty: interpreted under public international law, not domestic law; neither side's court rulings bind the other (Art 4 TCA); **no direct effect** (Art 5 TCA), save limited exceptions (social security coordination; for the EU, law enforcement and judicial cooperation). Review every five years (Art 776).

### 7. The UK Internal Market Act 2020
- Creates an internal market for goods, services and professional qualifications among England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland; described in the materials as the "legacy" of EU free movement law and a "codification exercise" of Art 34 case law.
- **Market access principles**:
  - **Mutual recognition** (s 3): applies to "relevant requirements" such as product requirements (statutory requirements on characteristics, presentation or production of goods) (modelled on *Cassis de Dijon*).
  - **Non-discrimination** (s 4): applies to relevant requirements such as manner-of-sale requirements; direct and indirect discrimination against goods from another part of the UK prohibited (modelled on *Keck*).
- **Exclusions and justifications**:
  - s 10 and Sch 1: market access principles excluded where there is a "serious threat to human, animal or plant health" from a "pest or disease" or "unsafe food".
  - s 8(6): indirectly discriminatory rules can be justified only by "the protection of the life or health of humans, animals and plants" or "the protection of public safety or security".
  - So the justifications are much narrower than under EU law (no open category of mandatory requirements) (Handout 1).
- **Scope and enforcement**: material and formal scope limited to statutory requirements; no "direct legal effect" of the market access principles (the materials).
- the materials asks whether the aim is centralisation of regulatory power in Westminster; Handout 1 notes it is seen as a threat to devolution.

## Key authorities
- Art 50 TEU (withdrawal); Withdrawal Agreement Arts 4, 158, 164-165, 168-181.
- Protocol on Ireland/Northern Ireland Arts 4, 5 (incl. 5(2), 5(5), 5(6)), 7-10, 12-13, 16.
- Windsor Framework (2023).
- TCA Arts 4, 5, 15-122, 19, 516, 776; Art 217 TFEU.
- UK Internal Market Act 2020 ss 3, 4, 8(6), 10, Sch 1.
- Arts 30, 34-36, 110 TFEU (still relevant for Northern Ireland).
- *Cassis de Dijon*; *Keck*: models for UKIMA ss 3-4.
- *R (Scotch Whisky Association) v Lord Advocate* [2017] UKSC 76: an example of a devolved health measure (minimum unit pricing) tested under EU law before Brexit; useful comparator for UKIMA's narrower justification regime.

## Debate and critique
- **Barnard and Leinarte, "Movement of Goods under the TCA" (2022) 13 Global Policy 106**: it would be "entirely wrong" to see the TCA as free movement of goods "minus". Apart from the Northern Ireland Protocol (part of the WA, not the TCA), the TCA's approach to goods has much more in common with WTO rules, on which it draws heavily, than with EU law: a "WTO-plus" agreement that removes tariffs but not the regulatory and institutional depth of the internal market, prioritising regulatory autonomy over seamless trade; customs and SPS formalities remain.
- **Peers, "So Close, Yet So Far" (2022) 59 CML Rev 49**: the TCA is a complex treaty that tries to draw a line under UK membership and place the relationship on international law; he analyses legal effect, legal basis, termination and suspension, dispute settlement and the "level playing field" provisions, and compares it with the WA and other EU third-country relationships. Handout 7 quotes his remark that the parties "do not agree on much of what the Protocol means".
- **Weatherill, "Comparative Internal Market Law: The UK and the EU" (2021) 40 YEL 431**: UKIMA invites comparative internal market law. Its structure resembles the EU's, but the UK model is **less tolerant of regulatory diversity** among its constituent parts. Given England's dominant size, a *more* tolerant model than the EU's is needed, not less. He suggests more generous justification (the EU is an obvious model) and stronger common frameworks (reversing the presumption so market access principles bite only after deciding a common framework is unnecessary). UKIMA forces Scotland and Wales into a project that subverts their autonomy, while the Protocol partly excludes Northern Ireland. In the EU, market integration breeds political integration; in the UK, prioritising market access over local autonomy risks breeding disintegration.
- **Armstrong, "The Governance of Economic Unionism after the UKIM Act" (2022) 85 MLR 635**: the statutory UK internal market is a strategy to **"de-constitutionalise"** internal market governance, replacing EU judicial authority with UK legislative authority; and, as "economic unionism", it disrupts the "collaborative unionism" of the UK's territorial constitution.
- **Essay themes**: whether a market can be integrated without a court and direct effect (TCA, UKIMA lack both); the cost of the Irish Sea border to UK integrity versus the benefit of an open Irish border; dynamic alignment and CJEU oversight in Northern Ireland as a sovereignty issue; UKIMA as evidence of how far EU doctrines (*Cassis*, *Keck*) have become a template, and how removing open-ended justification changes their character.

## Applying it to a problem question
1. **Where are the goods going and from where?**
   - EU State to EU State: ordinary EU law.
   - EU to Northern Ireland or vice versa: the Protocol applies Arts 30, 110 and 34-36 TFEU; CJEU jurisdiction remains.
   - Great Britain to Northern Ireland: Art 5 Protocol "at risk" test (commercial processing; Joint Committee criteria); Windsor Framework green/red lanes.
   - UK (Great Britain) to EU: TCA; zero tariffs if rules of origin met; customs and SPS checks; no Art 34 claim; no direct effect of TCA in national courts.
   - Between parts of the UK: UKIMA ss 3-4, exclusions in s 10/Sch 1 and narrow justifications in s 8(6).
2. **Is the measure a tariff/charge or a regulatory barrier?** Under the TCA, tariffs and quotas are prohibited but regulatory checks are permitted.
3. **Remedies**: under the Protocol, EU-law remedies and preliminary references; under the TCA, State-to-State dispute settlement only; under UKIMA, statutory routes without direct effect.
4. **Traps**: applying *Cassis* to GB-EU trade after 2020; forgetting that Northern Ireland still applies EU goods law; assuming UKIMA allows mandatory requirement justifications.
