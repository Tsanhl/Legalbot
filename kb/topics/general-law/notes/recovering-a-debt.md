# Recovering a debt: letter before claim, small claims, Money Claim Online, limitation and enforcement

## Overview
A creditor should first send a letter before claim giving the debtor time to respond, then, if unpaid, issue a money claim in the county court. Claims up to £10,000 go on the small claims track, with limited costs. Fixed-sum claims can be issued online. A claim must be brought within six years of the debt falling due (simple contract), and a judgment is enforced by bailiffs, attachment of earnings, third-party debt orders or charging orders.

## The law

### Before issuing: pre-action steps
- **Practice Direction on Pre-Action Conduct (PD-PAC):** the parties should exchange sufficient information about the claim, try to settle it, and consider ADR. The usual response period in a straightforward case is 14 days, up to a maximum of 3 months in a complex one. Failure to comply can lead to costs sanctions or stays.
- **Pre-Action Protocol for Debt Claims** (in force 1 October 2017): applies where a business (or public body) claims payment of a debt from an individual (including a sole trader). The creditor sends a letter of claim with prescribed information (the debt amount, how it arose, interest, a reply form, information on how to get debt advice) and the debtor has 30 days to respond (the creditor should not issue sooner). Debtors can ask for documents, propose a payment plan or dispute the debt. Claims against companies are covered by PD-PAC, not the Protocol.
- **Interest and compensation:** the court can award simple interest up to judgment under the County Courts Act 1984 s 69 at its discretion (claimants commonly claim 8% a year; the claim form must state the basis and rate). After judgment, County Court judgments carry interest at 8% only if they are for £5,000 or more (or the debt is under the Late Payment Act) (County Courts (Interest on Judgment Debts) Order 1991). Business-to-business debts are covered by the Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998: interest at 8% above the Bank of England base rate, plus fixed sums (£40 for debts under £1,000, £70 for debts £1,000 to £9,999.99, £100 for £10,000 or more) as compensation for recovery costs.
- **ADR:** the court expects the parties to consider mediation or other ADR; *Churchill v Merthyr Tydfil CBC* [2023] EWCA Civ 1416 (CA) held that the court can order the parties to engage in ADR or stay proceedings for that purpose, provided the order does not impair the very essence of the right to a judicial hearing and is proportionate; costs consequences follow unreasonable refusal (*Halsey v Milton Keynes General NHS Trust* [2004] EWCA Civ 576, whose contrary dictum on compulsion *Churchill* disapproved). Since 1 October 2024 the CPR expressly include ADR in the overriding objective and give the court power to order it (CPR 3.1(2)(o)). Since 22 May 2024, defended specified-money claims of up to £10,000 issued in the County Court are generally referred to a free one-hour telephone mediation by the HMCTS Small Claims Mediation Service, which the parties must attend.
- **Debt collection conduct:** the Financial Conduct Authority's rules (CONC) apply to creditors and collection agents in consumer credit; harassment is unlawful under the Protection from Harassment Act 1997.

### Choosing the track (CPR Parts 26 and 27)
- **Small claims track:** claims up to £10,000 (the general limit). The rules are in CPR Part 27: informal procedure, costs very limited (fixed court fee, limited expenses, no legal costs recovery beyond a small fixed sum; unreasonable behaviour is an exception). Commonly decided at a short hearing before a district judge.
- **Fast track:** £10,000 to £25,000. **Intermediate track:** £25,000 to £100,000 (introduced 1 October 2023). **Multi-track:** above that or complex.
- Different limits apply to claims for housing disrepair, personal injury (generally £1,000 for the injury element on small claims) and others; ignore here.

### Starting a claim
- **Online claim ("Make a court claim for money"):** HMCTS's online service is for fixed (specified) sums against defendants with an address for service in England and Wales; Money Claim Online has taken claims up to £100,000 against no more than two defendants. Issue fees (the same online and on paper): £35 (up to £300), £50 (to £500), £70 (to £1,000), £80 (to £1,500), £115 (to £3,000), £205 (to £5,000), £455 (to £10,000), 5% of the claim from £10,000.01 to £200,000, and £10,000 above that. A separate hearing fee is payable if a small claim goes to a hearing.
- **Paper:** form N1 to the County Court Money Claims Centre; or a claim can be issued in the local court.
- **Claim form and particulars:** state the sum, interest, the basis of the claim and a statement of truth.
- **Response:** the defendant has 14 days after service of the particulars to respond (acknowledgment or defence within 14 or 28 days); the response options: admit, part-admit, defend, counterclaim. If the defendant does not respond, the claimant can request default judgment (CPR Part 12). If the defendant admits and offers instalments, the claimant can accept or ask the court to set terms (CPR Part 14).
- **Allocation:** if defended, a small claims claim is allocated to the small claims track; the court may refer it to mediation. The claimant needs documents showing the contract, invoices and the demand.
- **Judgment may be set aside** if wrongly obtained or if the defendant has a real prospect of defending and acts promptly (CPR Part 13).
- Consumer credit debts: the creditor must have the credit agreement and comply with the Consumer Credit Act 1974 (default notice before termination: ss 87 to 89); an unenforceable agreement cannot be enforced without a court order.

### Limitation
- Limitation Act 1980 s 5: an action founded on simple contract (including a debt) cannot be brought after six years from the date the cause of action accrued, which is usually when the debt becomes due and unpaid. s 8: deeds and specialty claims, 12 years. s 24: a judgment can be enforced within six years (with permission after that); s 15: land, 12 years.
- A written acknowledgment signed by the debtor (s 29(5), s 30) or a part payment (s 29(5)) restarts the six years. A mere payment or letter by a third party does not. A statute-barred debt remains owing but cannot be enforced through the courts if the debtor pleads limitation; for consumer credit debts the FCA's rules (CONC 7.15) forbid threatening court action on a statute-barred debt.
- For regulated consumer credit, limitation is also generally six years; a creditor's failure to follow the Consumer Credit Act rules can make the agreement unenforceable regardless.
- Claims by the Crown and some tax debts are different.

### Enforcement of a judgment
- The creditor must first apply; the court does not enforce automatically. Options:
  1. **Warrant of control** (bailiffs seize goods; Taking Control of Goods Regulations 2013, SI 2013/1894; Tribunals, Courts and Enforcement Act 2007 Sch 12). Exempt goods include tools of the trade (up to £1,350) and household basics. A County Court judgment of £600 or more may be transferred to the High Court for enforcement by a High Court Enforcement Officer (writ of control), and one of £5,000 or more must be enforced there, except judgments on regulated consumer credit agreements, which stay in the County Court (High Court and County Courts Jurisdiction Order 1991 art 8).
  2. **Attachment of earnings order** (Attachment of Earnings Act 1971): the employer deducts from wages.
  3. **Third-party debt order** (CPR Part 72): freezes and pays over money held in the debtor's bank account.
  4. **Charging order** (Charging Orders Act 1979; CPR Part 73) over land or securities, which can be followed by an order for sale; interest in a jointly-owned home is affected; the court has discretion.
  5. **Order to obtain information** (CPR Part 71): the debtor attends court to be questioned about their means.
  6. **Insolvency:** a statutory demand for a debt of £750 or more (companies) or £5,000 or more for a bankruptcy petition against an individual; a winding-up petition against a company that cannot pay (Insolvency Act 1986 ss 122, 123, 268).
- Enforcement can be delayed by an application to vary instalments or by debtor insolvency.
- The creditor can recover fixed costs of issuing the claim and enforcement from the debtor in some cases.

## Key authorities
- *Churchill v Merthyr Tydfil CBC* [2023] EWCA Civ 1416 (CA): the court has power to order parties to engage in ADR.
- *Halsey v Milton Keynes General NHS Trust* [2004] EWCA Civ 576 (CA): costs penalties for unreasonable refusal to mediate.
- *Dunnett v Railtrack plc* [2002] EWCA Civ 303 (CA): refusal of ADR led to loss of costs (cited in the ADR cases).

## Applying it
1. Is the debt due, undisputed or disputed? Is it a consumer debt or a business debt (which protocol applies)?
2. Send a letter before claim in the right form, allow time (14 days under PD-PAC or 30 days under the Debt Protocol), keep evidence.
3. Check limitation: six years from the day the debt was due; an acknowledgment or part payment restarts it.
4. Issue the claim online (up to £100,000) or on form N1; the track is determined by amount (small claims up to £10,000).
5. If no response: default judgment. If defended: allocation, mediation, hearing.
6. Enforce: choose between bailiffs, earnings, bank account, charging order.
7. Common traps: threatening court action on a time-barred debt; failing to follow the pre-action steps (costs); issuing against the wrong party (limited company versus director); not knowing the debtor's assets; forgetting the court fee is recoverable if the claim succeeds.
8. Everyday question: "Someone owes me £3,000 and won't pay." Send a letter before claim giving 14 days (or 30 if the debtor is an individual and you are a business); issue an online claim; seek judgment; enforce.

## Debates and reform
- Access to justice: the small claims limit and the lack of cost recovery discourage some claimants; the court fee levels and the online service have been reformed.
- Enforcement: the bailiff system and the low rate of successful recovery; reform of the County Court enforcement.
- The roles of ADR and compulsory mediation (*Churchill*, the 2024 CPR amendments, and automatic small claims mediation).

## Recent changes and points to check
- 1 October 2023: intermediate track and fixed recoverable costs expansions.
- 22 May 2024: automatic small claims mediation for specified money claims; 1 October 2024: CPR ADR amendments.
- Small claims limit £10,000 unchanged at November 2026; court fees are uprated periodically (figures above are the current gov.uk fees).
