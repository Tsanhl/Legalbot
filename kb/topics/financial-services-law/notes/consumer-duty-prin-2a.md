# The Consumer Duty (Principle 12 and PRIN 2A)

## Overview
The Consumer Duty is a package of FCA rules, set out in new Principle 12 and chapter 2A of the Principles for Businesses sourcebook (PRIN 2A), requiring firms to deliver good outcomes for retail customers. It consists of the consumer principle, three cross-cutting rules and four outcome areas. It applied to open products from 31 July 2023 and to closed products from 31 July 2024. It is regulatory, not a free-standing private cause of action, but the FCA uses it for supervision and enforcement and the Ombudsman can take it into account.

## The law
**The consumer principle (Principle 12).** "A firm must act to deliver good outcomes for retail customers." Where Principle 12 applies, Principles 6 (customers' interests and fair treatment) and 7 (communications) do not; they continue to apply to activities outside the Duty. The Duty sets a higher, outcomes-focused standard: the firm must evidence that customers are actually getting good outcomes, not just that processes were followed.

**Cross-cutting rules (PRIN 2A.2).** A firm must:
1. act in good faith towards retail customers: honestly, fairly and openly, with no unfair or misleading behaviour, and with regard to their legitimate interests and reasonable expectations;
2. avoid causing foreseeable harm to retail customers (including by products, communications, practices and failing to act); and
3. enable and support retail customers to pursue their financial objectives (for example by helping them make good decisions, avoiding barriers to switching or claiming, and enabling customers to act on information).

**The four outcomes.**
- *Products and services* (PRIN 2A.3): products and services must be designed to meet the needs, characteristics and objectives of an identified target market, and distributed appropriately. Manufacturers must identify the target market, test and review; distributors must understand the product and its target market.
- *Price and value* (PRIN 2A.4): the price paid must be reasonable compared with the overall benefits of the product (fair value). Firms must assess value at product level, take into account distribution costs, and avoid products which offer poor value to groups of customers. The FCA has said it will not regulate prices as such; it does not set a "fair price" but expects firms to justify them. Insurance products are also subject to the PROD 4 product governance and fair value rules.
- *Consumer understanding* (PRIN 2A.5): communications must meet the information needs of customers, be likely to be understood, and equip customers to make decisions that are effective, timely and properly informed. Firms must test communications and have regard to customers' characteristics (including vulnerability).
- *Consumer support* (PRIN 2A.6): customers must get support that meets their needs throughout the product life cycle, with no unreasonable barriers (such as higher friction in leaving than in joining a product) and with complaint handling that is effective.

**Governance, monitoring and reporting.** A firm's board (or equivalent governing body) must review at least annually an assessment of whether the firm is delivering good outcomes consistent with the Duty and agree any action needed; the FCA also expects firms to identify a board-level champion for the Duty. Firms must monitor outcomes using data, including for customers with characteristics of vulnerability, and take action where outcomes are poor.

**Who and what is covered.** The Duty applies to firms' regulated activities and ancillary activities in the "retail market business" (including retail banking, insurance, mortgages, investments, pensions and consumer credit) in relation to **retail customers**. The definition of retail customer varies by sector, and the FCA has applied the rules to firms in the distribution chain, including manufacturers who do not deal directly with customers (the rules look at the firm's influence over outcomes). 

**Dates.** Implementation date for new and existing products or services that are open to sale or renewal: 31 July 2023. Closed products and services (no longer on sale or open to renewal): 31 July 2024.

**Private law effects.** The Consumer Duty did not introduce a right of action under FSMA s 138D. Principles are not actionable under s 138D, and the FCA did not make PRIN 2A the subject of a new private right of action. Claims for breach of other rules remain under s 138D(2) for private persons. The Financial Ombudsman Service decides complaints by reference to what is fair and reasonable in all the circumstances and may take into account relevant rules and good practice, which may include the Consumer Duty.

## Key authorities
There is no significant decided case law yet interpreting PRIN 2A; the primary sources are the FCA's rules and guidance (PS22/9 of July 2022, the finalised guidance FG22/5 on the Consumer Duty, and the Handbook).
- *R (British Bankers Association) v Financial Services Authority* [2011] EWHC 999 (Admin): the regulators' ability to rely on the Principles and on guidance in setting standards for complaint handling; relevant to how the FOS and the FCA interact with high-level Principles.
- The FCA's enforcement and supervisory outputs (for example "Dear CEO" letters, thematic reviews and the Consumer Duty annual reports) show how the rules are applied.

## Applying it
1. Is the firm within the Duty? Retail customer, regulated activity, product open to sale at 31 July 2023 or closed as at 31 July 2024.
2. Identify which outcome the facts engage: poorly designed or poorly targeted product (products and services); excessive fees or low-value add-ons (price and value); unclear or misleading communication (consumer understanding); unreasonable obstacles to cancelling, claiming or complaining (consumer support).
3. Apply the cross-cutting rules: good faith, foreseeable harm, support for financial objectives. Consider the position of vulnerable customers.
4. Evidence: the firm should be able to show testing, data, governance and board oversight.
5. Remedies for the customer: complaint to the firm and the FOS; the FCA may use supervisory and enforcement powers (public censure, penalties, restitution, requirement for redress); a private claim depends on a rule that is actionable under s 138D.
6. Common traps: assuming the Duty creates a damages claim; ignoring that the Duty applies to the manufacturer, not only to the seller; forgetting the 2025 insurance carve-outs for larger commercial customers and bespoke contracts.
Everyday version: a bank or insurer must design, price and explain its products so that customers get fair treatment and good outcomes; a customer who is treated badly can complain to the firm, then the Ombudsman.

## Debates and reform
- Whether an outcomes-based regime is workable and enforceable without detailed rules, and how it interacts with the FOS jurisdiction.
- Whether the Duty should create a private right of action (the FCA declined).
- Pressure from the government's growth agenda for the FCA to simplify rules: the FCA has simplified rules for insurance and consulted on narrowing the Duty's scope.

## Recent changes and points to check
- Insurance simplification (PS25/21, in force 9 December 2025): commercial customers above the DISP small business thresholds and "large risks" contracts are generally outside the Duty, ICOBS and PROD; where the bespoke contract exclusion applies, the products and services and price and value outcomes do not apply to product governance. Retail customers (for example residential leaseholders) remain protected.
- FCA 2026/33 (Targeted Clarifications of Handbook Materials Instrument 2026, mainly in force 26 June 2026) made clarifying amendments, including a Glossary definition of "Consumer Duty".
- CP26/23 (published 29 June 2026, closed 18 September 2026) proposes limiting the Duty to retail market business with customers usually resident in the UK, confirming that it does not apply where a firm's role is too limited or remote to influence retail outcomes, allowing firms to rely reasonably on information from others in the distribution chain, and excluding activities such as merchant acquiring, ESG ratings and market making. These are proposals; final rules are expected in Q1 2027.
