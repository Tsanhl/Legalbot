# The Financial Ombudsman Service and complaints handling (DISP)

## Overview
The Financial Ombudsman Service (FOS) is the statutory, free-to-consumer dispute resolution body for financial services complaints. It decides complaints by reference to what is **fair and reasonable in all the circumstances** (FSMA 2000 s 228(2)), not strictly by the law. Eligible complainants must first complain to the firm; if dissatisfied or after eight weeks they may go to the FOS within six months of the firm's final response. The award limit for complaints referred on or after 1 April 2026 about acts or omissions on or after 1 April 2019 is **GBP 455,000**.

## The law
**Statutory basis.** The Ombudsman scheme is established under Part 16 of FSMA 2000 (ss 225-234B and Schedule 17). The "compulsory jurisdiction" covers complaints against authorised firms about regulated activities (and certain ancillary activities); the consumer credit and voluntary jurisdictions cover other firms that opt in or are brought in by statute. The FCA sets rules in the Dispute Resolution sourcebook (DISP): DISP 1 (firms' complaint handling), DISP 2 (jurisdiction), DISP 3 (how complaints are decided), DISP 4 (voluntary jurisdiction).

**Firms' complaint handling (DISP 1).** Firms must have effective written complaint procedures; acknowledge complaints promptly; send a final response (or a response that the firm cannot yet provide one) within **eight weeks**; and tell complainants in the final response that they can refer to the FOS within **six months**. Complaints resolved by the end of the third business day after receipt may be closed with a "summary resolution communication". Complaints about payment services and e-money must be answered within 15 business days (35 business days in exceptional circumstances, with a holding reply). A complaint that is not resolved within eight weeks can be referred to the FOS.

**Eligible complainants (DISP 2.7).** In summary: consumers; micro-enterprises (fewer than 10 employees and turnover or balance sheet not exceeding EUR 2 million); small businesses (annual turnover under GBP 6.5 million and either fewer than 50 employees or a balance sheet total under GBP 5 million); charities with annual income under GBP 6.5 million; trustees of trusts with net asset value under GBP 5 million; and some guarantors and other persons. Larger businesses are excluded. The respondent must be a firm within the jurisdiction, the complaint about an act or omission in carrying on a covered activity, and the complainant a customer or a person with a relevant relationship. (These thresholds have applied since 1 April 2019.)

**Time limits (DISP 2.8).** Subject to exceptions, the Ombudsman cannot consider a complaint if it is referred:
- **more than six months** after the date the firm sent its final response or equivalent, provided the firm's letter told the complainant about the six-month limit and the FOS; or
- **more than six years** after the event complained of, or, if later, **more than three years** from the date the complainant knew, or ought reasonably to have known, that they had cause for complaint.
The Ombudsman may extend these limits in exceptional circumstances (for example where the complainant was incapacitated) and the firm may consent to waive them. The approach to whether a complainant "ought reasonably to have known" is fact-sensitive.

**How complaints are decided: fair and reasonable (s 228(2); DISP 3.6).** A complaint is determined by reference to what is, in the opinion of the ombudsman, fair and reasonable in all the circumstances of the case (s 228(2)). Under DISP 3.6.4R the ombudsman must take into account relevant law and regulations, regulators' rules, guidance and standards, codes of practice, and, where appropriate, what he considers to have been good industry practice at the relevant time. The ombudsman may therefore depart from the strict legal position but must take law into account and should explain a departure.
Process: the complaint is assessed by an investigator; if either party disagrees, an ombudsman makes a final decision. The decision is given in writing with reasons (s 228(3)-(4)); if the complainant accepts, it is binding on both sides and final (s 228(5)); a complainant who rejects it, or is treated as rejecting it, is free to pursue the claim in court, and the firm is not bound.

**Awards (s 229).** A determination in favour of the complainant may include a money award for fair compensation for financial loss or for other specified loss or damage (distress, inconvenience, damage to reputation) and a direction that the firm take just and appropriate steps (s 229(2)-(3)). A money award may not exceed the monetary limit, but the ombudsman may recommend that the firm pay the balance (s 229(5)); interest can be awarded (s 229(8)). A money award is enforceable by the complainant in court (via Schedule 17 and the County Court). The limit is set in the FCA's rules and indexed annually to CPI.
- **Current limits** (FOS website): for complaints referred on or after 1 April 2026: **GBP 455,000** where the act or omission was on or after 1 April 2019 (GBP 445,000 for referrals on or after 1 April 2025; GBP 430,000 from 1 April 2024; GBP 415,000 for referrals between 1 April 2023 and 31 March 2024); GBP 205,000 for acts or omissions before 1 April 2019 (GBP 200,000 for referrals from 1 April 2025).
- **Distress and inconvenience** awards are typically modest (sums of a few hundred pounds), as set out in the Ombudsman's published approach.
- The Ombudsman may also award costs in limited circumstances (s 230) and interest.

**Other protections.** The Financial Services Compensation Scheme (FSCS) pays compensation where a regulated firm has failed (deposits up to GBP 120,000 per eligible person per authorised firm for firm failures on or after 1 December 2025, raised from GBP 85,000 by the PRA's final rules of November 2025); the FOS and FSCS interact where a firm has gone out of business.

## Key authorities
- *R (IFG Financial Services Ltd) v Financial Ombudsman Service Ltd* [2005] EWHC 1153 (Admin): the "fair and reasonable" jurisdiction permits the ombudsman to depart from the strict legal position but he must take the law into account and explain departures; the ombudsman's decision is judicially reviewable for error of law, irrationality or procedural unfairness.
- *R (Heather Moor & Edgecomb Ltd) v Financial Ombudsman Service* [2008] EWCA Civ 642, [2008] Bus LR 1486: the Court of Appeal approved *IFG*: the ombudsman is not bound to decide as a court would, but must take the law into account; the "fair and reasonable" standard is sufficiently certain to be "prescribed by law", the scheme is compatible with Article 6 ECHR and Article 1 of Protocol 1, and there is no general right to an oral hearing.
- *R (British Bankers Association) v Financial Services Authority* [2011] EWHC 999 (Admin): approach to PPI complaints; the ombudsman's fair and reasonable standard operates alongside regulatory rules and guidance and was held lawful in that context.
These cases are relevant to essays on whether the Ombudsman applies a second, equitable standard alongside the common law.

## Applying it
1. Eligibility: is the complainant a consumer, micro-enterprise, small business, small charity or trustee? Is the respondent a firm covered by the jurisdiction and the complaint about a regulated or covered activity?
2. Procedure: has the complainant complained to the firm first? Eight weeks or final response. Is the referral within six months of the final response and within the six-year/three-year limits (or exceptional circumstances)?
3. Merits: apply "fair and reasonable": what did the law and regulation require; was the firm's conduct consistent with rules, guidance and good industry practice (for example the Consumer Duty and conduct of business rules); what would have happened if the firm had acted properly; what loss was suffered; was there contributory fault (the ombudsman may reduce an award where the complainant contributed).
4. Remedy: money award for financial loss and distress and inconvenience up to the limit; direction to take action (for example amend credit records); interest. If the loss exceeds the limit, the complainant can accept the award limit, or reject the decision and sue, or accept and take the Ombudsman's recommendation for the excess (which is not binding).
5. Judicial review: only an error of law, irrationality or unfair procedure.
6. Common traps: assuming the Ombudsman applies the strict law; forgetting the six-month deadline after final response; treating the award limit as the cap on all compensation (a court claim is not capped but is costlier and riskier); misreading small business turnover thresholds.
Everyday version: if a bank or insurer rejects a complaint, you can take it to the Financial Ombudsman, free of charge, within six months of the firm's final response; the Ombudsman decides what is fair, and can order up to GBP 455,000 in compensation for recent cases.

## Debates and reform
- Whether "fair and reasonable" makes outcomes unpredictable for firms and encourages a perception of unlimited liability; whether the Ombudsman should be bound more strictly by law and precedent.
- The Ombudsman's role in systemic issues such as motor finance commission after *Johnson v FirstRand Bank Ltd* [2025] UKSC 33 (1 August 2025: no fiduciary duty owed by car dealers, but the relationship in Mr Johnson's case was unfair under Consumer Credit Act 1974 s 140A) and the FCA's industry-wide redress scheme under FSMA s 404 (final rules in PS26/3, 30 March 2026). Four challenges to the scheme were brought in the Upper Tribunal under s 404D; on 2 July 2026 the FCA announced that parts of the scheme (calculating and paying redress) were suspended pending the outcome, with the hearing due on 14-18 December 2026 or 16-26 February 2027. Other scheme rules (identifying complaints, gathering data, notifying those not owed redress) continue to apply.
- Funding and case volumes (case fees for firms) and the call for a clearer boundary between the FOS and FCA schemes.

## Recent changes and points to check
- Award limit: GBP 455,000 for referrals on or after 1 April 2026 (acts on or after 1 April 2019), rising annually with CPI each 1 April; GBP 205,000 for earlier acts.
- Where an FCA consumer redress scheme under s 404 applies, complaints within its scope are handled by the firm under the scheme rules, and a complaint to the Ombudsman about the firm's scheme determination is decided by reference to the redress the scheme required (s 404B).
- The Financial Services and Markets Bill [HL] (introduced May 2026; in Commons committee in November 2026) contains reforms to the Financial Ombudsman Service aimed at quicker and more predictable outcomes; it was not law as of November 2026.
