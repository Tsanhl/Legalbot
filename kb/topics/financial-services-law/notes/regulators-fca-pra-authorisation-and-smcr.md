# The regulators: FCA and PRA objectives, authorisation (Part 4A), SM&CR and FSMA 2023

## Overview
Since 1 April 2013 the "twin peaks" model has applied: the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) regulates conduct of all authorised firms and the prudential regulation of those not supervised by the Prudential Regulation Authority (PRA), a part of the Bank of England, which supervises deposit-takers, insurers and major investment firms. Firms need Part 4A permission before carrying on regulated activities. Individuals in senior roles are regulated under the Senior Managers and Certification Regime (SM&CR). FSMA 2023 reshapes the legislative framework, adding a secondary competitiveness and growth objective and starting the move from assimilated EU law to regulator rules.

## The law
**Objectives.**
- *FCA* (FSMA 2000 s 1B): strategic objective of ensuring that the relevant markets function well; three operational objectives: securing an appropriate degree of protection for consumers (s 1C), protecting and enhancing the integrity of the UK financial system (s 1D), and promoting effective competition in the interests of consumers (s 1E). The regulator must act compatibly with these and, so far as compatible, advance the secondary objective of facilitating the international competitiveness of the UK economy and its growth in the medium to long term (added by FSMA 2023; the same applies to the PRA).
- *PRA*: general objective of promoting the safe and sound operation of PRA-authorised persons (s 2B), an insurance objective of contributing to the securing of an appropriate degree of protection for policyholders (s 2C), and a secondary competition objective (s 2H), as well as the competitiveness and growth objective.
- The regulators must also have regard to the "regulatory principles" (s 3B: proportionality, sustainable growth, transparency, and others). The Financial Policy Committee at the Bank of England has macroprudential responsibility.
- Consumer protection is not the whole of the FCA's role: the FCA also regulates market conduct (see the market abuse note) and listing.

**Authorisation (Part 4A).** A person who wishes to carry on a regulated activity must apply to the FCA (or PRA for dual-regulated firms, with FCA consent) for Part 4A permission (s 55A). The firm must satisfy and continue to satisfy the **threshold conditions** in Schedule 6 (for the FCA: legal status, location of offices, effective supervision, appropriate resources, suitability, and a viable business model). The regulator may grant permission subject to requirements, vary it on its own initiative or on application (ss 55J, 55L, 55M) and cancel it. It may refuse to approve or cancel for non-compliance. An authorised firm's ongoing conduct is governed by the Handbook rules: the Principles for Businesses (PRIN), Senior Management Arrangements, Systems and Controls (SYSC), the Conduct of Business Sourcebook (COBS), the Insurance Conduct of Business Sourcebook (ICOBS), the Mortgages and Home Finance Conduct of Business Sourcebook (MCOB), the Consumer Credit Sourcebook (CONC) and others. Appointed representatives can operate under a principal's authorisation; the FCA tightened principal oversight from 2022.

**Enforcement powers.** Part 5 (sanctions on authorised persons, individuals): power to impose public censure (s 205) and financial penalties (s 206), to require restitution (s 384), to vary or cancel permission, to prohibit individuals (s 56), and to prosecute certain offences. The statutory process involves warning notices, decision notices and referral to the Upper Tribunal (Tax and Chancery Chamber); third parties named in notices have rights under s 393.

**Senior Managers and Certification Regime (SM&CR).** The regime replaced the "approved persons" regime for banks (7 March 2016), insurers (10 December 2018), and all other FSMA-authorised firms (9 December 2019).
- *Senior Management Functions (SMFs)* (s 59): the prescribed roles (for example, chief executive, chair, finance, risk, compliance, money laundering reporting officer) need the regulators' prior approval; applicants must be fit and proper. Each SMF holder has a Statement of Responsibilities (s 60) and, in larger firms, must be mapped in a Management Responsibilities Map. Firms are classified as "core", "enhanced" or "limited scope".
- *Certification Regime*: a firm must itself certify that employees in specified significant-harm roles (and others, such as material risk takers and client-dealing staff) are fit and proper at least annually (s 63F).
- *Conduct Rules* (s 64A): individual conduct standards (acting with integrity, due skill care and diligence, openness with regulators, paying due regard to customers' interests, observing proper standards of market conduct), applying to almost all staff, plus senior manager conduct rules including taking reasonable steps in the senior manager's area of responsibility.
- *Statutory duty of responsibility* (ss 66A(5) and 66B(5)): a senior manager is guilty of misconduct if he or she fails to take such steps as a person in their position could reasonably be expected to take to prevent a regulatory breach occurring in the area of the firm for which they are responsible. The burden of proof is on the regulator.
- Separately, it is a criminal offence for a senior manager of a bank or PRA-designated investment firm to take (or agree to) a decision that causes the institution to fail, being aware of the risk and with conduct falling far below what could reasonably be expected (Financial Services (Banking Reform) Act 2013 s 36). It remains in force but has not been prosecuted.
- **Reforms.** The FCA and PRA published Phase 1 final rules on 22 April 2026 (FCA PS26/6, PRA PS12/26), mostly effective from 24 April 2026, with related changes to the conduct rules (COCON, aligned with the FCA's non-financial misconduct rules) from 1 September 2026. Phase 1 changed the 12-week rule (a candidate can act while an SMF application made within 12 weeks is determined), streamlined statements of responsibilities and responsibilities maps and removed overlapping certification roles (about 15% fewer). Phase 2 consultations of 22 October 2026 (FCA CP26/37, closing 22 January 2027; PRA CP14/26) propose removing the Certification Regime altogether and moving much of the SM&CR detail from FSMA into regulator rules; this depends on the Financial Services and Markets Bill [HL] being enacted. Final rules are expected in the first half of 2027. These Phase 2 proposals are not law.

**FSMA 2023.** The Financial Services and Markets Act 2023 (Royal Assent 29 June 2023) includes: the "Smarter Regulatory Framework" (power to revoke assimilated EU law in financial services and replace it with regulator rules and Treasury legislation); the secondary objective on international competitiveness and growth for the FCA and PRA; enhanced accountability measures (more consultation and cost-benefit analysis, with a duty to explain rule-making); a regime for critical third parties; a financial promotion approver gateway; a duty on the Payment Systems Regulator relating to APP fraud reimbursement (s 72); cash access provisions; and changes to short selling and fund regimes. Use the Act as the source for the detailed dates of commencement.

**PSR and the 2026 Bill.** The government announced in March 2025 that it would abolish the Payment Systems Regulator and consolidate its functions within the FCA; HM Treasury confirmed this in an April 2026 consultation response. The Financial Services and Markets Bill [HL], introduced in the Lords in May 2026, would abolish the PSR, reform the Financial Ombudsman Service, cut SM&CR administrative burdens and update the ring-fencing regime. It completed its Lords stages on 15 September 2026 and was in Commons committee stage in late November 2026. Until it is enacted and commenced, the PSR keeps its powers.

## Key authorities
- *Financial Conduct Authority v Macris* [2017] UKSC 19, [2017] 1 WLR 1095 (3-2; Lord Sumption for the majority, Lord Neuberger and Lord Wilson dissenting): s 393 gives third-party rights only to a person identified in the notice itself, by name or by a synonym such as an office or job title; outside material can be used only to identify who a name or synonym refers to, not to supplement the notice.
- *R (British Bankers Association) v Financial Services Authority* [2011] EWHC 999 (Admin) (Ouseley J): the banks' challenge to the FSA's PPI complaints-handling policy and the Ombudsman's approach failed; the Principles for Businesses impose free-standing obligations that are not displaced or limited by the specific rules, and the Ombudsman may decide complaints by reference to them.
- The leading authorities on regulatory standards are largely Upper Tribunal decisions on fitness and propriety and on penalties; consult the Handbook (DEPP, FIT) for how those are applied.

## Applying it
1. Which regulator? PRA-authorised (banks, building societies, insurers, major investment firms) are dual-regulated; all other firms are FCA-only.
2. Authorisation: has the firm got Part 4A permission covering the activity? Threshold conditions apply continuously. Appointed representatives need the principal's permission.
3. Individuals: is the person performing a senior management function? If so, was approval obtained (s 59), and has the person complied with the conduct rules and the duty of responsibility? For certification staff, has the firm certified?
4. Enforcement: identify the regulator's powers (penalties, restitution, prohibition) and the procedural stages; consider the Upper Tribunal route and third-party rights.
5. Problem-question traps: confusing Principles (not actionable by private persons) with rules (actionable under s 138D), confusing the PRA with the FCA, and relying on SM&CR rules that are only proposals.
Everyday version: before trading as a financial adviser, a firm needs FCA permission, and its senior managers and advisers must be vetted; if something goes wrong, the FCA can fine the firm and ban individuals.

## Debates and reform
- Whether the new competitiveness and growth objective weakens consumer protection or financial stability (critics suggested potential dilution of the primary duties; the Treasury regards it as secondary).
- Whether SM&CR is disproportionately burdensome for smaller firms and individuals, or whether it improves accountability; the 2025-26 reforms are the response.
- Whether the PSR's merger into the FCA is the best structure for payments regulation.
- The effectiveness of the FCA as an enforcer (the length and number of enforcement cases) and the use of the Upper Tribunal.

## Recent changes and points to check
- SM&CR Phase 1: in force from 24 April 2026 (COCON changes from 1 September 2026). Phase 2: consultation only (closing 22 January 2027), dependent on the Bill.
- Financial Services and Markets Bill [HL] 2026-27: passed the Lords, in Commons committee in November 2026; not law.
- FSMA 2023 is commenced in stages by regulations, so not every provision is yet in force.
