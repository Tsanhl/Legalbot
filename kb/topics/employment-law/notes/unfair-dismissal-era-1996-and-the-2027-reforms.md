# Unfair dismissal - ERA 1996 ss 94-98, the qualifying period change and remedies

## Overview
An employee dismissed in Great Britain can claim unfair dismissal if they have the required length of service, the employer cannot show a potentially fair reason, or the employer acted unreasonably in treating that reason as sufficient. Until the end of 2026 the qualifying period is two years; for dismissals with an effective date of termination on or after 1 January 2027 it is six months, and the statutory cap on the compensatory award is removed. Some dismissals are automatically unfair and need no qualifying service (see the separate note on automatically unfair dismissal).

## The law
**Right and qualification.** ERA 1996 s 94(1): an employee has the right not to be unfairly dismissed by their employer. Section 108(1) requires continuous employment for not less than two years ending with the effective date of termination (EDT); s 108(3) lists the exceptions where no qualifying period applies (for instance, reasons under ss 99, 100, 103A, 104). Section 97(2) applies only where the employer gives less notice than s 86 requires (or none): for working out service under s 108(1), the EDT is then treated as the date on which the s 86 notice would have expired had it been given on the material date. An employee dismissed without notice, or with short notice, shortly before the threshold may therefore still qualify. Where the employer gives at least the s 86 notice, s 97(2) has no effect. Only employees (s 230(1)) can claim (see the employment status note). Time limit (s 111(2)): the claim must be presented before the end of the period beginning with the EDT (subject to the early conciliation extension), unless it was not reasonably practicable to do so in time and the claim is brought within a reasonable further period. That period was three months; the Employment Rights Act 2025 (s 152 and Sch 12) changed it to six months from 1 October 2026, but only where the relevant date (here, the EDT) is on or after 1 October 2026 (SI 2026/954 reg 4). Where the EDT was before 1 October 2026 the three-month limit still applies.

**What is a dismissal (s 95).** (a) termination by the employer with or without notice; (b) expiry of a fixed-term contract without renewal; (c) the employee terminates, with or without notice, in circumstances where they are entitled to do so because of the employer's conduct (constructive dismissal). The EDT is fixed by s 97.

**Two-stage inquiry (s 98).** Stage one: the employer must show the reason (or, if more than one, the principal reason) for the dismissal and that it falls within s 98(2) (capability or qualifications, conduct, redundancy, or contravention of a statutory duty or restriction) or is "some other substantial reason" (SOSR) under s 98(1)(b), such as a business reorganisation, a refusal of a reasonable contract change or a third-party pressure. Stage two (s 98(4)): whether the dismissal was fair or unfair depends on whether in the circumstances (including the size and administrative resources of the employer) the employer acted reasonably or unreasonably in treating the reason as sufficient for dismissing the employee, in accordance with equity and the substantial merits of the case. The burden is neutral at this stage.

**Conduct cases.** *BHS v Burchell*: the employer must have a genuine belief in the misconduct, reasonable grounds for that belief, after carrying out as much investigation as was reasonable in the circumstances. The dismissal must also fall within the band of reasonable responses, and a fair procedure (including the Acas Code of Practice on Disciplinary and Grievance Procedures, with a power under TULRCA 1992 s 207A to adjust awards by up to 25% for unreasonable failure to follow it) is essential.

**Capability.** The employer should consider whether the employee was given a fair chance to improve, warnings where appropriate, and medical evidence in ill-health cases (and whether adjustments were required, which also engages the Equality Act).

**Redundancy and SOSR.** See the separate redundancy note: fairness depends on warning, consultation, selection and alternatives.

**Remedies.** A tribunal may order reinstatement or re-engagement (ss 113-116), taking into account whether it is practicable and whether the claimant contributed; this is rare and an employer who fails to comply is exposed to an additional award (s 117). Usually the remedy is compensation:
- Basic award (s 119): age-related, calculated like a statutory redundancy payment (0.5, 1 or 1.5 weeks' pay per year of service depending on age, up to 20 years), reduced for conduct (s 122) and capped by the statutory week's pay.
- Compensatory award (s 123): "just and equitable" in the circumstances, having regard to the loss sustained by the claimant, including expenses and loss of benefits; the duty to mitigate applies; it can be reduced under s 123(6) for contributory conduct, and under the principle in *Polkey* where a fair procedure would still have led to dismissal (by a percentage or for a period). Uplifts or reductions of up to 25% for failure to follow the Acas Code (TULRCA s 207A). The compensatory award is currently capped (s 124) at the lower of 52 weeks' pay and a fixed sum; whistleblowing and health and safety dismissals are uncapped.
- Figures from 6 April 2026 (SI 2026/310): week's pay limit £751 (so the maximum basic award is 30 x £751 = £22,530); cap on the compensatory award £123,543 (or 52 weeks' pay if lower). The previous figures (£719; £118,223) apply where the EDT was before 6 April 2026. These increase each April.

**The 2025 Act reforms.** Employment Rights Act 2025 s 25 amends ERA s 108 by replacing "two years" with "six months", and omits s 124 (the limit on the compensatory award). It also removes the Secretary of State's power to change the qualifying period by order (s 209(5)). The qualifying period and cap changes come into force on 1 January 2027 by the Employment Rights Act 2025 (Commencement No. 4 and Transitional and Saving Provisions) Regulations 2026 (SI 2026/559). Under reg 4 they apply where the EDT is on or after 1 January 2027, even if the employee was dismissed (for example, given notice) before that date; where the EDT is before 1 January 2027 the two-year rule and the capped regime remain. The six-month period replaced the Bill's original "day one" right (with a statutory probation period) as a compromise reached during the final parliamentary stages in late 2025.

## Key authorities
- *Iceland Frozen Foods Ltd v Jones* [1983] ICR 17 (EAT, Browne-Wilkinson J): the "band of reasonable responses" test. The tribunal must not substitute its own view for the employer's; the question is whether dismissal was within the range of responses a reasonable employer might have adopted. Approved repeatedly, including in *Sainsbury's Supermarkets Ltd v Hitt* [2002] EWCA Civ 1588 (Court of Appeal), which applied the same band to the investigation.
- *British Home Stores Ltd v Burchell* [1980] ICR 303 (EAT, decision from 1978): three-stage test for conduct dismissals (genuine belief, reasonable grounds, reasonable investigation). The burden of proof on the reasonableness elements is neutral.
- *Polkey v AE Dayton Services Ltd* [1988] AC 344 (HL): a dismissal is not made fair by the fact that a fair procedure would have made no difference, but that may reduce compensation. Procedure is relevant to reasonableness, with limited exceptions where the employer could reasonably have concluded that consultation or warning would be "utterly useless".
- *London Ambulance Service NHS Trust v Small* [2009] EWCA Civ 220 (Court of Appeal): tribunals must not substitute their own view of what they would have done; the test is not whether the employee committed the misconduct.
- *W Devis & Sons Ltd v Atkins* [1977] AC 931 (HL): conduct discovered after dismissal cannot make a dismissal fair at the time but is relevant to compensation (just and equitable).
- *Abernethy v Mott, Hay and Anderson* [1974] ICR 323 (CA): the "reason" is the set of facts known to the employer or the beliefs held by them that caused dismissal.
- *Hollister v National Farmers' Union* [1979] ICR 542 (CA): a sound business reason for reorganisation can be SOSR, so that dismissal of an employee who refuses new terms may be fair.
- *Western Excavating (ECC) Ltd v Sharp* [1978] QB 761 (CA): constructive dismissal requires a repudiatory breach by the employer, the employee leaving in response, and not affirming the contract.

## Applying it
1. Does the claimant have a claim? Employee status; dismissal under s 95 (check for constructive dismissal and fixed term expiry); qualifying service (two years for EDT up to 31 December 2026, six months from 1 January 2027, plus any s 97(2) statutory notice extension); time limit (three or six months depending on whether the EDT is before or on/after 1 October 2026) and early conciliation; any exception that removes the need for service.
2. What was the reason? Ask the employer's reason and whether it is within s 98(1)-(2). If the employee says the real reason is an inadmissible one, deal with automatic unfairness first.
3. Was it fair under s 98(4)? Conduct: apply the three-part Burchell test, check procedure against the Acas Code (notice of allegations, investigation by someone other than the decision-maker where possible, right to be accompanied, a right of appeal), consistency, mitigating factors and the employee's service. Capability: warnings and time to improve, medical evidence. Redundancy: consultation, selection, alternatives. Keep the band of reasonable responses in view; an overall test of fairness (including the appeal) applies.
4. Remedy: reinstatement is rare; work out the basic award (age and service) and compensatory award; discuss Polkey, contributory fault, mitigation and an Acas Code uplift or reduction; check which cap applies by reference to the EDT.
5. Note any parallel claims (wrongful dismissal for notice, discrimination, whistleblowing).

Common traps: using the wrong qualifying period for the EDT; forgetting the extension by statutory notice; confusing wrongful and unfair dismissal; treating the burden of proving fairness as on the employer at stage two; and ignoring that tribunals do not decide whether the employee was guilty but whether the employer's decision was reasonable.

Everyday question: "I was dismissed after eight months; can I claim?" Answer: at present (before 2027) no, unless an automatic reason or discrimination applies, but a dismissal with an EDT on or after 1 January 2027 will qualify after six months, and other claims may still be available.

## Debates and reform
- The "band of reasonable responses" is criticised for diluting the statutory test and favouring employers, while its defenders say it prevents tribunals from managing businesses.
- Removing the cap and shortening the qualifying period is defended as strengthening job security and criticised as increasing the cost and risk of hiring; the compromise move from "day one" to six months reflects that debate. The two-year period has applied since April 2012 (it was one year before that).
- Reformers have argued that the capped award under-compensates higher earners.

## Recent changes and points to check
- 1 January 2027: six-month qualifying period and removal of the compensatory cap (SI 2026/559), for EDTs on or after that date.
- 6 April 2026 limits are listed above (SI 2026/310); the figures are revalued each April.
- Fire and rehire: the Act adds a new automatically unfair reason (ERA 1996 s 104I) for dismissal over restricted contract variations. The government timeline (updated 5 November 2026) gives March 2027; no commencement regulations for it had been made by November 2026. See the contract of employment note.
- Time limits: six months for most tribunal claims (including tribunal contract claims) where the relevant date is on or after 1 October 2026 (SI 2026/954; SI 2026/759).
