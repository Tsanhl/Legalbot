# Business transfers (TUPE 2006) and an overview of the Employment Rights Act 2025

## Overview
The Transfer of Undertakings (Protection of Employment) Regulations 2006 (TUPE) preserve employees' jobs and terms when a business, or a service, changes hands: employment contracts pass automatically to the new employer, dismissals because of the transfer are automatically unfair, and both employers must inform and consult. The Employment Rights Act 2025 (Royal Assent 18 December 2025, c. 36) is the largest reform of UK employment law for decades; it is being brought into force in stages up to at least 2027, and several parts have no date yet.

## The law (TUPE 2006, SI 2006/246)
**Relevant transfer (reg 3).** Two kinds: (a) a transfer of an undertaking, business or part of one situated in the UK to another person where there is a transfer of an economic entity which retains its identity (reg 3(1)(a), (2)); and (b) a service provision change (reg 3(1)(b)): outsourcing, insourcing or re-tendering, where before the change there is an organised grouping of employees whose principal purpose is carrying out the activities for the client, the client intends the transferee to carry them out other than for a single specific event or a short-term task, and the activities do not consist wholly or mainly of the supply of goods. In business transfers, the court looks at all factors (type of business, transfer of tangible assets, value of intangibles, taking over of the majority of employees, customers, degree of similarity before and after, and any period of interruption). In labour-intensive sectors, an entity can be a group of workers with a common task, so a transfer occurs if the transferee takes on a major part of the workforce in terms of numbers or skills.

**Effect on contracts (reg 4).** On a relevant transfer, the contracts of employees assigned to the transferring undertaking (or organised grouping) immediately before the transfer pass to the transferee; all the transferor's rights, powers, duties and liabilities under or in connection with those contracts (including accrued liabilities, such as unpaid holiday pay, and tortious liabilities for personal injury) transfer (reg 4(1), (2)). An employee dismissed before the transfer by reason of it is treated as employed immediately before (reg 4(3)). An employee may object to the transfer, in which case the contract ends but there is no dismissal (reg 4(7), (8)). If the transfer involves or would involve a substantial change in working conditions to the employee's detriment, the employee may treat the contract as terminated and the employer as having dismissed them (reg 4(9), (11)). Variations to contracts are void if the sole or principal reason is the transfer, unless an economic, technical or organisational (ETO) reason entailing changes in the workforce applies, or the variation is permitted by reg 9 (reg 4(4), (5)). Collective agreements transfer (reg 5); under reg 4A, terms that incorporate collective agreements "as agreed from time to time" do not carry forward any provision agreed after the transfer where the transferee takes no part in the bargaining for it (reflecting *Alemo-Herron*). Occupational pension rights relating to old age, invalidity and survivors' benefits are excluded (reg 10) though other pension terms transfer.

**Dismissals (reg 7).** A dismissal of an employee (before or after the transfer) is automatically unfair if the sole or principal reason is the transfer itself. If the reason is an ETO reason entailing changes in the workforce (including, since 2014, a change in the place where employees work: reg 7(3A)), the dismissal is not automatically unfair but is treated as for SOSR (or redundancy) and tested for reasonableness. Unlike the ERA automatic categories, a reg 7 claim requires the ordinary unfair dismissal qualifying period (reg 7(6)): two years where the EDT is before 1 January 2027, six months where it is on or after that date.

**Information and consultation (regs 11, 13-16).** The transferor must provide the transferee with employee liability information (identity, age, particulars, disciplinary and grievance records, claims) at least 28 days before the transfer (reg 11). Both employers must inform and consult appropriate representatives (recognised trade union or elected employee representatives) of the affected employees "long enough before" the transfer about the fact and approximate timing, the reasons, the legal, economic and social implications for the affected employees, and measures envisaged (reg 13(2)); the transferee must give the transferor the information needed (reg 13(4)). Regulation 13A allows direct consultation with the affected employees (not representatives) where there are no appropriate representatives, the employer has not invited the employees to elect any, and either the employer employs fewer than 50 employees or fewer than 10 employees are to transfer. (The "fewer than 10 transferring" alternative, and the 50-employee threshold, were introduced by SI 2023/1426 for transfers taking place on or after 1 July 2024; before then the exception applied only to micro-businesses with fewer than 10 employees.) Failure to comply gives rise to a complaint with a maximum award of 13 weeks' pay per affected employee (reg 15), and the transferor and transferee are jointly and severally liable (reg 15).

**Insolvency.** Certain pre-transfer insolvency situations (administration, as opposed to liquidation) limit the transfer of debts to the new employer (reg 8) and allow variations to terms.

## Key authorities
- *Spijkers v Gebroeders Benedik Abattoir CV* (Case 24/85) [1986] ECR 1119 (ECJ): the test for a transfer of an undertaking is whether the entity retains its identity, taking into account all the facts (type of undertaking, assets, employees, customers, similarity of activities, duration of any interruption).
- *Süzen v Zehnacker Gebäudereinigung GmbH* (Case C-13/95) [1997] ECR I-1259 (ECJ): a mere loss of a service contract to a competitor, without a transfer of significant assets or of a major part of the workforce, is not a transfer; an entity cannot be reduced to the activity entrusted to it.
- *Litster v Forth Dry Dock & Engineering Co Ltd* [1990] 1 AC 546 (HL): purposive construction of the regulations so that employees dismissed just before the transfer for a reason connected to it are treated as employed immediately before, and so liability passes to the transferee.
- *Foreningen af Arbejdsledere i Danmark v Daddy's Dance Hall A/S* (Case 324/86) [1988] ECR 739 (ECJ): employees cannot waive their transfer rights and any agreement to vary terms for the transfer is not binding.
- *Cheesman v R Brewer Contracts Ltd* [2001] IRLR 144 (EAT, Lindsay J): guidance for the approach to a business transfer in labour-intensive sectors, including the Süzen principles.
- *Metropolitan Resources Ltd v Churchill Dulwich Ltd* [2009] IRLR 700 (EAT): for an SPC, there must be an organised grouping of employees whose principal purpose is the client activities; an assessment of the activities, not an entity, is relevant.
- *Eddie Stobart Ltd v Moreman* [2012] ICR 919 (EAT): to be assigned to the organised grouping, an employee must be carrying out the activities for the client as their principal purpose; the grouping must be deliberately organised and not an accident of rotas.
- *Alemo-Herron v Parkwood Leisure Ltd* (Case C-426/11), EU:C:2013:521 (CJEU, 18 July 2013): EU law (the Acquired Rights Directive) did not allow a Member State to require a private-sector transferee to be bound by future collective agreements it had no part in negotiating; domestic reg 4A reflects that outcome.

## Applying it
1. Is there a relevant transfer: a business transfer (economic entity retains identity; labour-intensive or asset-based) or a service provision change (outsourcing, insourcing, second-generation re-tendering)? Check activities, grouping, geographical presence, client intent.
2. Which employees transfer: those assigned immediately before the transfer (look at time spent, work done, contract, managers' treatment), not those who object; liabilities (accrued pay, holiday, personal injury) transfer.
3. Who has been dismissed and why? Reg 7: transfer reason, or ETO reason entailing changes in the workforce (a reduction in numbers or functions, not just a change in terms); remedies against the transferee, with employees dismissed before the transfer covered by reg 4(3). Apply s 98 and Polkey for ETO dismissals.
4. Changes to terms: void if connected with the transfer unless ETO or reg 9; employee's reg 4(9) right; collective agreement and pension carve-outs.
5. Information and consultation duties and employee liability information; remedies up to 13 weeks' pay.
6. Also consider redundancy collective consultation if 20 or more dismissals follow.
7. Everyday question: "My employer lost its cleaning contract and a new contractor is taking over; what happens to my job?" Answer: probably an SPC, so your contract and service transfer to the new contractor on the same terms, and a dismissal because of the transfer is automatically unfair (if you have the qualifying service); if you object to transferring, your employment simply ends without a dismissal, so you cannot claim redundancy pay or unfair dismissal (unless the transfer involves a substantial detrimental change in working conditions: reg 4(9)).

Traps: treating TUPE as limited to sale of a business (it also covers outsourcing); failing to separate the "assigned" test from the "worked part-time for the contract" fact; missing that reg 4 transfers liabilities; mixing up regs 7 and 4(4).

## An overview of the Employment Rights Act 2025
The Act is in four main groups: (1) individual rights (zero hours and guaranteed hours, shift notice and cancelled-shift payments, agency workers, exclusivity terms, flexible working, SSP, family and bereavement leave); (2) harassment and discrimination measures (employers' duty to prevent harassment, third-party harassment, protection of harassment disclosures and limits on confidentiality terms); (3) dismissal and redundancy (unfair dismissal qualifying period and cap, restricted variations ("fire and rehire"), collective redundancy and protective award, pregnancy and family leave dismissals, and changes to public sector outsourcing); and (4) collective labour law and enforcement (trade union reforms, repeal of most of the Trade Union Act 2016 and minimum service levels, the Fair Work Agency and tribunal time limits). Section 159 sets the commencement: a handful of sections on Royal Assent, a group of trade union sections two months later, and the rest by regulations.

**Timetable** (in force: from the commencement regulations; future: from the government timeline updated 5 November 2026, which may change)
- 18 December 2025: Royal Assent; repeal of the minimum service levels legislation (s 78).
- 18 February 2026: s 159(2) trade union reforms (most of the Trade Union Act 2016 reversed, simpler ballot and notice rules) and stronger protection against dismissal and detriment for taking industrial action (ss 76-77, SI 2026/3).
- 6 April 2026: statutory sick pay from the first day and no lower earnings limit (ss 10-13, SI 2026/373); day-one paternity leave and unpaid parental leave and paternity leave after shared parental leave (ss 15-17, SI 2026/3); protective award maximum 180 days for dismissals taking effect on or after that date (s 30); sexual harassment disclosures protected (s 23); annual leave record-keeping (s 35); voluntary equality action plans (s 33) (SI 2026/323). The Fair Work Agency was established on 7 April 2026.
- 25 August 2026: electronic and workplace balloting for statutory union ballots.
- 1 October 2026: six-month limit for most tribunal claims where the relevant date is on or after that day (s 152, Sch 12, SI 2026/954), with tribunal contract claims aligned by SI 2026/759 (EDT on or after that day).
- 30 October 2026: "all reasonable steps" duty to prevent sexual harassment, the duty not to permit third-party harassment and the power to specify reasonable steps (ss 20-22); union access and recognition reforms and rep facilities (ss 59, 60, 64, 65) (SI 2026/1065).
- 1 January 2027: six-month unfair dismissal qualifying period and removal of the compensatory cap, for EDTs on or after that date (s 25, SI 2026/559).
- March 2027 (planned): fire and rehire protections (s 28); mandatory equality action plans for public authorities.
- April 2027 (planned): organisation-wide collective redundancy threshold (s 29); bereavement leave including pregnancy loss (s 18); mandatory equality action plans for private and voluntary employers; extended blacklisting protections.
- September 2027 (planned): regulations limiting NDAs on harassment and discrimination (s 24).
- October 2027 (planned): enhanced dismissal protection for pregnant women and new mothers (ss 26-27); flexible working changes (s 9).
- December 2027 (planned): regulation of umbrella companies (s 36).
- Not yet dated: zero hours reforms (guaranteed hours, reasonable notice of shifts, payment for cancelled or curtailed shifts, ss 1-8), pending consultation.

## Debates and reform
- The balance between worker security and employer flexibility, particularly in the six-month qualifying period compromise and the guaranteed-hours scheme.
- Whether the enforcement agency (Fair Work Agency) will have the resources to enforce, and how legal aid and tribunal capacity will manage longer limitation periods.
- TUPE critics argue that the service provision change test is more mechanical than the business transfer test and that the ETO exception is narrow; the government has consulted on workforce reform for public sector outsourcing (two-tier workforce) in the 2025 Act (s 32).

## Recent changes and points to check
- Future (2027) dates above are government plans, not law, until commencement regulations are made; several have already slipped (fire and rehire was earlier expected in January 2027).
- TUPE: direct consultation changes (reg 13A) apply to transfers on or after 1 July 2024.
