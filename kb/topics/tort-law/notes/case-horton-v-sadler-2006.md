# Horton v Sadler [2006] UKHL 27 (case note)

> The original judgment was not read. Reported at [2007] 1 AC 307. Judgment 14 June 2006. The panel and the outline
> of the facts are confirmed by two sources; the dates of the accident and of each action are not. No paragraph
> pinpoints are given.

## Overview
House of Lords, 14 June 2006. Panel: Lord Bingham of Cornhill, Lord Hoffmann, Lord Rodger of Earlsferry, Lord
Carswell and Lord Brown of Eaton-under-Heywood; Lord Bingham gave the leading speech. The House departed from its
earlier decision in *Walkley v Precision Forgings Ltd* [1979] 1 WLR 606, which had limited the court's discretion
under s 33 Limitation Act 1980 where an earlier action had been started in time.

## Facts
The claimant was injured in a road accident caused by the defendant, an uninsured driver, so that the Motor Insurers'
Bureau (MIB) agreement applied. He began a personal injury action within the three-year limitation period but did not
give the MIB the notice its agreement required, so the MIB would not have had to satisfy a judgment in that action. He
then started a second action, with proper notice, after the primary limitation period had expired. The defendants
relied on the limitation period under s 11 of the 1980 Act, and the judge, applying *Walkley*, ruled against the
claimant on s 33.

## Held
- The House **allowed the claimant's appeal**, so the second action could proceed.
- It departed from *Walkley*, which had held that a claimant who issued proceedings within the primary limitation
  period could not rely on s 33 to disapply the limit for a second action begun after expiry.
- The reasoning in *Walkley* was held to be unsound. The court may consider disapplying the limitation period under s 33
  in a second action even though an earlier action was brought in time.
- The Court of Appeal later confirmed, in *McDonnell v Walker*, that s 33 discretion is available in such cases.

## Applying it to a problem question
1. Work out the primary limitation period (s 11, usually three years for personal injury from the date of injury or
   knowledge) and whether it has expired.
2. Where an earlier claim was issued in time but a fresh claim has been started late, say that after *Horton v Sadler*
   the earlier action does not prevent the court exercising the s 33 discretion.
3. Apply s 33: ask whether it would be equitable to allow the action to proceed, weighing the prejudice to each party
   and the matters listed in s 33(3), including the reasons for delay and the effect on the evidence.
4. Conclude on whether the claim can proceed.
