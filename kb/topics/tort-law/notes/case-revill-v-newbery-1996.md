# Revill v Newbery [1996] QB 567 (case note)

> Case note written from consistent secondary accounts; the judgment itself is not in the knowledge base.
> Check pinpoints against the report before citing them. The defendant's name is spelt Newbery in the reports, not Newbury. Reported also at [1996] 1 All ER 291 and [1996] 2 WLR 239.

## Overview
Court of Appeal (Neill LJ, Evans LJ and Millett LJ), heard on 2 November 1995. A shotgun-wielding occupier injured a would-be burglar. The case concerns the duty owed to **trespassers** under the **Occupiers' Liability Act 1984 s 1**, and the defences of *ex turpi causa* and contributory negligence.

## Facts
Newbery, a pensioner, slept in his allotment shed because it had been broken into before. Revill and an accomplice tried to break in at night. Newbery fired a shotgun through a hole in the door, intending to scare them, and injured Revill. Revill admitted attempted burglary; Newbery was charged with wounding but acquitted. Revill sued in negligence and under the 1984 Act. Newbery relied on *ex turpi causa* and contributory negligence.

## Held
- The trial judge found Newbery negligent, his use of the shotgun being excessive, and reduced damages for Revill's own fault. The Court of Appeal **dismissed Newbery's appeal**.
- The *ex turpi causa* defence did not succeed: the claimant's criminal conduct did not remove the occupier's liability.
- An occupier cannot treat a trespasser as an **outlaw**; the 1984 Act requires the occupier to take reasonable care that the trespasser is not injured on the premises, even a trespasser who is a criminal.

## Why it matters
- Applies the 1984 Act duty to injury inflicted deliberately by an occupier, rather than by the state of the premises.
- Illustrates the limited effect of the claimant's wrongdoing: it can reduce damages (contributory negligence) but, on these facts, did not bar the claim.

## Applying it to a problem question
1. Is the claimant a trespasser, and does the duty under s 1 of the 1984 Act arise (check the statutory conditions in the Act itself)?
2. Was the force used reasonable in the circumstances, and so was the duty breached?
3. Consider *ex turpi causa* and contributory negligence, and apportion damages.
