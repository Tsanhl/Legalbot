# Undertakings and Economic Actors

## Overview
Competition rules apply only to "undertakings" (and associations of undertakings). Art 101 TFEU and CA 1998 Chapter I catch agreements between undertakings, decisions by associations of undertakings and concerted practices. Art 102 and Chapter II catch abuse by "one or more undertakings". The US Sherman Act speaks of "trusts", contracts, combinations and persons. Who counts as an undertaking decides whether the law applies at all: public bodies acting on solidarity or exercising public powers fall outside it. It also decides who is liable and fined: parent companies are liable for subsidiaries within a single economic unit,. The module treats these as "the actors playing the game" (the materials).

## The law

### 1. The functional definition of an undertaking
- An undertaking is **every entity engaged in an economic activity, regardless of its legal status and the way in which it is financed** (*Höfner and Elser*, C-41/90, para 21). The approach is functional: it looks at the activity, not the form.
- The concept may include legal persons, natural persons, public bodies and, exceptionally, even non-profit organisations (handout p 25).
- Example: the sale of package tours (with advertising contracts) for the FIFA 1990 World Cup involved economic activity.

### 2. Activities outside the concept
The rules on competition do not apply (handout p 25–26, slides p 14) to:
1. **Activities based on solidarity**, which by nature, aim and governing rules are not economic. Example: management of the public social security system (*Poucet and Pistre*, Joined Cases C-159/91 and C-160/91 [1993] ECR I-637, paras 18–19). These were sickness funds giving compulsory social protection and statutory benefits, pursuing an exclusively social purpose, which the handout likens to the NHS.
2. **Exercise of public authority powers**, for example control and supervision of air space (*SAT Fluggesellschaft*, C-364/92 [1994] ECR I-43, para 30, concerning Eurocontrol's tasks in the public interest of air navigation safety) and anti-pollution surveillance of the maritime environment (*Diego Calì & Figli*, C-343/95 [1997] ECR I-1547, paras 22–23).
- **Contrast – capitalisation schemes:** where benefits depend on the financial results of the provider's investments, the activity is economic. The handout cites a supplementary pension fund (*Albany*, C-67/97) and *Ministère de l'Agriculture et de la Pêche* (C-244/94).

### 3. Associations of undertakings
- Decisions of associations (trade associations, professional bodies) are caught by Art 101(1) and Chapter I.
- *Wouters v Algemene Raad van de Nederlandse Orde van Advocaten* (C-309/99) [2002] ECR I-1577 asked whether a Dutch Bar regulation was a decision of an association of undertakings and whether members of the Bar are undertakings. The slides pose the same question for UK solicitors and barristers.

### 4. Single economic unit and parent-subsidiary liability
- Firms in the same group can make enforceable agreements with each other unless their relationship is so close that they form a single economic entity. In that case there is no "agreement between undertakings" (handout p 26, citing *Viho*, C-73/95 P, where the subsidiaries' sales and marketing were directed and controlled by the parent).
- **Indicators of independence:** the parent's shareholding, board composition, and the extent to which the parent influences the subsidiary's policy.
- *Akzo Nobel v Commission* (C-97/08 P) [2009] ECR I-8237, paras 58–59: a subsidiary's conduct may be imputed to the parent where the subsidiary does not decide independently but carries out the parent's instructions in all material respects, having regard to economic, organisational and legal links. They then form a single undertaking.
- *AKZO Nobel v Commission* (C-516/15 P, ECLI:EU:C:2017:314):
  - para 52: unlawful conduct of a subsidiary may be attributed to the parent where the subsidiary does not determine its own market conduct independently;
  - para 53: parent and subsidiary form a single economic unit, so a single undertaking;
  - para 54: there is a **rebuttable presumption** that the parent exercises decisive influence over its subsidiary;
  - para 55: the presumption lets the Commission hold the parent responsible without further evidence;
  - para 56: the parent is held **individually liable**, deemed to have infringed the rules itself because of its decisive influence.
- Consequence: a parent may be liable for its wholly owned subsidiary's infringement even though the parent took no part in it.

### 5. States and state aid (fourth category of anti-competitive practice)
The Unit 1 slides list state aid or subsidies alongside abuse, agreements and mergers.
- **Art 107(1) TFEU:** any aid granted by a Member State or through State resources which distorts or threatens to distort competition by favouring certain undertakings or the production of certain goods is incompatible with the internal market, in so far as it affects trade between Member States. Art 107(2) lists aid that is compatible, such as social aid to individual consumers or disaster aid. Art 107(3) lists aid that may be compatible, such as regional, important projects of common European interest, sectoral development, and culture.
- **Art 106 TFEU:** Member States must not maintain measures contrary to the Treaties for public undertakings or undertakings with special or exclusive rights (Art 106(1)). Undertakings entrusted with services of general economic interest are subject to the competition rules in so far as this does not obstruct their particular tasks (Art 106(2)).
- The handbook warns that state aid injections (for example to banking) can create the false expectation that the state will always cover inefficiency.

## Key authorities
- *Höfner and Elser* (C-41/90), para 21: functional definition of undertaking.
- *Poucet and Pistre* (C-159/91, C-160/91) [1993] ECR I-637: social security based on solidarity is not economic activity.
- *SAT Fluggesellschaft* (C-364/92) [1994] ECR I-43: Eurocontrol's air space supervision is an exercise of public authority.
- *Diego Calì & Figli* (C-343/95) [1997] ECR I-1547: anti-pollution surveillance is a public authority function.
- *Albany* (C-67/97); *Ministère de l'Agriculture* (C-244/94): capitalisation-based pension schemes are economic.
- *Wouters* (C-309/99) [2002] ECR I-1577: Bar regulation as a possible decision of an association of undertakings.
- *Viho* (C-73/95 P): intra-group arrangements within a single economic unit fall outside Art 101.
- *Akzo Nobel* (C-97/08 P) [2009] and *AKZO Nobel* (C-516/15 P) [2017]: parental liability and the rebuttable presumption of decisive influence.
- Arts 106 and 107 TFEU: public undertakings and state aid.

## Debate and critique
- **Breadth of the functional test.** Because the test turns on activity, the same body may be an undertaking for some activities and not others. The solidarity/capitalisation line is delicate: compulsory, redistributive schemes fall outside, while investment-based ones fall inside. This matters for public health systems (the handout compares the NHS) and professional regulation (*Wouters*).
- **Parental liability and the presumption.** The rebuttable presumption in *AKZO Nobel* (C-516/15 P) eases the Commission's burden, but it can make a parent liable without participation. This raises questions of personal responsibility and rights of defence (see institutions-enforcement-and-procedure.md on procedural rights).
- **Big Tech groups.** In the problem questions, conduct is often spread across a parent and subsidiaries (e.g. Robotronics owned by Universal E-Salezone; UK E-Sales owned by Global E-Sales). Single-economic-unit analysis lets conduct and market shares be aggregated and lets the parent be fined.
- **Bork's observation:** no firm containing more than one person could exist without eliminating some rivalry. The single-economic-unit doctrine reflects this: competition law does not police coordination inside a firm.

## Applying it to a problem question
1. **Is each actor an undertaking?** Look for economic activity (offering goods or services on a market). Public bodies exercising official powers or running solidarity schemes are outside the rules.
2. **Is a trade or professional body involved?** Its rules or recommendations may be a "decision of an association of undertakings" (Art 101(1) / s 2).
3. **Corporate groups:** if a parent and subsidiary are involved, apply the single economic unit doctrine. For a wholly owned subsidiary, rely on the rebuttable presumption of decisive influence (C-516/15 P para 54). Then (a) intra-group agreements are not caught by Art 101, (b) the parent can be fined for the subsidiary. Name the parent as an addressee of any fine.
4. **Agents and employees** act as part of the principal's undertaking. Distinguish genuine agents from independent distributors when analysing vertical agreements.
5. **State involvement:** if a state subsidy or a special/exclusive right is in issue, consider Arts 106–107 TFEU (EU) rather than Arts 101–102.
6. **Trap:** do not treat legal form (company, charity, public body) as decisive. The test is functional.
