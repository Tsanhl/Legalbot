# Big Tech (GAFAM / "Magnificent 7") Platform Cases – EU, UK and US

## Overview
the materials studies how competition law has dealt with Google, Apple, Facebook/Meta, Amazon and Microsoft (and, in the Mag 7 report, Nvidia and Tesla). The EU has used Art 102 TFEU case by case (*Microsoft*, *Google Shopping*, *Google Android*, Apple music streaming, Meta Marketplace) and now ex ante regulation (the DMA). The UK uses CA 1998 Chapter II, collective actions before the CAT and, since 2024, the DMCCA's strategic market status regime. The US relies on litigation under Sherman Act s 2 (monopolisation): DOJ and FTC cases against Google, Meta, Amazon and Apple, plus private suits such as *Epic*. The common theories of harm are ecosystem control, self-preferencing, tying, exclusive defaults, anti-steering, data accumulation, and "buy-or-bury" acquisitions. Statuses below are as stated in the source files (the Mag 7 report is stated to be current as of 30 September 2025).

## The law

### 1. US legal framework
- **Sherman Act s 2:** monopolising, attempting to monopolise, or conspiring to monopolise inter-State or foreign trade is a felony.
- **Monopolisation has two elements** (*United States v Microsoft*, D.C. Cir. 2001, headnote 1): (1) possession of monopoly power in a relevant market, and (2) wilful acquisition or maintenance of that power, as distinguished from growth through a superior product, business acumen or historic accident.
- **Tying in the US:** in *Microsoft* (2001), the D.C. Circuit held that the rule of reason, not per se analysis, applied to tying the browser to the OS and remanded the s 1 tying claim.

### 2. US v Microsoft (D.C. Cir. 2001)
- Affirmed in part the finding that Microsoft violated s 2 by anticompetitive means to maintain its OS monopoly. Reversed the attempted monopolisation of the browser market. Remanded the s 1 tying claim (rule of reason). Vacated the remedies decree, including the divestiture plan, because liability findings did not all survive, no evidentiary hearing had been held on remedies, and the trial judge's press contacts required disqualification on remand.

### 3. Google (US)
- ***US v Google* (search; D.D.C., No. 20-cv-3010, Mehta J):** the liability ruling held that "Google is a monopolist, and it has acted as one to maintain its monopoly". For more than a decade Google paid billions for default placement with browser developers, OEMs and carriers. This prevented rivals gaining scale (queries and data), "froze" the search ecosystem and left Google with "no true competitor".
  - **Remedies opinion (2 September 2025):** no Chrome divestiture and no contingent Android divestiture ("plaintiffs overreached"). No general ban on payments for placement, because of harm to partners. Bans on exclusive contracts and on conditioning revenue share on placing other Google apps (Search, Chrome, Assistant, Gemini). Google must share certain search index and user-interaction data (not ads data) with "Qualified Competitors" and offer search and search-text-ads syndication largely on ordinary commercial terms. No choice screens (courts avoid compelling product design). Generative AI was treated as a nascent threat the remedies must protect.
- ***US v Google* (ad tech; E.D. Va., 1:23-cv-108, 17 April 2025):** Google wilfully acquired and maintained monopoly power in the open-web display publisher ad server and ad exchange markets, and unlawfully tied DFP and AdX (ss 1 and 2). No market for advertiser ad networks was proved. Remedies were to follow.
- ***Epic v Google* (9th Cir.):** affirmed the jury verdict for Epic on Android app distribution and in-app billing, and the permanent injunction.

### 4. Apple
- ***Epic v Apple* (N.D. Cal. 2021):** the court defined the market as digital mobile gaming transactions and could not conclude that Apple was a monopolist under federal or state antitrust law (despite a share over 55% and very high margins: "Success is not illegal"). But it held Apple's **anti-steering provisions** violated California's unfair competition law. They hide critical information from consumers and stifle choice, and with Apple's "incipient antitrust violations" they warranted a nationwide injunction. The Mag 7 report records that Apple was later held in contempt for violating the injunction (appeal pending).
- ***US v Apple* (D.N.J., filed 21 March 2024):** DOJ and states allege monopolisation of smartphones and "performance smartphones" through a course of conduct including suppressing "super apps", cloud gaming, degrading cross-platform messaging, and diminishing third-party smartwatch functionality (among the five categories in the complaint, per the Mag 7 report). Per the Mag 7 report, Apple's motion to dismiss was denied on 30 June 2025.
- ***US v Apple* (2013, e-books):** horizontal price-fixing with publishers (hub-and-spoke) under s 1. See cartels-leniency-and-information-exchange.md.
- **EU:** music streaming anti-steering, fined €1.84bn. DMA anti-steering non-compliance, fined €500m (appeal pending) (Mag 7 report).
- **UK (handout):** CAT class action 1403/7/7/21 on restrictive App Store terms. A 30% commission said to make it impossible for Spotify to compete with Apple Music. Apple pre-installs about 40 apps. "Sherlocking" of rival apps.

### 5. Meta / Facebook
- ***FTC v Meta* (D.D.C., No. 20-3590):** the FTC alleges monopolisation of "personal social networking" through the acquisitions of Instagram and WhatsApp ("buy-or-bury"). The court granted in part and denied in part the cross-motions for summary judgment. Meta argued the PSN market no longer exists amid competition for attention. Trial began in April 2025 (Mag 7 report).
- **EU:** Facebook Marketplace tying and use of competitors' ad data, fined €797.72m (appeal pending) (Mag 7 report). *Meta v Bundeskartellamt* (C-252/21) on data combination: see abuse-of-consumer-data-and-privacy.md.
- **UK:** CMA blocked *Facebook/Giphy* (Giphy divested to Shutterstock). CAT class action 1433/7/7/22 on unfair terms extracting data at zero price.

### 6. Amazon
- ***FTC v Amazon* (W.D. Wash., 2:23-cv-01495):** alleged monopolisation of online retail and marketplace services via anti-discounting measures and coercion to use Fulfilment by Amazon. Trial scheduled for 2027. A separate FTC "Prime" dark-patterns case settled for $2.5bn (Mag 7 report).
- **EU commitments (handout):** Amazon will refrain from using non-public seller data and apply equal treatment for Buy Box and Prime eligibility. A CMA investigation also took place.

### 7. Microsoft, Nvidia, Tesla
- **EU Microsoft:** WMP (2004), Internet Explorer (2009) (see tying-and-bundling.md). Teams tying resolved by binding commitments to unbundle (Mag 7 report).
- ***FTC v Microsoft/Activision* (9th Cir. No. 23-15992):** the FTC appealed the denial of a preliminary injunction against the $69bn acquisition. Per the Mag 7 report, the FTC's challenge failed and the merger closed (see vertical-and-conglomerate-mergers.md).
- ***Xockets v Nvidia* (W.D. Tex., 6:24-cv-453):** first amended complaint for antitrust violations and patent infringement. It alleges Nvidia and Microsoft took Xockets' patented DPU technology ("predatory infringement") with RPX. These are allegations only.
- ***Lambrix v Tesla*:** class action alleging Tesla leveraged EV power to monopolise Tesla repair services and compatible parts (aftermarkets). Dismissed by stipulation in June 2025 (Mag 7 report).

### 8. EU v US v UK approaches (Mag 7 report; study materials)
- **US:** litigation-heavy, s 2 monopolisation cases. Slow, but potentially structural remedies.
- **EU:** Art 102 cases plus the ex ante DMA, with gatekeeper "do's and don'ts" and large fines. Speed and broad application.
- **UK:** hybrid DMCCA. The Digital Markets Unit designates SMS firms and imposes tailored conduct requirements.
- **Common targets:** self-preferencing, tying, interoperability, access to data.

## Key authorities
- *United States v Microsoft Corp* (D.C. Cir. 2001): monopolisation test; rule of reason for platform tying; remedies vacated.
- *US v Google* (D.D.C. 20-cv-3010): liability and remedies (2 September 2025).
- *US v Google* (E.D. Va. 1:23-cv-108) (17 April 2025): ad tech monopolisation and tying.
- *Epic Games v Apple* (N.D. Cal. 2021); *Epic Games v Google* (9th Cir.).
- *FTC v Meta Platforms* (D.D.C. 20-3590); *FTC v Amazon* (W.D. Wash. 2023); *US v Apple* (D.N.J. 2024).
- EU: *Microsoft* (T-201/04); *Google Shopping* (C-48/22 P); *Google Android* (T-604/18).
- Sherman Act s 2.

## Debate and critique
- **Monopoly or "moligopoly"?** Petit (*Big Tech & the Digital Economy: The Moligopoly Scenario*, 2020) asks whether experts are wrong to view Big Tech as unassailable monopolies. On his view this is oligopoly territory, with competition among MAGNAF (Microsoft, Apple, Google, Netflix, Amazon, Facebook) and others. Meta's argument in *FTC v Meta* (the PSN market no longer exists) echoes this.
- **"Data-opolies" (Stucke, *Breaking Away*, 2022).** Data-opolies can cut off rivals' "oxygen" by self-preferencing, colonise next-generation ecosystems and use scale and network effects offensively to widen data and attention advantages.
- **Surveillance capitalism (Zuboff, 2019).** "There was a time when you searched Google, but now Google searches you". Platforms move from predicting behaviour to shaping it. The 2022 exam asked what this means for the concept of abuse.
- **Chirita's GAFAM typology:** data leveraging, data combination, algorithmic self-preferencing, data-driven behavioural discrimination, personalised discrimination, unfairness (excessive fees, zero price), Sherlocking, and tying. She argues Art 102 needs interpretation together with the DMA to close gaps.
- **Ordoliberal critique (Wörsdörfer).** Private power of large corporations threatens freedom. Fair treatment requires prohibiting self-preferencing, Sherlocking, unfair de-platforming and envelopment.
- **US FTC 2013 Google statement and Patterson.** Product design deference contrasts with the EU's *Google Shopping* approach (see self-preferencing-and-refusal-to-supply.md).
- **Remedies humility.** Mehta J stressed courts' lack of expertise and the risk of hobbling GenAI competition, so he rejected divestiture and choice screens. Contrast EU behavioural remedies and DMA obligations, and UK conduct requirements.
- **Neo-Brandeis movement.** It challenges consumer-welfare-only antitrust in favour of broader concerns (concentration, democracy). Chirita's "olive branch" seeks consensus (see goals-of-competition-law.md). Guggenberger ("Moderating Monopolies", 2023) is on the reading list.

## Applying it to a problem question
1. **Identify the ecosystem:** core platform (search, OS, app store, marketplace, social network, AI assistant), adjacent markets, and data flows.
2. **Jurisdictions:** EU (Art 102, DMA), UK (Chapter II, DMCCA SMS), and US if asked (s 2, with the two-element monopolisation test).
3. **Map conduct to theories of harm:**
   - default and pre-installation deals → exclusive dealing/tying (*US v Google*, *Android*);
   - ranking rivals lower → self-preferencing (*Google Shopping*);
   - anti-steering and commissions → exploitative and exclusionary (*Epic v Apple*, Apple music streaming);
   - data combination across services → data abuse (*Meta*);
   - acquiring nascent rivals → merger control or s 2 "buy-or-bury" (*FTC v Meta*).
4. **Market power:** zero-price services are still economic activity (*Google Shopping*). Use non-price parameters and data/scale barriers. Address "moligopoly" or GenAI-disruption arguments.
5. **Justifications:** security, privacy, integrated user experience, quality. Courts test pretext and proportionality.
6. **Remedies:** behavioural (equal treatment, no exclusive defaults, data sharing or syndication), structural (rarely: Chrome divestiture rejected), and regulatory alternatives (DMA/DMCCA).
7. **Use precise statuses:** many US matters are complaints or pending. Do not describe allegations as findings.
