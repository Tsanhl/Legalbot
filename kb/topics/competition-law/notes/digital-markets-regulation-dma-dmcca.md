# Digital Markets Regulation – EU Digital Markets Act and UK DMCCA 2024

## Overview
Art 102 TFEU and Chapter II CA 1998 work ex post and case by case. They require market definition, dominance and proof of abuse, and cases such as *Google Shopping* took years. Both the EU and the UK have therefore added **ex ante** regimes for the largest digital platforms. The **EU Digital Markets Act** (Regulation (EU) 2022/1925, "on contestable and fair markets in the digital sector") designates "gatekeepers" providing "core platform services" and imposes a fixed list of obligations and prohibitions. The **UK Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024** (DMCCA) lets the CMA designate firms with **strategic market status (SMS)** in a digital activity and impose tailored **conduct requirements** and **pro-competition interventions (PCIs)**. The Mag 7 report calls the UK model a hybrid of EU-style ex ante rules and US-style case-by-case flexibility. These regimes run in parallel with competition law and are central to the discussion and to the digital problem questions.

## The law

### A. EU Digital Markets Act (as described by Wörsdörfer, 2023)
Wörsdörfer notes that his direct quotations are from the 2020 DMA proposal. Article numbers below follow his account, and the final Regulation may number some provisions differently.
1. **Objectives:** contestability and fairness of digital markets, lower entry barriers, a level playing field, and overall consumer welfare (Art 1). It also aims to harmonise rules and reduce national fragmentation.
2. **Core platform services (CPS):** online intermediation (e.g. app stores), search engines, social networks, video-sharing, interpersonal communications, operating systems, cloud computing, advertising services (Art 2).
3. **Gatekeeper criteria (Art 3) – three-criteria test:**
   - significant impact on the internal market: EEA turnover of at least €7.5bn in the last three years, or average market capitalisation of at least €75bn (compromise figures; the proposal said €6.5bn / €65bn), and CPS in at least three Member States;
   - an important gateway: at least 45 million monthly active end users and more than 10,000 yearly active business users in the EU;
   - an entrenched and durable position (thresholds met in the past three years).
   Firms must notify. The Commission reviews status at least every two years (Art 4).
4. **Obligations (Arts 5 and 6):** a "black list" of directly applicable prohibitions and a "grey list" of prescriptions (de Streel and Larouche's "18 Commandments"). They include:
   - no combining personal data from different sources without consent;
   - no MFN or parity clauses, and no anti-steering;
   - no bundling or tying;
   - allowing uninstallation of pre-installed software and side-loading of apps and app stores;
   - no self-preferencing, with FRAND access to rankings;
   - no restrictions on switching or multi-homing;
   - interoperability, data portability, and real-time data access;
   - FRAND access to ranking, query, click and view data for search engines.
5. **Flexibility:** suspension for economic viability (Art 8). Exemptions for public morality, health and security (Art 9). Updating obligations after a market investigation (Art 10).
6. **Mergers and profiling:** gatekeepers must inform the Commission of intended concentrations involving digital services (Art 12 in his numbering). They must submit an independently audited description of consumer-profiling techniques (Art 13).
7. **Enforcement:** the Commission is sole enforcer. Market investigations (gatekeeper designation; systematic non-compliance, meaning at least three non-compliance or fining decisions in five years, permitting behavioural or structural remedies; new CPS). Information requests, including access to databases and algorithms. Interviews and on-site inspections. Interim measures. Commitments.
   - **Fines:** up to 10% of total worldwide turnover, and up to 20% for repeat offences. Up to 1% for procedural breaches. Periodic penalties up to 5% of average daily turnover. The CJEU has unlimited jurisdiction.
   - **In practice:** the Mag 7 report records an Apple fine of €500m for non-compliance with DMA anti-steering obligations (appeal pending).
8. The discussion notes the DMA protects the right to opt for a less personalised experience without deception or manipulation into consent, and bans combining data from multiple sources (Reg 2022/1925).

### B. UK DMCCA 2024 – Part 1 (Digital Markets)
1. **Designation (s 2):** the CMA may designate an undertaking as having SMS in respect of a **digital activity** where (a) the activity is linked to the UK, and (b) the undertaking meets the **SMS conditions**: (i) **substantial and entrenched market power** and (ii) a **position of strategic significance**, in respect of that activity. Designation requires an SMS investigation (s 2(4)) and is subject to the turnover condition (s 2(3)).
2. **Digital activities (s 3):** providing a service by means of the internet, or digital content, whether or not for consideration, and connected activities. Similar or combinable activities may be treated as one.
3. **UK link (s 4):** a significant number of UK users; or the undertaking carries on business in the UK in relation to the activity; or the activity is likely to have an immediate, substantial and foreseeable effect on UK trade.
4. **Substantial and entrenched market power (s 5):** a forward-looking assessment over at least 5 years, including developments expected or foreseeable absent designation.
5. **Strategic significance (s 6):** one or more of: (a) significant size or scale; (b) a significant number of other undertakings use the activity in carrying on their business; (c) the position would allow it to extend market power to a range of other activities; (d) the position allows it to determine or substantially influence how other undertakings conduct themselves.
6. **Turnover condition (s 7):** global group turnover above **£25bn** or UK group turnover above **£1bn** in the relevant 12-month period. The Secretary of State may amend these by regulations (affirmative procedure).
7. **Conduct requirements (ss 19–20):** imposed by notice. They must be proportionate for one or more objectives: **fair dealing**, **open choices**, **trust and transparency** (s 19(5)). Permitted types (as summarised in the discussion from the Bill) include obliging the firm to trade on fair and reasonable terms, provide clear and accurate information, and present options and defaults that allow informed choices. They may also prevent:
   - discriminatory terms;
   - self-preferencing, including through access to data;
   - activities that materially increase market power or bolster strategic significance;
   - requiring or incentivising use of its other products (tying);
   - restricting interoperability;
   - restricting how users use the activity;
   - **using data unfairly**;
   - restricting users from using other undertakings' products.
8. **Countervailing benefits exemption (s 29):** the CMA must close a conduct investigation where the undertaking shows that the conduct:
   - (a) gives rise to benefits to users or potential users;
   - (b) those benefits outweigh any actual or likely detriment to competition from the breach;
   - (c) the benefits could not be realised without the conduct;
   - (d) the conduct is proportionate to realising them; and
   - (e) it does not eliminate or prevent effective competition.
   The conduct is then treated as no breach (s 29(3)).
9. **Findings and enforcement orders (ss 30–31):** findings within 6 months of the conduct investigation notice. Enforcement orders to stop the breach, prevent recurrence and address damage.
10. **Pro-competition interventions (s 46):** after a PCI investigation, where a factor or combination of factors relating to a relevant digital activity has an **adverse effect on competition** and a PCI would be proportionate to remedy, mitigate or prevent it. The CMA may consider benefits to UK users. A PCI may be a pro-competition order or recommendations to public bodies. An adverse effect means preventing, restricting or distorting competition in connection with the activity in the UK (s 46(5)).
11. **Merger reporting (s 57):** an SMS firm must report before completion certain events. These include acquiring qualifying status in shares or voting rights in a UK-connected body corporate where consideration is at least **£25m**, and certain joint ventures.
12. **Penalties (s 86):** fixed penalties up to **10%** of worldwide (group) turnover. Daily penalties up to **5%** of daily turnover. Separate penalties for investigative breaches, including on named senior managers (s 87).

## Debate and critique
- **Ordoliberal evaluation (Wörsdörfer 2023).** The DMA reflects ordoliberal concern that private power of large corporations threatens freedom. It reverses the burden: firms must show compliance rather than authorities proving infringements. For fair treatment of business and end users, it is important to prohibit discriminatory practices such as self-preferencing, Sherlocking, unfair de-platforming and envelopment. Wörsdörfer critically assesses gaps, for example the European Parliament's call to add browsers, connected TV and virtual assistants as CPS.
- **Data recalibration (Baschenhof 2022).** The DMA's data obligations (access rights and limits on collecting, combining and using data) contain significant ambiguities and could even make "Big Tech" the winners of an act designed to tackle them. They might also favour Chinese tech firms against US competitors in the EU. Still, it is a positive first step towards contestable markets and freer data flows, once enforcement and case law clarify it.
- **Chirita, evidence to HM Treasury's Digital Competition Expert Panel.** Digital markets bring benefits (a "digital renaissance", choice and reach to remote areas, e-payments). But there is a tendency toward high concentration across social media, search and ads, PC software, mobile ecosystems, cloud, data analytics and online distribution. Harms include less privacy, more targeted advertising, large-scale accumulation of sensitive data for marketing and strategic pricing, fear of surveillance, and higher prices from online price discrimination. Drivers include first-mover advantage, free-of-charge models and undercutting rivals.
- **Relationship with Art 102 / Chapter II.** The conclusion is that legislative gaps (AI predictive analytics, personalised offers) may be closed by interpreting Art 102 together with the DMA's provisions on data combination, digital tying and algorithmic discrimination. The UK SMS test ("substantial and entrenched market power" plus "strategic significance") is distinct from dominance. The countervailing benefits exemption resembles Art 101(3) / s 9 but adds "does not eliminate or prevent effective competition".
- **Comparative models (Mag 7 report).** The EU emphasises speed and broad, uniform obligations. The UK offers tailored, firm-specific requirements. The US relies on slow s 2 litigation. All converge on self-preferencing, tying, interoperability and data access.

## Applying it to a problem question
1. **UK facts:** consider whether the firm could be designated with SMS:
   - turnover (£25bn global / £1bn UK);
   - a digital activity linked to the UK (significant UK users; business in the UK);
   - substantial and entrenched power (5-year forward look);
   - strategic significance (scale; many business users depend on it; ability to extend power, e.g. from e-commerce into AI assistants or delivery).
   Note that designation requires a CMA investigation, so advise on likely conduct requirements rather than existing ones unless the facts say the firm is designated.
2. **Match conduct to conduct requirements:**
   - ranking rivals' brands at the end of page 2 → self-preferencing;
   - denying discounts to rival brands → discriminatory terms;
   - terms surrendering "customer experience data" or sharing location with third parties → using data unfairly, and trust and transparency;
   - non-interoperable assistants → restricting interoperability;
   - bundled subscriptions → requiring use of other products.
3. **Defence:** argue the countervailing benefits exemption (innovation, convenience, environmental pledge discounts). Test all five limbs, including indispensability and no elimination of competition.
4. **Mergers by SMS firms:** acquisitions over £25m must be reported (s 57). Link to data-driven-mergers.md.
5. **EU facts:** check gatekeeper thresholds (45m users, 10,000 business users, turnover or market cap). Apply Art 5/6 obligations (no data combination without consent, no self-preferencing, allow uninstalling pre-installed apps, no anti-steering). Fines up to 10% (20% repeat).
6. **Run both tracks:** DMA/DMCCA compliance and Art 102 / Chapter II liability. The regimes are cumulative.
