# Predatory Pricing and Margin Squeeze

## Overview
Low prices are normally what competition is for, so competition law intervenes against price cuts only cautiously. **Predatory pricing** is where a dominant firm prices below an appropriate measure of cost to eliminate or discipline a rival, intending to raise prices later. **Margin squeeze** is where a vertically integrated dominant firm sets the gap between its upstream (wholesale/access) price and its downstream (retail) price so narrow that an equally efficient downstream rival cannot trade profitably. Both are exclusionary abuses under Art 102 TFEU and s 18 CA 1998. The EU tests are cost-based (*AKZO*), inspired by Areeda and Turner's 1975 article. EU law does not require proof of recoupment (*Tetra Pak II*, *France Télécom*). Algorithms and personalised pricing raise new questions about targeted predation.

## The law

### 1. Predatory pricing – the AKZO cost tests (EU)
From *AKZO Chemie v Commission* (C-62/86) [1991] paras 70–72, as summarised in the handout and slides:
1. **Prices below average variable cost (AVC)** charged by a dominant undertaking are abusive. The only interest the undertaking can have in such prices is eliminating competitors, so that it can later raise prices using its monopoly position. AVC are costs that vary with the quantities produced.
2. **Prices below average total cost (ATC) but above AVC** are abusive if they are determined as part of a **plan for eliminating a competitor** (intent required).
3. Prices above ATC are, by implication, not predatory under these tests.
- Confirmed and applied in *Michelin II* (T-203/01, paras 241–242) and *France Télécom (Wanadoo)* (T-340/03, paras 130, 195).

### 2. Further principles
- **Failure to achieve the result is no defence:** that the hoped-for result is not achieved does not prevent abuse (*Compagnie Maritime Belge* paras 104, 109; *Irish Sugar* T-228/97 para 191; *France Télécom* T-340/03 para 196).
- **Recoupment not required (EU):** in *Tetra Pak II* (C-333/94 P, paras 41–44) proof of intention to eliminate and proof of a realistic chance of recouping losses were not necessary in the circumstances. In *France Télécom* (C-202/07 P, paras 37, 113) proof of recoupment is not a necessary precondition for a finding of predatory pricing.
- **Meeting competition is no absolute defence:** Wanadoo could not rely on an absolute right to align its prices on competitors' (T-340/03, para 187). Selective price cuts by a dominant liner conference to match a competitor were abusive (*Compagnie Maritime Belge*).
- **Unfair competition comparison:** under unfair competition law, predatory pricing is a price not covering costs that is objectively capable of squeezing competitors out and is used to do so (handout p 37).

### 3. Selective low pricing and incremental cost (Post Danmark I)
*Post Danmark* (C-209/10) [2012] concerned secondary-line price discrimination: lower prices to a rival's customers. The handout and slides set out the ruling:
- The Danish authority could not prove intent to eliminate the rival.
- Prices were below ATC (average incremental costs plus a share of common costs) but above **average incremental costs** (costs that would disappear in the short or medium term if the activity were given up).
- Such pricing cannot be considered abusive "simply because" it is below ATC but above average incremental cost. As a general rule an **as-efficient competitor** could match such prices without unsustainable long-term losses.
- It is necessary to consider whether the pricing policy, without objective justification, produces an **actual or likely exclusionary effect** to the detriment of competition and consumers.
- The firm may show objective necessity or outweighing efficiencies that benefit consumers.

### 4. Margin squeeze
- **Definition (Commission, *Wanadoo España v Telefónica*, COMP/38.784, para 312):** the margin between the upstream access price charged to competitors and the dominant operator's downstream price is insufficient to allow a reasonably efficient downstream provider to obtain a normal profit.
- ***Deutsche Telekom* (C-280/08 P) [2010]** (on appeal from T-271/03 [2008]; Commission decision 2003/707/EC): the CJ upheld a €12.6m fine for abuse on the market for direct access to its landline (local loop) services.
  - DT charged competitors wholesale access prices that, compared with its own retail prices, forced competitors to charge end-users more than DT did.
  - Regulation of wholesale prices by the national authority did not prevent the abuse. The GC was right to find that DT's price squeeze was unfair to end-users.
  - The squeeze strengthened DT's dominance and limited consumer choice and the prospect of lasting lower retail prices.
  - **Test (as summarised in the handout):** whether the spread is negative or insufficient to cover DT's own product-specific downstream costs. This is an as-efficient competitor test using the dominant firm's own charges and costs.
  - Local loop access was **indispensable**. No alternative infrastructure permitted viable entry. The handout links this to essential facilities reasoning and to "equality of opportunity".
  - Art 102 "aims to protect consumers" (para 180).
- Margin squeeze can be treated as an autonomous abuse. Faella and Pardolesi (cited in the handout) question this, arguing that a theory entitling non-integrated rivals to a "fair" margin has no place in competition law and that the focus should be exclusion.

### 5. US contrast (Areeda and Turner)
Areeda and Turner, "Predatory Pricing and Related Practices under Section 2 of the Sherman Act" (1975) 88 Harv L Rev 697, concluded:
- In principle, a short-run profit-maximising or loss-minimising price is not predatory even if below average cost. A price at or above average cost is not predatory.
- Because marginal cost data are typically unavailable, they used AVC as a surrogate: a price at or above reasonably anticipated AVC should be **conclusively presumed lawful**, and a price below it **conclusively presumed unlawful**.
- Below that level a monopolist cannot defend on the grounds that the price was "promotional" or met a rival's equally low price.
- *AKZO*'s first limb tracks the AVC benchmark, but EU law adds an intent-based middle band (AVC–ATC) and drops recoupment.

## Key authorities
- *AKZO Chemie v Commission* (C-62/86) [1991] ECR I-3359, paras 69–74: AVC/ATC tests. Organic peroxides, 50% share.
- *Tetra Pak II* (C-333/94 P) [1996], paras 41–44: no need to prove recoupment or intent in that case.
- *Compagnie Maritime Belge* (C-395/96 P etc.) [2000], paras 104, 109, 117: failure to succeed no defence. Selective price matching abusive.
- *Irish Sugar* (T-228/97) [1999], paras 186–191.
- *France Télécom (Wanadoo)* (T-340/03) [2007], paras 130, 185–196, 227; (C-202/07 P) [2009], paras 37, 105, 113: predation in broadband. Recoupment unnecessary. No absolute right to align prices.
- *Post Danmark I* (C-209/10) [2012]: incremental-cost benchmark. Pricing between AIC and ATC is not abusive per se. Effects required.
- *Deutsche Telekom* (C-280/08 P) [2010]: margin squeeze. €12.6m fine upheld.
- Commission, *Wanadoo España v Telefónica* (COMP/38.784), para 312: margin squeeze definition.
- Areeda and Turner (1975): AVC rule.

## Debate and critique
- **Intent versus cost rules.** *AKZO*'s middle band relies on proving a "plan" to eliminate, which brings in documents and motives. The handout notes that the subjective approach effectively requires an actual exit, and that a smaller rival cannot eliminate a more powerful one. Areeda and Turner preferred clear cost-based presumptions to protect genuine price competition.
- **Recoupment.** EU law does not require proof of recoupment (*France Télécom*). Lesley's work (below) suggests that data-driven barriers make recoupment easier in digital markets.
- **Algorithmic predation (Lesley, "Predatory Pricing Algorithms" (2023) 98 NYU L Rev, as summarised in the file).** Personalised pricing lets a predator target below-cost prices only at a rival's customers while charging loyal, price-insensitive customers high prices. Loyal customers thus cross-subsidise the price war, which lowers the cost of predation. After the rival exits, prices rise for all. Lesley uses *Standard Oil* (1911) as the analogue precedent and Uber's differential surge pricing as a modern example. Big data also creates barriers to re-entry that make recoupment easier.
- **Amazon's "pricing paradox" (Harvard JOLT 2023, as summarised in the file).** The authors argue that Amazon's low-price image masks higher effective prices. Their study of 4,800 products found the best deal on page one was on average in the 17th slot. "Burying", "complexifying" (Prime fees, missing unit prices, defaults), "anchoring" and personalised pricing were used. They argue antitrust has been distracted by the low-price perception, and they propose monitoring and data-sharing reforms. This links predation and low pricing to behavioural exploitation (see excessive-pricing-and-price-discrimination.md).
- **Margin squeeze as a standalone abuse.** Faella and Pardolesi argue rivals have no entitlement to a fair margin. *Deutsche Telekom* rests on the as-efficient competitor test and indispensability, which keeps the focus on exclusion.

## Applying it to a problem question
1. **Dominance** in the market where the low prices are charged (and, for margin squeeze, upstream dominance over an indispensable input).
2. **Identify the price and the cost benchmark.** If cost data are given, compare price with AVC (or average avoidable cost) and ATC. If not, flag what evidence is needed.
3. **Below AVC:** presumptively abusive (*AKZO*).
4. **Between AVC and ATC:** look for a plan to eliminate. Memos such as "increase UK prices upon rival's imminent departure" or "eliminate rival before any future price increases" are strong evidence. Promotional discounts that are temporary and seasonal (e.g. 20% winter/spring sales) may be legitimate competition.
5. **Selective or targeted pricing** to a rival's customers: apply *Post Danmark I* (incremental costs, as-efficient competitor, likely exclusionary effect). Consider algorithmic personalised pricing (Lesley).
6. **Recoupment:** not needed in the EU, but evidence of planned later price rises strengthens the case.
7. **Margin squeeze:** compare the wholesale price charged to rivals with the dominant firm's retail price minus its own downstream costs. A negative or insufficient spread suggests abuse (*Deutsche Telekom*). Regulation of wholesale prices is no defence if the firm had pricing freedom.
8. **Justifications:** meeting competition (limited), loss-leading promotions, launching a new product, economies of scale. Test necessity and proportionality.
9. **Small firm trap:** a non-dominant firm (e.g. a rival with an "unstable and low market share") cannot commit predation under Art 102 / s 18.
