# Contract Law: answer rules

## Learned from testing
- In contract problems, work through every route the facts raise, in this order: incorporation of any onerous or small-print term (signature, notice, course of dealing), construction, statutory control (UCTA 1977 between businesses, CRA 2015 Part 2 for consumers), misrepresentation and any collateral warranty or term, then remedies; state the effect of each step on the claim. [2026-11-21 · ref 2026-11-21-2159 (early test run; see its changes.md)]
- For damages claims, address remoteness, mitigation and causation as separate steps, applying each to the facts, rather than naming them. [2026-11-21 · ref 2026-11-21-2159/q03]

## Everyday questions (learned from testing)
- Discuss formation (agreement, certainty, consideration, intention to create legal relations) only where the facts put an element in doubt, e.g. an informal or family promise or no agreed price; otherwise say in one line that there is a contract and go to breach and remedy. [2026-11-21 · ref 2026-11-21-2159 (early test run; see its changes.md)]
- Where work or a benefit came before the promise to pay, say whether the consideration is past: past consideration is no consideration unless the act was done at the promisor's request, both understood it would be paid for, and payment would have been enforceable if promised in advance (*Lampleigh v Brathwait*; *Pao On v Lau Yiu Long*). [2026-11-21 · ref 2026-11-21-2159 (early test run; see its changes.md)]
- For payment claims, say whether the claim is for the agreed sum (debt) or a reasonable sum (quantum meruit, mainly where no price was fixed or there is no enforceable contract). Where no price is set for a service, a trader supplying a consumer must charge a reasonable price under CRA 2015 s 51; s 15 Supply of Goods and Services Act 1982 now covers only non-consumer contracts.
