# Leaf v International Galleries [1950] 2 KB 86 (case note)

> Case note written from consistent secondary accounts; the judgment itself is not in the knowledge base.
> Check pinpoints against the report before citing them.

## Overview
Court of Appeal (Denning LJ gave the leading judgment, with Jenkins LJ and Lord Evershed MR). The case is cited for two points: the **lapse of time bar** to rescission for innocent misrepresentation, and the rule that a mistake about the **quality** (here, the artist) of the subject matter does not make the contract void.

## Facts
The buyer bought a painting that the seller described as by Constable. Five years later, when he tried to sell it, he was told it was not by Constable. He then claimed to rescind the contract for innocent misrepresentation and recover the price.

## Held
- The seller's attribution was a misrepresentation (innocent), but **rescission was refused**: the buyer had kept the painting for five years, far longer than a reasonable time.
- Time ran from the date of the contract (and delivery), not from the buyer's discovery of the truth.
- The claim based on **common mistake** failed: the buyer bought and received that specific painting; the mistake about its artist went to quality, not to the essence of the subject matter.
- The buyer had claimed only rescission and no damages, so he was left with no remedy.

## Why it matters
- Standard example of the bar of **affirmation/lapse of time**; the contract is not rescindable for innocent misrepresentation after a reasonable time has passed.
- Later authority has treated lapse of time as a bar only where rescission would be inequitable in all the circumstances, and notes that *Leaf* pre-dates the Misrepresentation Act 1967 (see *Salt v Stratstone Specialist Ltd* [2015] EWCA Civ 745, referred to in secondary commentary).
- Mistake as to quality: compare the mistake material.

## Applying it to a problem question
1. Identify the misrepresentation and the date of the contract.
2. Ask whether the claimant has delayed or affirmed; under *Leaf*, time runs from the contract, though modern authority asks whether delay makes rescission inequitable.
3. Consider damages under s 2(1) or s 2(2) Misrepresentation Act 1967 if rescission is barred.
