# Dolphin Maritime & Aviation Services Ltd v Sveriges Angfartygs Assurans Forening (The Swedish Club) [2009] EWHC 716 (Comm) (case note)

> This is a Commercial Court decision on the **Contracts (Rights of Third Parties) Act 1999**, s 1(1)(b), applied to a
> letter of undertaking. It is not, on the sources found, a case about exclusion or jurisdiction clauses in the 1999
> Act sense. The detail below is taken from a text summary of the judgment and two secondary summaries; check the
> report ([2009] 2 Lloyd's Rep 123) before citing.

## Overview
High Court, Queen's Bench Division (Commercial Court), Christopher Clarke J; hearing 18 February 2009, judgment 2 April
2009. The claimant, an English cargo recovery agent, tried to enforce a letter of undertaking (LOU) issued by a
Swedish P&I club, as a third party under the 1999 Act. The contract claim failed at summary judgment. The tort claims
against the club were not struck out for want of jurisdiction.

## Facts
The *New Flame* collided with another vessel off Gibraltar in 2007 and was lost with her cargo of scrap steel. Cargo
underwriters paid the cargo owners and instructed Dolphin to recover the loss. Dolphin's terms with the underwriters
required recoveries to be paid to Dolphin. The club, the vessel owner's P&I insurer, issued an LOU addressed to the
cargo interests care of Dolphin, promising payment of sums due either to Dolphin on the underwriters' behalf or to
solicitors they appointed. The club then negotiated directly with the underwriters and paid a settlement to them,
bypassing Dolphin and its commission. Dolphin sued in contract (as a third party to the LOU) and in tort (inducing
breach of contract and unlawful means conspiracy).

## Held
- **Section 1(1)(b).** The payment provision in the LOU did not "purport to confer a benefit" on Dolphin. It was a
  mechanism by which the club could discharge its obligation to the underwriters, who were the intended
  beneficiaries. Any benefit to Dolphin (receipt of payment, or deduction of its fee) was incidental. Summary
  judgment was given for the club on the contract claim.
- The LOU also did not show an intention that Dolphin should have an enforceable right.
- **Jurisdiction.** The club applied to strike out the tort claims; the court refused. Applying art 5(3) of the
  Judgments Regulation, the harm (loss of commission) was treated as occurring in England, so the English court had
  jurisdiction over those claims. Whether they would succeed was not decided.

## Effect
- A useful illustration that s 1(1)(b) requires the term to purport to confer a benefit on the third party; mere
  incidental benefit is not enough.
- The claim also shows that a third party who cannot sue on the contract may consider tort claims against the
  promisor, which engage separate jurisdiction rules.

## Applying it to a problem question
1. Ask whether the term in question **purports to confer a benefit** on the third party (s 1(1)(b)) rather than
   merely benefiting them incidentally, for instance by describing how the promisor may perform.
2. Look for an express right of enforcement (s 1(1)(a)) as well.
3. Even where s 1(1)(b) is satisfied, check whether the parties did not intend the term to be enforceable by the third
   party (s 1(2)).
4. If the third party has no direct contractual claim, consider whether any tort claim is available and where it may
   be brought.
