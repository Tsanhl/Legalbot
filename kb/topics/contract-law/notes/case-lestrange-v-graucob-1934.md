# L'Estrange v F Graucob Ltd [1934] 2 KB 394 (case note)

> Case note written from two consistent secondary accounts; the judgment itself is not in the knowledge base.
> One source styles the defendant "E. Graucob"; the Wikipedia account uses "F Graucob". Check the report for the correct form.

## Overview
Court of Appeal. The case states the **signature rule**: a person who signs a written contract is bound by its terms, including an exclusion clause, whether or not they read it.

## Facts
L'Estrange agreed to buy a cigarette vending machine from Graucob and signed a "Sales Agreement". Small print in the document excluded implied conditions and warranties (including statutory ones as to quality or fitness). She did not read it. The machine did not work properly, and she refused to continue paying and sued. The county court judge found for her, applying the "reasonable notice" approach from the ticket cases.

## Held
- The Court of Appeal found for Graucob. The exclusion clause formed part of the contract and **excluded the implied terms**.
- Signing bound her to the contents, even though she had not read them or understood their effect. The notice requirements of the ticket cases do not apply to signed documents.
- Per the Wikipedia account, Maugham LJ agreed but expressed regret at the result.

## Why it matters
- Core authority on **incorporation by signature**; the starting point before considering misrepresentation, non est factum or statutory control.
- Modern consumer and unfair-terms legislation would now prevent such an exclusion in a consumer sale.

## Applying it to a problem question
1. Was the document signed? If so, begin from *L'Estrange*: the signatory is bound.
2. Then test for qualifications (misrepresentation about the document's effect, non est factum) and for statutory control (UCTA 1977, Consumer Rights Act 2015).
3. If unsigned, use the notice rules instead.
