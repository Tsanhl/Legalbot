# Clarke v Dickson (1858) El Bl & El 148; 120 ER 463 (case note)

> Case note written from two consistent secondary accounts; the judgment itself is not in the knowledge base. One source gives the report as EB & E 148; the other as El Bl & El 148, 120 ER 463.
> Check pinpoints against the report before citing them.

## Overview
Court of Queen's Bench, 1858 (Lord Campbell CJ, Erle J and Crompton J; Crompton J gave the main reasoning). The case illustrates the common law requirement of **restitutio in integrum**: a party cannot rescind a contract if they cannot give back what they received.

## Facts
Clarke was persuaded, he said by the fraud of the defendants, to take shares in the Welsh Potosi Lead and Copper Mining Company, a business run on the cost-book principle. He paid deposits and over time took further shares in place of dividends. The business failed and was wound up. After discovering the alleged misrepresentations he sued to recover his deposits as money paid, claiming the contract was rescinded.

## Held
- He could not rescind and recover the money in this action. A contract induced by fraud is **voidable, not void**, and to rescind the claimant had to be able to restore the parties to their original position.
- That was impossible because his original interest had become shares in a company being wound up.
- His remedy, if any, lay in an **action for deceit** for the actual loss caused by the fraud.

## Why it matters
- Classic statement of the **restitutio in integrum** bar to rescission at common law.
- Shows the strictness of the common law rule, later relaxed in equity (substantial restitution can be enough); mention as context only and verify elsewhere.
- Contrasts with the equitable approach in cases like *Redgrave v Hurd* and with other bars in *Leaf v International Galleries*.

## Applying it to a problem question
1. Ask whether the claimant can make counter-restitution (return the asset, or its substantial equivalent).
2. If rescission is barred, consider damages (deceit for fraud; Misrepresentation Act 1967 s 2(1) or s 2(2)).
3. State the bar and cite *Clarke v Dickson* for the rule, noting the approach in equity.
