# St Albans City and District Council v International Computers Ltd [1996] EWCA Civ 1296, [1996] 4 All ER 481 (case note)

> Case note written from consistent secondary accounts; the judgment itself is not in the knowledge base.
> Court of Appeal, 26 July 1996. Sir Iain Glidewell and Nourse LJ are named in the accounts read; the full panel was not confirmed. Check the report before citing pinpoints.

## Overview
Court of Appeal. The case is a leading illustration of the **reasonableness test for limitation clauses under the Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977** in a business-to-business supply of software.

## Facts
In 1988 the council contracted with ICL for a computer system (COMCIS) to administer the community charge. A population figure extracted from the system in December 1989 overstated the number of chargepayers by 2,966, so the charge was set too low. The council's agreed losses were a £484,000 shortfall in receipts and £685,000 of additional expenditure. ICL's standard terms capped liability at £100,000. The trial judge (Scott Baker J) found the cap unreasonable and awarded about £1.3 million.

## Held
- UCTA s 3 applied because ICL dealt on its written standard terms; negotiation over the clause did not automatically take it outside s 3.
- The trial judge's finding that the cap failed the s 11 reasonableness test was not interfered with: an appellate court will intervene only if the lower court erred in principle or was plainly wrong (following *George Mitchell v Finney Lock Seeds*).
- Factors against ICL included its size and its £50 million worldwide product liability insurance, the weaker bargaining position of the council, and that the loss was better borne by ICL than by local taxpayers.
- The appeal succeeded only on quantum: the £484,000 shortfall was disallowed (chargepayers had made it up the following year), though interest for the delay remained recoverable.

## Why it matters
- Frequently cited on the weight given to **insurance, bargaining power and resources** in the UCTA reasonableness assessment.
- Shows that a business can still be protected by UCTA where the supplier uses standard terms.

## Applying it to a problem question
1. Does UCTA s 3 apply (written standard terms, or dealing as consumer)?
2. Apply s 11 and Schedule 2: bargaining strength, whether the cap was negotiated or justified, availability and cost of insurance, and who can better bear the loss.
3. Remember the appellate deference point if the facts involve an earlier finding on reasonableness.
