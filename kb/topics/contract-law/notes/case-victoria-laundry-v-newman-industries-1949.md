# Victoria Laundry (Windsor) Ltd v Newman Industries Ltd [1949] 2 KB 528 (case note)

> Case note written from consistent secondary accounts; the judgment itself is not in the knowledge base.
> Check pinpoints against the report before citing them.

## Overview
Court of Appeal (Asquith LJ gave the leading judgment). The case refines *Hadley v Baxendale* for **lost profits**: ordinary profits flowing from late delivery are recoverable where the seller knew of the buyer's business and intended use, but profits from exceptionally lucrative contracts are not unless the seller knew enough to be treated as accepting that risk.

## Facts
Victoria Laundry ordered a large boiler from Newman Industries for its laundry and dyeing business. Newman knew the nature of the business and that the boiler was wanted for immediate use. Delivery came about five months late. The laundry claimed lost profits, including profits it would have earned on particularly lucrative dyeing contracts it could not take on without the boiler.

## Held
- Newman was liable for the **ordinary loss of profit** that the laundry would normally have made from using the boiler during the delay, because it knew of the intended immediate use.
- It was **not liable for the extraordinary profits** from the exceptionally lucrative contracts, which it did not know about and which were a different, higher risk.
- Asquith LJ rejected a rule giving complete indemnity for every loss actually caused by breach, however improbable, as too harsh in contract.

## Why it matters
- Reformulates *Hadley* around what the defendant knew, or ought reasonably to have foreseen, at the time of contracting.
- Standard authority for splitting lost profits into ordinary and extraordinary heads.
- Some summaries simplify the result to "lost profits recovered"; the accurate position is the split above.

## Applying it to a problem question
1. State what the defendant knew about the claimant's business and the purpose of the goods or service.
2. Treat normal commercial profits as recoverable if that knowledge was held, but exceptional contracts as recoverable only if communicated.
3. Compare *The Achilleas* where the loss is unquantifiable or outside market expectation.
