# Derry v Peek (1889) 14 App Cas 337 (case note)

> Case note written from consistent secondary accounts; BAILII was not accessible and the judgment itself is not in the knowledge base.
> Check pinpoints against the report before citing them.

## Overview
House of Lords, decided 1 July 1889; Lord Herschell's speech is the source of the classic definition of **fraud** in the tort of deceit. The case is cited for the rule that **carelessness is not fraud**.

## Facts
A tramway company's prospectus stated that it had the right to run its trams by steam power. That right in fact depended on Board of Trade consent. The directors expected consent to be a formality; it was refused and the company was wound up. Shareholders who had bought shares relying on the prospectus sued the directors in deceit.

## Held
- The claim failed: it had not been shown that the directors lacked an honest belief in what they stated.
- Fraud is proved where a false statement is made (i) knowingly, (ii) without belief in its truth, or (iii) recklessly, careless whether it is true or false.
- Making a false statement through want of care falls short of fraud. Lack of reasonable grounds for a belief is evidence from which dishonesty may be inferred, but it is not fraud by itself.

## Why it matters
- Authority for the **mental element** of fraudulent misrepresentation; the burden is on the claimant to prove dishonesty.
- Left a gap for careless misstatements: the idea that a careless statement without contract or fiduciary relationship attracts no liability was later displaced by *Hedley Byrne v Heller* (see that note).
- Fraud matters in practice because of remedies: damages in deceit (see *Smith New Court*), and the fact that exclusion clauses cannot protect against fraud (see *S Pearson v Dublin Corporation*).

## Applying it to a problem question
1. Identify whether the maker of the statement knew it was false, did not believe it, or did not care.
2. Honest but unreasonable belief is not fraud; look instead to s 2(1) Misrepresentation Act 1967 (statutory negligence) or *Hedley Byrne*.
3. Note the claimant must plead and prove dishonesty, not merely carelessness.
