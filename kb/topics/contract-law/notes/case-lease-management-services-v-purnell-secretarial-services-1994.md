# Lease Management Services Ltd v Purnell Secretarial Services Ltd [1994] CCLR 127 (case note)

> Case note written from a later Court of Appeal judgment that summarises the case, a case-database record and
> commentary; the judgment itself is not in the knowledge base. The court and the bench come from *Last Bus v
> Dawsongroup* [2023] EWCA Civ 1297, which cites the case as [1993] Tr LR 337; other sources give [1994] CCLR 127.
> The vLex record shows an earlier Court of Appeal hearing (Neill LJ, 7 December 1992) that appears to deal with costs
> applications, not the substantive appeal. The date of the substantive judgment is not confirmed; check the report.

## Overview
Court of Appeal (Sir Donald Nicholls V-C, with whom Hirst and Waite LJJ agreed, as recorded in *Last Bus*), on appeal
from Taunton County Court. A leading case on the **reasonableness of exclusion clauses under UCTA 1977 in lease and
hire finance**: a widely drawn clause excluding the finance company's liability, including for an express oral
warranty, was held unreasonable.

## Facts
Purnell, a small printing company, hired a Canon photocopier from Lease Management Services (a finance company) under
a five-year agreement dated February 1987. The machine was arranged through a representative of the dealer, Canon
(South West) Ltd, who dealt with Purnell's director. It could not do what Purnell needed (produce paper plates for
litho printing), so Purnell stopped paying and the finance company sued. Purnell had been given assurances by the
supplier's representative, who had apparent authority to act for the finance company. The finance company relied on
widely drawn exclusion clauses within s 3 UCTA. The county court judge held the clauses reasonable, largely because
the finance company was only a finance house, entered judgment for the finance company, and gave Purnell an indemnity
against Canon.

## Held
- The Court of Appeal reversed the judge: it was **not reasonable** for the finance company to exclude liability for
  an express oral warranty.
- The Vice-Chancellor rejected the argument that a clause which would be unreasonable in a supply contract becomes
  reasonable in a hire or finance agreement because the finance company did not inspect the goods or take part in
  the negotiations.
- The burden of proving that a clause excluding all liability for warranties is reasonable "will not be lightly
  discharged" (as quoted in *Last Bus*).
- In *Last Bus v Dawsongroup* [2023] EWCA Civ 1297 the Court of Appeal read *Purnell* as making such blanket
  exclusions prima facie unreasonable under UCTA, and treated that as the starting point.

## Why it matters
- Frequently cited with *St Albans v ICL* and later finance cases (*Sovereign Finance*, *Last Bus*) in challenges to
  exclusion clauses in finance and lease documentation.

## Applying it to a problem question
1. Identify whether UCTA applies (standard terms; s 3) and what the clause excludes.
2. Treat a total exclusion of liability as prima facie unreasonable, putting the burden on the party relying on it.
3. Check whether the finance structure (no inspection, no negotiation) is argued as a reason for reasonableness;
   *Purnell* rejects that argument.
4. Ask whether the dealer's assurances bind the finance company (for example through the dealer's apparent authority).
