# Jackson v Horizon Holidays Ltd [1975] 1 WLR 1468 (case note)

> Built from secondary summaries only; the judgments were not read. Check [1975] 1 WLR 1468 (also [1974] EWCA Civ 12)
> before citing. Sources do not agree on every detail (see Facts), so those details are left out.

## Overview
Court of Appeal (Civil Division), Lord Denning MR, James LJ and Orr LJ, judgment 5 February 1974 (reported 1975). A
husband booked a family holiday that turned out badly; the Court of Appeal upheld an award that included damages for
the distress of his wife and children, who were not parties to the contract. The decision is a leading consumer
exception on recovery for loss suffered by others, though it was later limited by the House of Lords.

## Facts
Mr Jackson booked a holiday for himself and his family through Horizon Holidays at a hotel in Ceylon (now Sri Lanka),
having told the company about the family's requirements. The accommodation and facilities were substantially worse
than promised; the family suffered distress, vexation, discomfort and inconvenience. The
trial judge, following *Jarvis v Swans Tours*, awarded £1,100. Horizon appealed the part of the award that reflected
the distress of Mr Jackson's wife and children, who were not parties to the contract. (The sources give differing
figures for the price paid, so the amount is not stated here.)

## Held
- The appeal was dismissed and the damages stood. Lord Denning MR treated the award as £600 for the defective
  performance and £500 for disappointment and mental distress, covering Mr Jackson and his family.
- He rejected the idea that the family were all parties to the contract, calling it a fiction. Instead, Mr Jackson in
  substance contracted for the benefit of the whole family, and so could recover damages for the loss they suffered.
  Horizon knew he was booking for the family.
- One source reports that James LJ and Orr LJ agreed without further comment. Another source, which could not be
  verified, suggests a difference of view on the point, so how the other two judges aligned is **not confirmed**.

## Later treatment
- In *Woodar Investment Development Ltd v Wimpey Construction UK Ltd* [1980] 1 WLR 277 the House of Lords disapproved parts of Lord Denning's reasoning and said *Jackson* could
  be supported only on its special facts, a narrow category of consumer-type contracts.
- The Contracts (Rights of Third Parties) Act 1999, s 1(1)(b), would now partly resolve the facts, by allowing a third
  party to enforce a term purporting to confer a benefit on them.

## Applying it to a problem question
1. Identify a contract made for the benefit of a group (a family holiday, a group booking) where the promisee has the
   group's enjoyment in view and the other side knows it.
2. State the general rule that a claimant recovers only its own loss, then argue *Jackson* as a narrow exception for
   distress or disappointment in such consumer contracts, noting its limited status after *Woodar*.
3. Consider the 1999 Act, which may give the family direct rights, and the *Jarvis v Swans Tours* head of damages for
   loss of enjoyment.
