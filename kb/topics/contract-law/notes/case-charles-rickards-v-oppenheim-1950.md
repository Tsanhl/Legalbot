# Charles Rickards Ltd v Oppenheim [1950] 1 KB 616 (case note)

> Case note written from consistent secondary accounts; the judgment itself is not in the knowledge base.
> Sources differ on dates and the exact notice period, so those details are omitted. Check the report ([1950] 1 KB 616; [1950] 1 All ER 420) before citing pinpoints.

## Overview
Court of Appeal. The case deals with **waiver of a time stipulation**: a party who has waived the original delivery date can later make time of the essence again by giving **reasonable notice**.

## Facts
Oppenheim ordered a motor car body to be built on a Rolls-Royce chassis, with delivery promised within about six to seven months. Delivery was not made in time, and Oppenheim kept pressing for it rather than treating the contract as at an end, which was treated as waiving the original date. After further delay he gave notice setting a fresh final date. The work was not completed by then and he refused to accept it. The company sued.

## Held
- The buyer's waiver was not permanent: having waived the original date, he was entitled to give **reasonable notice** requiring delivery by a new date, making time of the essence again.
- The seller is protected by the requirement that the notice be reasonable. Time under the notice runs from the notice, not from the original waived default.
- The notice given was reasonable, the seller missed the new date, and the buyer was entitled to refuse delivery; the seller's claim failed.

## Why it matters
- Standard authority for restoring time as of the essence after waiver or delay.
- Links with the law on conditions and termination: a reasonable-notice mechanism lets a buyer exit a contract where time was not originally of the essence or has been waived.

## Applying it to a problem question
1. Was time originally of the essence (by agreement or because the term is a condition)?
2. If the date was waived or the buyer tolerated delay, can a notice be served?
3. Judge reasonableness of the new period by the stage of the work and what remained to be done; if the notice period is missed, termination may follow.
