# Thomas Witter Ltd v TBP Industries Ltd [1996] 2 All ER 573 (case note)

> Case note written from consistent secondary accounts; the judgment itself is not in the knowledge base.
> Check pinpoints against the report before citing them. Sources describe the judgment as given on 15 July 1994 and reported in 1996.

## Overview
High Court (Chancery Division), Jacob J. The case concerns (a) whether an **entire agreement / non-reliance clause** can exclude liability for misrepresentation, tested under **s 3 Misrepresentation Act 1967**, and (b) whether **damages in lieu of rescission under s 2(2)** are available when rescission is barred.

## Facts
TBP sold a carpet company to Thomas Witter. During the negotiations TBP negligently misrepresented matters in the accounts and forecasts, including that a cost was one-off when it was in fact recurring. The sale agreement contained a clause stating that the buyer had not been induced by any representation other than those in the contract, and the buyer sued for misrepresentation and breach of warranty.

## Held
- TBP was liable for negligent misrepresentation.
- **Rescission** was barred, because the company had changed too much after completion for the parties to be restored to their original positions.
- The clause was ineffective: it did not clearly exclude liability for pre-contract misrepresentation, and in any case it was too wide, failing to distinguish between fraud, negligence and innocence, so it could not satisfy the reasonableness requirement of s 3 (as substituted by Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977 s 8). Sources differ on whether the s 3 point was part of the decision or obiter.
- Obiter, Jacob J considered that s 2(2) damages can still be awarded even where the right to rescind has since been lost, as long as the right once existed. He relied on a ministerial statement during the Act's passage; commentators have questioned this reliance.

## Why it matters
- Shows that a party wanting to exclude liability for misrepresentation must make that clear, and that the clause must pass s 3 reasonableness.
- Starting point for the debate on s 2(2) when rescission is barred.
- A clause purporting to exclude fraud cannot be reasonable.

## Applying it to a problem question
1. Does the clause, properly construed, cover pre-contract misrepresentations?
2. If it does, apply the s 3 / UCTA reasonableness test to the clause as a whole.
3. If rescission is barred (for example, by change in the subject matter), consider s 2(2) damages and the question whether they remain available.
