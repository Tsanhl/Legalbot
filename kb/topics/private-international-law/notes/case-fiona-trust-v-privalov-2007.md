# Fiona Trust & Holding Corp v Privalov [2007] UKHL 40 (case note)

> Built from secondary sources only; the speeches were not read. The House of Lords appeal was heard under the title
> *Premium Nafta Products Ltd v Fili Shipping Co Ltd* (the sub-proceedings), with *Fiona Trust* decided at the same
> time. The date is given only as October 2007 (one source says 17 October). Do not quote paragraph numbers.

## Overview
House of Lords, October 2007. Lord Hoffmann gave the leading speech. The case reshapes the English approach to
arbitration clauses in two ways: a presumption that commercial parties want a **one-stop** forum for all disputes
arising from their relationship (construction), and a strict approach to **separability** of the arbitration agreement
under section 7 of the Arbitration Act 1996.

## Facts
Eight Shelltime 4 charterparties were made between companies in the Sovcomflot group (the owners) and three
charterers. Each contained a clause sending disputes "arising under" the charter to the English courts, with
arbitration in London available. In 2006 the owners purported to rescind the charters, alleging that the charterers had
bribed senior officers of the owners to procure them. The owners sought a declaration in court that the charters were
validly rescinded. The charterers applied for a stay under section 9 of the Arbitration Act 1996 in favour of
arbitration.

## Procedure and outcome
The first-instance court refused the stay; the Court of Appeal (judgment of Longmore LJ, per one source) allowed the
charterers' appeal and granted it. The House of Lords **dismissed the owners' appeal**, so the dispute went to
arbitration.

## Principles
- **Construction:** the court should read an arbitration clause giving effect to the commercial purpose, starting
  from the assumption that rational businesspeople intend all disputes arising from their relationship to be decided by
  the same tribunal, unless the language makes clear that certain questions are excluded. The fine distinctions among
  "arising under", "arising out of" and similar wording were rejected as unhelpful. A dispute about whether the
  contract was validly formed or induced by bribery fell within the clause.
- **Separability (section 7):** the arbitration agreement is treated as distinct from the main contract. It can be
  challenged only on grounds that relate directly to the arbitration agreement itself. Allegations that the main contract
  was induced by bribery do not automatically taint the arbitration clause; the owners had to show that the arbitration
  agreement itself was procured by the bribery.

## Applying it to a problem question
1. Read the clause broadly and purposively; do not draw a distinction between wordings such as "arising under" and "arising
   out of".
2. Ask whether the clause excludes the kind of dispute (for example, validity or rescission of the main contract).
3. If a party says the main contract is void or voidable (bribery, misrepresentation, lack of authority), check
   whether the ground attacks the arbitration agreement itself; if not, the tribunal decides.
4. Link to a stay application under section 9 and the anti-suit injunction notes if the other party sues elsewhere.
