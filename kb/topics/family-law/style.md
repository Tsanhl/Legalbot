# Family Law: answer rules

## Learned from testing
- In financial remedies problem questions, analyse needs, compensation and sharing as separate heads (*Miller*; *McFarlane*) and say which head drives the outcome. Compensation is rarely awarded: test it only where one party gave up a career or earning capacity, and say whether a relationship-generated loss is shown on the facts. [2026-11-22 · ref 2026-11-22-0840/q17]
- Where the facts suggest hidden or understated assets, state the duty of full and frank disclosure and what follows from breach (adverse inferences; setting aside an order). [2026-11-22 · ref 2026-11-22-0840/q17]
- In financial remedies problems, start with an asset schedule listing each asset, its net value, whose name it is in and whether it is matrimonial or non-matrimonial, and total the net pot. The sharing principle applies only to matrimonial property; non-matrimonial wealth is relevant chiefly where needs cannot otherwise be met (*Standish v Standish* [2025] UKSC 26). [2026-11-22, 2026-11-24 · ref 2026-11-22-0840/q17; 2026-11-22-1301/q17,q18]
- Apply the s 25 MCA 1973 factors to the facts, giving first consideration to the welfare of any child under 18, and test each party's housing and income needs against the available resources. [2026-11-22 · ref 2026-11-22-0840/q17]
- Set out the proposed overall division in figures and percentages with the working, and name the MCA 1973 power for each order recommended: s 23 for periodical payments or a lump sum, s 24 for property adjustment, s 24A for an order for sale, and s 24B for pension sharing. [2026-11-24 · ref 2026-11-22-1301/q17,q18]
- In separation questions, first establish whether the parties are married, civil partners or cohabitants. Cohabitants have no MCA 1973 claims: disputes over the home turn on trust law and TOLATA 1996, and provision for a child on the Child Support Act 1991 and Sch 1 Children Act 1989. [2026-11-24 · ref 2026-11-22-1301/q17,q18]
- For separation questions involving a jointly owned home or a child, cover each party's share in the home, the right to stay in it or resist a sale, child maintenance, mediation, and protective steps such as severing a joint tenancy and making or updating a will. State plainly any of these the materials do not cover. [2026-11-24 · ref 2026-11-22-1301/q17,q18]
- When advising on protective steps, explain that a joint tenancy passes the whole home to the survivor unless it is severed (e.g. by written notice to the other owner), and that separation alone does not revoke a will or, for spouses, end intestacy rights. [2026-11-24 · ref 2026-11-22-1301/q17,q18]
- In big-money financial remedy problems, test the strongest grounds for departing from equal sharing before concluding, and say whether each succeeds on the facts: special contribution (exceptional and rarely made out: *Charman v Charman (No 4)* [2007] EWCA Civ 503); whether illiquidity or risk in business assets should affect valuation, the split or the form of order; and whether pre-marital cohabitation that moved seamlessly into marriage brings earlier accrual into the matrimonial pot. [2026-11-24 · ref 2026-11-24-1510/q17]
- When giving next steps in a financial remedy problem, set out the procedural route: MIAM, Form A application under FPR 2010 Part 9, simultaneous exchange of Form E under the duty of full and frank disclosure, First Directions Appointment, FDR appointment, then final hearing. Add the costs position: the general rule is no order as to costs (FPR r 28.3), subject to orders for litigation conduct. State plainly any step the materials do not cover. [2026-11-24 · ref 2026-11-24-1510/q17]
