# Intestacy: Administration of Estates Act 1925 s 46, the statutory legacy and cohabitants

## Overview
Where a person dies without a valid will covering all their property, the Administration of Estates Act 1925 (AEA) s 46 sets a fixed order of entitlement. A surviving spouse or civil partner takes the personal chattels, a fixed net sum (£322,000 for deaths on or after 26 July 2023, the "statutory legacy") and half of the remainder if there are children, or everything if there are no children. Unmarried partners have no rights on intestacy and must rely on the Inheritance (Provision for Family and Dependants) Act 1975.

## The law

### When intestacy arises
Total intestacy (no valid will), or partial intestacy (a will that does not dispose of all property, for example a failed residuary gift). The residuary estate (after debts, funeral and testamentary expenses and any legacies) passes under s 46 (AEA 1925, Part IV). The property is held by the personal representatives on trust for sale/with power to sell (s 33).

### Order of entitlement (s 46(1))
1. **Spouse or civil partner (s 46(1)(i))**, who must survive the intestate by 28 days (s 46(2A)):
   - If there are **no issue**: the spouse or civil partner takes the whole estate (since the Inheritance and Trustees' Powers Act 2014, in force 1 October 2014; before then, parents, siblings might take shares).
   - If there are **issue** (children, grandchildren etc.): (A) the personal chattels absolutely; (B) a fixed net sum, free of death duties and costs, with simple interest from the date of death at the Bank of England rate (s 46(1A)); (C) half the residue absolutely; the other half goes to the issue on the statutory trusts.
   - Personal chattels (AEA s 55(1)(x)) are tangible movable property, other than money, securities and property used for business or as an investment. Cars and household items count; money does not.
   - The fixed net sum is set by order under Schedule 1A: £322,000 for deaths on or after 26 July 2023 (Administration of Estates Act 1925 (Fixed Net Sum) Order 2023, SI 2023/758); £270,000 for deaths from 6 February 2020 to 25 July 2023. Sch 1A requires a new order if CPI rises by more than 15%, and a review at least every five years.
   - Spouses and civil partners must be married or in a civil partnership at death; a final divorce or dissolution order (decree absolute) ends entitlement, a conditional order (decree nisi) does not. A judicially separated spouse is treated as having predeceased (Matrimonial Causes Act 1973 s 18(2)).
2. **Issue, if no spouse (s 46(1)(ii))**: statutory trusts for issue (s 47): equal shares among the children who survive, with the issue of a deceased child taking their parent's share per stirpes. A child takes a vested interest at 18 or on earlier marriage or civil partnership (s 47(1)(i); marriage under 18 is no longer possible in England and Wales since 27 February 2023); a minor's interest is held on trust. The old hotchpot rule (lifetime advancements brought into account, s 47(1)(iii)) was repealed for deaths on or after 1 October 2014. Adopted children count as children of their adoptive parents, and the parents' marital status is irrelevant (Family Law Reform Act 1987).
3. **Parents (s 46(1)(iii) and (iv))**: both in equal shares, or the survivor absolutely.
4. **Siblings of the whole blood (s 46(1)(v))**, on statutory trusts; then siblings of the half blood; then grandparents; then aunts and uncles of the whole blood (on statutory trusts for their issue); then of the half blood.
5. **The Crown, Duchy of Lancaster or Duke of Cornwall (bona vacantia, s 46(1)(vi))**: the Crown may make provision for dependants and others (discretionary grants by the Treasury Solicitor/Government Legal Department).
Each class takes only if the class before it is empty. Each person's entitlement requires survival of the intestate (s 46(2A) specifies 28 days for spouses only; where the order of deaths is uncertain, Law of Property Act 1925 s 184 presumes the elder died first).

### Who administers
Letters of administration are granted to those entitled under the Non-Contentious Probate Rules 1987 r 22: the spouse or civil partner first, then children, parents, siblings, and so on. There must be a grant before the estate can be collected. No more than four administrators may be appointed, and where a minority or life interest arises there must be at least two (or a trust corporation) (Senior Courts Act 1981 s 114). The court may require a guarantee from administrators (s 120) but in practice rarely does.

### Cohabitants
- A cohabiting partner has no entitlement under s 46, however long the relationship.
- Cohabitants can claim under the Inheritance (Provision for Family and Dependants) Act 1975 s 1(1)(ba) and s 1(1A)-(1B) if they lived in the same household as if a spouse or civil partner for the whole of the two years immediately before death (see the 1975 Act note). The standard of provision for a cohabitant is maintenance, not the surviving-spouse standard.
- Claimants can also look to proprietary estoppel, constructive trust or a claim to beneficial interest in a joint home (property rules), and to survivorship if property was jointly owned.
- Cohabitants should make wills; the intestacy rules do not recognise them.

### Partial intestacy and related points
- Where a will does not dispose of the whole estate, s 49 AEA applies s 46 to the undisposed-of property, subject to the will. For deaths on or after 1 October 2014 a spouse no longer has to bring gifts under the will into account against the fixed net sum (the partial-intestacy hotchpot was repealed by the 2014 Act).
- Disclaimer and variation: the beneficiaries may vary an intestacy within two years by deed of variation (IHTA 1984 s 142, TCGA 1992 s 62(6)) and treat it as made by the deceased for tax purposes.
- The family home passes as part of the estate: if jointly owned as beneficial joint tenants it passes by survivorship outside the estate.
- A surviving spouse may elect within 12 months of the grant to have the matrimonial home appropriated towards their entitlement (Intestates' Estates Act 1952 Sch 2), so PRs should not sell it in that period without consent.

## Key authorities
- Statute is the primary source: AEA 1925 ss 33, 46, 47, 49, 55 and Sch 1A; Inheritance and Trustees' Powers Act 2014 (amendments to ss 46, 47, 49 and repeal of s 47A).
- On the 28-day survival: the statutory text s 46(2A).
- The leading modern cases on the distribution scheme are few; most questions turn on applying the statute.

## Applying it
1. Was there a valid will, and does it dispose of all property? If not, intestacy or partial intestacy applies to the undisposed property.
2. Identify the surviving relatives in the order of s 46; check marriage or civil partnership, survival of 28 days (spouse), and any divorce.
3. Spouse and issue: chattels, fixed net sum (check the date of death for the sum and the interest rate), half of the residue; the issue take the other half.
4. Spouse and no issue: all.
5. No spouse: issue, then parents, siblings, and so on; check whole and half blood.
6. Check for jointly owned property and nominated assets (life policies in trust, pension death benefits) that fall outside the estate.
7. Consider claims under the 1975 Act (cohabitants, children, dependants).
8. Common traps: assuming parents take a share when there is a spouse and no issue (not since 2014); using the old fixed sum; treating cohabitants as entitled; confusing the statutory legacy with a share of the estate; forgetting that stepchildren are not issue (but are eligible for a 1975 Act claim if treated as children of the family); forgetting the 28-day survival rule.
9. Everyday question: "My partner (unmarried) died without a will. Do I inherit?" Not automatically; you may be able to claim under the 1975 Act and may take any jointly owned assets by survivorship.

## Debates and reform
- The Law Commission, *Intestacy and Family Provision Claims on Death* (Law Com No 331, 2011), recommended extending intestacy rights to cohabitants who have lived together for five years (or two years if they have a child), and index-linking the statutory legacy; the Inheritance and Trustees' Powers Act 2014 enacted the spouse's entitlement changes and index-linking but not the cohabitant rights.
- The Law Commission's report on cohabitation (Law Com No 307, 2007) also fed into the debate; no legislation giving cohabitants intestacy rights had been enacted by November 2026.
- The statutory legacy is reviewed periodically (inflation), and the fixed net sum can be increased by order.
- Debate over whether the entitlement of the spouse should be higher where the couple have children (some argue that the half share to children harms the surviving parent).

## Recent changes and points to check
- 26 July 2023: fixed net sum £322,000 (no later order by November 2026).
- 1 October 2014: spouse takes all where no issue; half of residue absolutely (no life interest); hotchpot rules repealed.
- Interest on the fixed net sum is simple interest at the Bank of England base rate at the end of the day of death (s 46(1A)).
- Cohabitant intestacy rights have not been enacted.
