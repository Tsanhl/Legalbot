# Law and Medicine: answer rules
- Keep to the treatment-decision issues the question raises; unless asked, do not stray into mental health law, DoLS,
  public health law, or negligence/Montgomery-based claims.
- Problem order for treatment decisions: capacity (MCA 2005 ss 1–3; *A Local Authority v JB*) → advance decision
  (exists s 24, voluntariness *Re T*; valid s 25(2), esp. s 25(2)(c) *Re PW*; applicable s 25(4)–(6), life-sustaining
  treatment formalities s 25(5)–(6)) → effect s 26 → s 5 general defence and best interests s 4 (*Aintree v James*;
  *An NHS Trust v Y*) → seek a declaration. Avoid fence-sitting on whether a recent refusal applies.
- Children: Gillick competence; parental responsibility (Children Act 1989 ss 2, 4); necessity only in emergencies;
  welfare override (*Re R*, *Re W*); FLRA 1969 s 8 for 16–17s.
- Transplants: HTA 2004 s 1 appropriate consent and the s 3(6) hierarchy (deceased, nominated representative, deemed
  consent, qualifying relative); living child donors (ss 32–33, Code F); NHSBT 2023 allocation policy; consent need not
  be unconditional under the Act, but conditional deceased donations are rejected by policy.
- Essays: justify every legal point (say what the section does), define key terms early, answer the question set with
  2–3 examples, use statistics and academic views, and address counter-arguments fully (a common gap even in strong answers).

## First-class checklist (from the marking guide)

- In problem questions, use the Mental Capacity Act 2005: ss 1–3 (presumption, functional test, unwise decisions), s 4 (best interests, including past wishes), and ss 24–26 (advance decisions; s 25(5)–(6) for life-sustaining treatment). Add *Re T*, *Ms B*, *Re MB* and *Bland*, Family Law Reform Act 1969 s 8 and *Gillick* for minors, and the Human Tissue Act 2004 and HTA Codes for donation. Commit on borderline points (for example whether a refusal is contemporaneous or an advance decision) and name the exact statutory condition. In essays, take a qualified stance backed by scholarship and data, and deal properly with the opposing arguments.
