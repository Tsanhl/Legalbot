# Abortion 2 - The Abortion Act 1967 - Grounds, Certification, Good Faith, Sex Selection and Conscientious Objection

## Overview
The Abortion Act 1967 (as amended by the HFE Act 1990) does not create a right to abortion. It provides a **defence** to the crimes in the OAPA 1861 and ILPA 1929 where a registered medical practitioner terminates a pregnancy and **two registered medical practitioners** have formed, **in good faith**, the opinion that one of four grounds applies. Almost all abortions (98% in 2023) are under the "social ground" in s.1(1)(a). The structure gives doctors wide discretion, which Sheldon argues the courts have protected, and which explains the debate about sex-selective abortion (Greasley) and medicalisation (Halliday). Fetal abnormality (s.1(1)(d)) has its own note.

## The law
1. **s.1(1)**: a person is not guilty of an offence under the law relating to abortion when a pregnancy is terminated by a registered medical practitioner if two registered medical practitioners are of the opinion, formed in good faith:
   - **(a)** that the pregnancy has not exceeded its **24th week** and continuance would involve risk, greater than if terminated, of injury to the physical or mental health of the pregnant woman or any existing children of her family [statistics Grounds C (woman) and D (existing children)];
   - **(b)** termination is necessary to prevent **grave permanent injury** to the physical or mental health of the pregnant woman [Ground B];
   - **(c)** continuance would involve **risk to the life** of the pregnant woman greater than if terminated [Ground A];
   - **(d)** there is a substantial risk that if the child were born it would suffer from such physical or mental abnormalities as to be seriously handicapped [Ground E].
   Only (a) has a time limit; (b)-(d) apply up to birth.
2. **s.1(2)**: for grounds (a) and (b), account may be taken of the woman's actual or reasonably foreseeable environment.
3. **s.1(3)**: treatment must be carried out in an NHS hospital or a place approved by the Secretary of State (s.1(3A)-(3D) concern approvals, including classes of place for medical abortion).
4. **s.1(4)**: the two-doctor requirement (and place requirement) does not apply where one doctor forms the good faith opinion that termination is **immediately necessary** to save the woman's life or prevent grave permanent injury (grounds (b)/(c)). In 2023, 5 of the 170 (b)/(c) abortions relied on this.
5. **Good faith.** *R v Smith* [1974] 1 All ER 376 (conviction under s.58): "The question of good faith is essentially one for the jury to determine on the totality of the evidence."
6. **24 weeks** is calculated from the first day of the woman's last period, though conception usually occurs about 2 weeks later: *R (BPAS) v Secretary of State for Health and Social Care* [2020] EWCA Civ 355.
7. **Statistics (2023)**: s.1(1)(a) – 98% of abortions, of which 0.4% relate to risk to existing children. (b) and (c) together – about 0.06% (170). (d) – 3,075 (1%).
8. **The social ground in practice.** The evidence suggests continuing a pregnancy always involves greater health risk than abortion, at least in the first trimester, so ground (a) is in effect available on request in early pregnancy if doctors so certify.
9. **Two signatures.** RCOG (2007 evidence to the Science and Technology Committee): the two-signature requirement seems to have been included to mark the seriousness of the decision; given 98% of abortions are under grounds C/D, it is "anachronistic" for most cases and causes delay, increasing clinical risk; most women referred are certain. A Private Member's Bill to reduce the requirement to one doctor failed: Abortion Act 1967 (Amendment) Bill [HL] 2014-15.
10. **Abortion on grounds of fetal sex?** s.1(1)(a) does not mention fetal sex.
    - CPS statement (Sept 2013): prosecution of doctors who allegedly agreed to sex-selective abortions not in the public interest; a "highly relevant factor" was the GMC's powers; and the law gives doctors "a wide discretion" to decide when a risk to health exists.
    - DH *Guidance in Relation to Requirements of the Abortion Act 1967* (May 2014): the threshold of risk to health is a matter for each doctor's clinical opinion (para 13); "Abortion on the grounds of gender alone is illegal" (para 25).
    - Serious Crime Act 2015 required publication of an assessment; DH (August 2015): "no substantiated concerns of gender abortions occurring in England, Wales and Scotland".
    - Attempt to explicitly prohibit: Abortion (Sex-Selection) Bill 2014-15 (failed).
    - Contrast the HFE Act, which prohibits embryo sex selection for non-medical reasons (PGT note).
11. **Conscientious objection (s.4).** No person is under any duty to participate in treatment authorised by the Act to which they have a conscientious objection; the burden of proof lies on the objector (s.4(1)); this does not affect any duty to participate in treatment necessary to save the woman's life or prevent grave permanent injury (s.4(2)). Brazier & Cave: *Janaway v Salford AHA* [1988] (HL) – "participate" means actually taking part in treatment, not typing referral letters; *Greater Glasgow and Clyde Health Board v Doogan* [2014] UKSC 68 – narrow, "hands-on" reading excluding administrative/managerial tasks, though employers may need to make reasonable adjustments under human rights or equality law.
12. **Fathers**: no right to be consulted or to prevent a lawful abortion (*Paton v BPAS* [1979] QB 276).

## Debate and critique
- **No right, only a medical defence.** Halliday (2020) argues the Act entrenches medical dominance: women are "supplicants" and doctors gatekeepers; despite strict-sounding rhetoric, early abortion is effectively at medical discretion and generously funded. RCOG's critique of two signatures supports reform; decriminalisation (cl.191 Crime and Policing Bill) would not remove the s.1 structure for doctors.
- **Sheldon, 'Subject Only to the Attitude of the Surgeon Concerned: The Judicial Protection of Medical Discretion' (1996) 5 Social & Legal Studies 95.** In abortion cases, judicial reluctance to interfere with medical discretion (the same deference seen elsewhere as paternalism) has often worked *to women's advantage*, protecting a space for doctors to agree to abortion (e.g. Smith; Paton). She ends by noting problems with the medicalisation of abortion.
- **Greasley, 'Is Sex-Selective Abortion against the Law?' (2016) 36(3) OJLS 535.** Abortions where knowledge of fetal sex is a "but-for" cause can be lawful if one of the health grounds is attested in good faith by two doctors. Official bodies misstated the law (cf. DH 2014 "illegal") by failing to distinguish the legal *grounds* from factual *explanations* for abortion. She also examines the claim that such abortions should be prohibited because of doubts about the woman's free consent (coercion), noting this consent-based objection is hard to square with general abortion permissions.
- **Why should sex selection be treated differently from other reasons?** Arguments: sex selection expresses and entrenches gender stereotyping and son preference (cf. the 2006 White Paper reasons on embryo sex selection); counter: restricting it invites scrutiny of women's reasons generally and may drive abortions underground.
- **Conscientious objection** as part of the 1967 compromise v women's access (Brazier & Cave note areas where NHS abortion is hard to obtain).
- **Moral theory link**: the 24-week limit for (a) but none for (d) suggests a gradualist (limited/proportional) view of fetal status, but with an exception for disability that is criticised (fetal abnormality note). The HFE Act's stages (pre-implantation; to 24 weeks; after 24 weeks; birth) reinforce the proportional reading.

## Applying it to a problem question
1. **Who terminated?** Must be a registered medical practitioner (otherwise OAPA/ILPA liability).
2. **Two RMPs' opinion in good faith** (Smith): were two opinions formed honestly, not as a rubber stamp? Exception: s.1(4) emergency (one doctor; grounds (b)/(c) only).
3. **Which ground?**
   - Before 24 weeks (counted from LMP: BPAS) → (a); consider s.1(2) environment; risks to existing children.
   - Any gestation → (b) grave permanent injury; (c) risk to life; (d) serious handicap.
4. **Place** (s.1(3)): NHS hospital or approved place (approved classes for medical abortion).
5. **Sex as the reason**: no explicit ground; lawful only if a s.1 ground is genuinely certified in good faith (Greasley v DH 2014). Advise on CPS/GMC consequences.
6. **Staff refusing to participate**: s.4; burden on objector; not for emergency life-saving treatment; scope "hands-on" (Doogan).
7. **Partner's objections**: irrelevant in law (Paton).
8. **Minors**: capacity rules (Gillick; FLRA s.8) govern consent to the procedure.
9. Traps: applying the 24-week limit to (b)-(d); describing the woman as having a legal right; ignoring good faith.
