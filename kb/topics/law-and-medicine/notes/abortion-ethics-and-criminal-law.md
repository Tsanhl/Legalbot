# Abortion 1 - Moral Status, Practice and the Underlying Criminal Law (OAPA 1861, ILPA 1929, Decriminalisation)

## Overview
Abortion is lawful and common in Britain (RCOG and others estimate 1 in 3 women in the UK will have one), yet it remains structurally a **crime** subject to defences. The Offences Against the Person Act 1861 (OAPA) ss.58-59 criminalise unlawfully procuring a miscarriage; the Infant Life (Preservation) Act 1929 (ILPA) criminalises "child destruction"; the Abortion Act 1967 (AA) provides defences to both. The Crime and Policing Bill (cl.191) would remove women from the criminal law relating to their own pregnancies, while keeping offences for third parties including doctors. The ethical debate turns on the **moral status** of the fetus and, for Thomson, whether status even settles the question.
## The law
1. **Moral status positions** (duties owed directly to the fetus by virtue of its characteristics):
   - *Full status* – owed the same protection as born persons;
   - *Limited status* – a lower level of protection (fixed or proportional);
   - *No status* – no direct protection.
2. **Practice** (DHSC statistics; 2023 figures published 15 January 2026):
   - 277,970 abortions for residents of England and Wales (rising from 23,641 in 1968); 98% NHS-funded (81% in the independent sector under NHS contract);
   - gestation: 89% at 2-9 weeks; 4% 10-12; 5% 13-19; 1% 20+;
   - methods: *medical* (up to 10 weeks; mifepristone and misoprostol; 87%); *surgical vacuum aspiration* (up to 14 weeks; 8%); *dilatation and evacuation* (after 14 weeks, general anaesthetic; 4%). RCOG (2010): feticide should precede medical abortion after 21 weeks 6 days to ensure no live birth.
3. **OAPA 1861 s.58**: a pregnant woman who, intending to procure her own miscarriage, unlawfully administers to herself any poison or noxious thing or unlawfully uses any instrument or other means; and anyone who, intending to procure the miscarriage of any woman (whether or not pregnant), unlawfully administers poison etc. or uses instruments – felony, maximum life. **s.59**: unlawfully supplying or procuring poison or instruments knowing they are intended to be unlawfully used to procure miscarriage.
   - AA s.5(2): anything done with intent to procure miscarriage is "unlawfully done" unless authorised by s.1.
   - *R (Smeaton) v Secretary of State for Health* [2002] EWHC 610: "miscarriage" means termination of an **established pregnancy** (after implantation); so the "morning after" pill is not covered. Interpreting it otherwise would make about 34% of women aged 16-49 potential criminals. Criticised from a full status view by John Keown, "'Morning After' Pills, 'Miscarriage' and Muddle" (2005) 25(2) LS 296.
   - **Men procuring miscarriage without consent**: *R v Magira* [2008] EWCA Crim 1939 (sentence 3 years 9 months); Dr Edward Erin (convicted Oct 2009; 6 years).
   - **Women procuring their own miscarriage**: *Sarah Catt* – pleaded guilty to administering misoprostol to herself with intent to procure miscarriage near term; Cooke J (sentencing remarks, 17 Sept 2012) imposed 8 years; reduced on appeal to 3.5 years (*R v Catt* [2013] EWCA Crim 1187). *Carla Foster* – *R v Foster* [2023] EWCA Crim 1196: sentence reduced to 14 months suspended given "a constellation of exceptionally strong mitigating factors".
4. **ILPA 1929 s.1(1)**: anyone who, intending to destroy the life of a child capable of being born alive, by a wilful act causes it to die before it has an existence independent of its mother commits **child destruction** (max life), unless the act was done in good faith only to preserve the mother's life. Passed to close a loophole: the 1861 Act did not appear to cover a fetus during childbirth, which was not yet a legal person whose killing was murder.
   - s.1(2): pregnancy of 28 weeks or more is prima facie proof of a child capable of being born alive (an evidential presumption).
   - *Rance v Mid-Downs HA* [1991] 1 QB 587: capable of being born alive if it could breathe "through its lungs alone".
   - AA s.5(1): no ILPA offence by a doctor terminating in accordance with the AA.
5. **Crime and Policing Bill 2025, cl.191** ("Removal of women from the criminal law related to abortion"; HL Bill 111 as brought from the Commons): for the purposes of the law related to abortion, including OAPA ss.58-59 and the ILPA, "no offence is committed by a woman acting in relation to her own pregnancy". If enacted and in force, it would prevent prosecutions like Catt and Foster, but third parties including doctors would remain liable. the materials its status and content may change.
6. **Legal status of the fetus**: no legal personality until birth. *Paton v BPAS* [1979] QB 276: the fetus has no right of its own at least until born with a separate existence; a husband had no right to stop his wife's lawful abortion (Brazier & Cave). Under Art 2 ECHR, scope is left to the state's **margin of appreciation** (*Paton v UK*; *H v Norway*).

## Key authorities
- OAPA 1861 ss.58, 59; ILPA 1929 s.1; AA 1967 ss.1, 5.
- *R (Smeaton) v SoS for Health* [2002] EWHC 610: miscarriage = termination of established pregnancy.
- *R v Magira* [2008] EWCA Crim 1939; Dr Erin (2009): non-consensual procurement by men.
- *R v Catt* (2012 sentencing; [2013] EWCA Crim 1187); *R v Foster* [2023] EWCA Crim 1196.
- *Rance v Mid-Downs HA* [1991] 1 QB 587.
- *Paton v BPAS* [1979] QB 276; *Paton v UK*; *H v Norway*.
- Crime and Policing Bill 2025 cl.191.

## Debate and critique
- **Full status (duty-based).** John Finnis, 'The Rights and Wrongs of Abortion: A Reply to Judith Thomson' (1973) 2(2) Phil & Pub Aff 117: it is an absolute wrong intentionally to kill an innocent human being from conception; he operates with the principle of double effect (see Beauchamp & Childress). John Keown takes a similar sanctity of life position.
- **Utilitarian (fixed limited status).** Peter Singer, *Practical Ethics* (2nd edn, 1993): the early fetus has no status; once it can feel pain its interests count in the utility balance. Compare RCOG Fetal Awareness Evidence Review (2022): pain perception before 28 weeks is unlikely.
- **Rights-based (no status).** Mary Anne Warren, 'On the Moral and Legal Status of Abortion' (1973) 57 Monist 43. Other rights theorists (Pattinson, *Medical Law and Ethics*, 7th edn 2025, ch 17) give the fetus proportional status.
- **Thomson's violinist.** Judith Jarvis Thomson, 'A Defence of Abortion' (1971) 1 Phil & Pub Aff 47: even if the fetus has full status, abortion can be permissible – if you woke to find a famous violinist plugged into you to keep him alive, unplugging yourself would not wrong him. She suggests the right to life is best understood as a right not to be killed *unjustly*, so showing the fetus is a person is not enough: one must also show that killing it violates its right to life. Finnis's reply targets this argument.
- **Halliday (2020), 'Maintaining the criminal prohibition of abortion as a means of protecting women'** (in Sinn et al (eds), *Populismus und alternative Fakten*): the AA is not a "great gift of choice" to women; it entrenches medical dominance, making women supplicants who may only request an abortion while doctors keep a gate-keeping role; rhetoric in England, Germany and the USA does not match regulatory reality.
- **Halliday et al, 'The mis-use of fetal viability as the determinant of non-criminal abortion'** (comparing England and Wales with the Netherlands): viability as a threshold makes the law uncertain, sustains continued criminalisation and frames abortion as a medical matter rather than a reproductive choice; they conclude viability is misconceived in its application.
- **Decriminalisation debate.** Supporters: prosecutions of women (Catt, Foster) are cruel and deterrent; abortion is healthcare. Opponents: concern about late self-induced abortions. Cl.191 is a partial step: it does not create a right to abortion or remove the AA's medical gatekeeping. Sheldon & Wellings (eds), *Decriminalising Abortion in the UK* (2020) is listed reading.

## Applying it to a problem question
1. Identify who acted (woman, partner, doctor, supplier) and what was done (drugs, instruments, timing).
2. **Is there a pregnancy established** (Smeaton)? Before implantation, no "miscarriage".
3. **OAPA s.58/59**: intent to procure miscarriage; was it "unlawful", i.e. outside s.1 AA (two doctors, good faith, grounds, approved place)?
4. **ILPA**: is the fetus capable of being born alive (28-week presumption; Rance)? Was the act in good faith to preserve the mother's life? Doctors acting under the AA are protected (s.5(1)).
5. **Woman's own liability**: currently liable under s.58 (Catt, Foster); if cl.191 is enacted, not liable; third parties still liable.
6. **Partner's rights**: none to prevent a lawful abortion (Paton).
7. Traps: treating abortion as a legal right; forgetting ILPA for late terminations outside the AA; citing the Bill as law.
