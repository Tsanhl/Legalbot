# Blackwell v Blackwell [1929] AC 318 (case note)

> Built from secondary sources only; the speeches were not read. The date within 1929 and the full bench are not
> confirmed and are omitted; the speeches usually cited are those of Viscount Sumner and Lord Buckmaster. Check the
> report before relying on any detail. Do not quote wording or paragraph numbers from this note.

## Overview
House of Lords, 1929 ([1929] UKHL 1). The case is a leading authority on **secret and half-secret trusts**: the House of Lords
upheld a half-secret trust, with evidence of the trust's terms admitted outside the will, and the case is cited for
when communication to the trustees must take place.

## Facts
A testator left £12,000 by codicil to five trustees, to be invested and the income applied "for the purposes indicated
by me to them". At or around the time the codicil was executed, four of the trustees were told the general objects and the fifth was
given detailed instructions; all five accepted. The fifth trustee wrote the instructions down in a memorandum a few
hours after the codicil was executed. The intended beneficiaries were a woman and her son. The testator's widow and son
(the residuary legatees) challenged the trust, arguing that evidence of the purposes should not be admitted and the
trust failed.

## Decision
- The House of Lords **upheld the trust** and allowed evidence of the terms that had been communicated to the
  trustees. Eve J and the Court of Appeal had also upheld it.
- The trustees were to hold on the terms communicated, not for the residuary beneficiaries.
- Viscount Sumner treated enforcement of such a trust as a normal exercise of equity's general jurisdiction, not
  inconsistent with the Wills Act 1837, and identified intention, communication and acceptance (acquiescence) as the
  essential elements.
- He also said that a testator cannot reserve a power to make later unwitnessed dispositions simply by naming a
  trustee and leaving the purposes to be supplied afterwards.
- Lord Buckmaster stressed that equity will not allow a trustee who has accepted the testator's confidence to defeat
  the intended beneficiaries, whether the trust is fully secret or half-secret.

## Communication
Secondary sources say that for a half-secret trust the terms must be communicated to the trustees before or at the time
the will (here, codicil) is made. Commentators also point out that the ratio may not strictly require communication
before execution, because in this case the communication was close in time to the codicil. Treat the "before or at
the time" rule as the orthodox reading, which *Re Keen* applied, and not as uncontested.

## Applying it to a problem question
1. Identify a half-secret trust: the will states that property is held on trust but does not set out the terms.
2. Check communication: when were the terms communicated and accepted? Under the orthodox rule, communication must
   be before or at the time the will is executed.
3. Apply *Blackwell* to admit outside evidence of the terms and to uphold the trust if the rules are met.
4. Mention the contrary reading of the ratio and the strictness shown in *Re Keen* if the timing is late or the will
   allows later communication.
