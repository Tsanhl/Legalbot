# Trusts Law: answer rules
- Purpose trusts of imperfect obligation: perpetuity period is **a life in being plus 21 years**; always test
  **capriciousness**; be careful distinguishing purpose and beneficiary trusts (a trust for people "as T sees fit"
  is a beneficiary trust, not charitable).
- Uncertainty of objects where you cannot say who is even in the class = straightforward failure.
- Spot a **trust of successive interests** when the gift is "to A for life, then B".
- Gifts to recipients "to apply for X": risk that recipients are under only a moral obligation.
- Apply the cases, e.g. *Boyce v Boyce* (certainty of subject matter); an answer that does not apply case law will
  not improve.
- Secret trusts: consider them where property passes on an agreement to transfer it on (an often missed claim).
- Tracing: the allocation method decides who claims what; *Roscoe v Winder* applies only where funds were withdrawn and
  later replenished; consider the rolling charge method; apportion the balance of assets, not the loss.
- Do not mix proprietary defences with the personal claim for knowing receipt; change of position is a common law
  defence. Consider **subrogation** and the resulting charge, and the recipient's knowing-receipt liability.
- Breach of trust: address causation generally; liability as between co-trustees (contribution, s 61 TA 1925,
  limitation); a trustee's basic liability is substitutive compensation for misappropriated property. Distinguish
  fiduciary and non-fiduciary duties.
- Advise the right person: where the question is about liability that burdens an estate, advise from the estate's side.
