# Pension trustees' investment powers and duties

## Overview
Investment is the trustees' most financially significant function. UK schemes hold more than £2.2 trillion of assets. A DB shortfall becomes a cost to the employer, and a DC shortfall reduces members' pots. The rules come from four layers: (1) **IORP II** Arts 7 and 19 (activities limited to retirement benefits; the "prudent person" rule), transposed by PA 2004 s255 and PA 1995 ss33-36A with the **Occupational Pension Schemes (Investment) Regulations 2005**; (2) the **trust deed's** investment powers, widened by PA 1995 s34; (3) general trust law, especially *Cowan v Scargill*; and (4) **FSMA 2000** limits on trustees making day-to-day investment decisions. Most of the Trustee Act 2000 does **not** apply (s36: "the Trustee Act 2000 can be viewed as setting traps for its readers"). Liability for investment negligence **cannot be excluded** (PA 1995 s33).

## The law

### 1. Scope and purpose limits
- **IORP II Art 7 / PA 2004 s255**: trustees must limit the scheme's activities to **retirement-benefit activities** (and ancillary benefits such as death, disability or sickness). The discussion used this against a proposal to run a mobility-scooter business directly from scheme assets. The discussion notes the Law Commission did not consider this limit, and ask whether taking account of non-financial factors is consistent with it.
- **PA 1995 s40 / Investment Regs reg 12**: restrictions on **employer-related investment** (in general no more than 5%). Outside scope, but be aware of it.

### 2. Power to invest and delegate (PA 1995 s34)
1. Subject to s36(1) and to any restriction in the scheme, trustees have the same power to invest **as if absolutely entitled** to the assets.
2. Investment discretions may be delegated only (a) to a **fund manager** who can lawfully take the decisions under FSMA s19, to be exercised in line with s36, or (b) under Trustee Act 1925 s25 (up to 12 months), or (c) as in s34(5).
3. **s34(4)**: trustees are **not responsible** for the acts or defaults of a fund manager to whom they delegated under s34(2)(a) **if they took all reasonable steps to satisfy themselves** that the manager has the appropriate knowledge and experience and is carrying out the work competently and in compliance with s36.
4. s34(5): trustees may authorise two or more of their number, or a non-FSMA manager for activities that are not regulated. The trustees remain liable as if the acts were theirs. Exclusion of liability for a s34(5)(b) delegate's defaults is possible only if the same reasonable-steps conditions are met (s34(6)).
5. s34 overrides inconsistent restrictions under other law, except those in PA 1995 Part I or PSA 1993.

### 3. Statement of Investment Principles (s35; reg 2)
- Trustees must prepare, maintain and review a written **SIP** in the prescribed form.
- Before preparing or revising it they must obtain and consider **written advice** from a suitably qualified person and **consult the employer** (reg 2(2)). But neither the scheme nor the SIP may make investment powers subject to **employer consent** (s35(4)).
- **Content (reg 2(3))**: the policy for complying with s36; policies on kinds and balance of investments, risk, expected return, realisation, **"financially material considerations"** (defined to include ESG and climate change considerations the trustees consider financially material) over the appropriate time horizon, and **the extent (if at all) to which "non-financial matters"** (members' and beneficiaries' views, including ethical views and views on social and environmental impact and quality of life) are taken into account; voting and engagement policies; and asset manager arrangements.
- Breach: PA 1995 s10 civil penalties for trustees who failed to take all reasonable steps.

### 4. Choosing investments (s36; reg 4)
- Trustees, and delegated fund managers, must exercise powers in accordance with the regulations and **obtain and consider proper advice**, confirmed in writing, before investing (other than in Part I Sch 1 Trustee Investments Act 1961 investments). They must decide at what intervals to take advice on investments they retain (s36(3)-(4), (6)). They must give effect to the SIP so far as reasonably practicable (s36(5)).
- **Reg 4** (transposing IORP II Art 19):
  - invest in the **best interests of members and beneficiaries**, and in the **sole interest** where there is a potential conflict;
  - exercise powers to ensure the **security, quality, liquidity and profitability of the portfolio as a whole**;
  - match technical-provision assets to the nature and duration of liabilities;
  - invest **predominantly in regulated markets**, keeping unlisted assets to a prudent level;
  - **diversify properly**, avoiding excessive reliance on any asset, issuer or group and accumulations of risk;
  - use derivatives only to reduce risk or for efficient portfolio management.
- **Reg 5 and s36A**: no borrowing or guarantees from scheme assets, except temporary borrowing for liquidity.
- **IORP II Art 19(1)**: the "prudent person" rule, which the Directive does not define. (a) Invest in the **best long-term interests** of members and beneficiaries as a whole, and in the sole interest where there is a potential conflict. (b) Member States **shall allow** IORPs to take account of the potential long-term impact of investment decisions on **ESG factors**, but only *within* the prudent person rule. The discussion asks where UK law actually imposes the prudent person rule itself on OPS trustees.

### 5. Duty of care and exclusion (s33; s117; s256)
- **s33**: liability for breach of a duty of care or skill in performing investment functions, by a trustee or a s34 delegate, **cannot be excluded or restricted** by any instrument or agreement. This includes onerous conditions, limits on remedies, and evidence or procedure restrictions. Prescribed exceptions apply.
- **s117**: overriding requirements over scheme rules.
- **PA 2004 s256**: no reimbursement from scheme assets for trustees' fines or civil penalties.
- For **directors of a trustee company**: CA 2006 ss232-239 apply (see trustee-companies-directors-duties-and-conflicts).
- **Accessory liability** of third parties and directors: *Royal Brunei v Tan* (dishonest assistance).

### 6. General law: Cowan v Scargill [1985] Ch 270 (Megarry V-C, 4 April 1984)
- **Facts**: union-nominated trustees of the mineworkers' pension scheme refused to approve an investment plan unless it barred overseas investment and investment in industries competing with coal.
- **Held**: in breach of duty.
  1. The duty is to exercise powers in the **best interests of present and future beneficiaries**, holding the scales impartially. Where the trust's purpose is financial benefits, best interests are **normally their best financial interests**: the best return judged in relation to risk.
  2. Trustees must **put aside personal interests and views**, including strongly held social or political views. They may even have to act "dishonourably (though not illegally)" (*Buttle v Saunders*).
  3. Powers must be exercised for their proper purposes, not ulterior ones (*Balls v Strutt*; *Duke of Portland v Topham*).
  4. A caveat: "benefit" is not inevitably only financial. If all beneficiaries were adults with very strict moral views (for example against alcohol, tobacco, armaments), it might not be for their benefit to receive more from "tainted" sources. But the burden on those asserting this is heavy.
  5. Standard of care: that of an **ordinary prudent man investing for others for whom he felt morally bound to provide** (*Learoyd v Whiteley*). Trustees must take advice on matters they do not understand and may not reject it merely because they sincerely disagree.
  6. There is a duty to consider **diversification**. Excluding vast sectors such as all overseas investment is inconsistent with it.
  7. These principles apply to pension fund trusts.
- The discussion notes *Cowan* **pre-dates** PA 1995 s67, s75 and the PA 2004 Part 3 funding regime. For a solvent DB employer, each £1 of underperformance is £1 of extra employer cost.

### 7. FSMA constraints
- FSMA s19 (general prohibition); RAO art 37 (managing investments) and art 66 (trustees' exception); Carrying on Regulated Activities by Way of Business Order art 4 (OPS trustees). In general, trustees cannot make **day-to-day investment management decisions** unless authorised. There is an exception for decisions to buy or sell **pooled investment vehicle** interests taken with advice from a permitted person. See FCA PERG 10.

### 8. The Tontine problem, Q1 (individual trustees)
Facts: the trustees delegated to an FSMA-authorised manager under a "standard, non-negotiable" IMA, signed without legal advice. The IMA said the manager need not check the trust deed restrictions. The manager bought £10m of **life insurance shares, which the deed prohibits**, and £6m was lost after a tax change. The deed exonerates trustees except for "personal conscious bad faith", carves out s33 and s256 for individual trustees, and includes a directors' carve-out.
- **For liability**: the investment breached the deed (the s34(1) power is "subject to any restriction imposed by the scheme"). The trustees let the IMA dispense with compliance checks, so s34(4) protection may fail because they did not take all reasonable steps to ensure competent compliance with s36 and the deed. There was no legal advice on the IMA. Section 33 voids reliance on the exoneration clause for negligence in investment functions.
- **Against**: the manager was authorised and selected after a beauty parade with consultant advice (reasonable steps on appointment). The loss was caused by an unforeseeable tax change (causation and hindsight). Section 33 covers negligence in investment functions, so is choosing to sign the IMA an investment function? Is the clause "personal conscious bad faith" relevant beyond s33?
- **Remedies against third parties** (the manager, consultant) are excluded from the question.

## Key authorities
- IORP II Arts 7, 19 and recital 58; PA 2004 ss255, 256; PA 1995 ss10, 33, 34, 35, 36, 36A, 40, 117; Investment Regs 2005 regs 2, 4, 5, 12; Trustee Act 2000 s36; Trustee Act 1925 s25; FSMA s19; RAO arts 37, 66.
- *Cowan v Scargill* [1985] Ch 270; *Learoyd v Whiteley* (1887) 12 App Cas 727; *Buttle v Saunders* [1950] 2 All ER 193; *Royal Brunei Airlines v Tan* [1995] 2 AC 378.

## Debate and critique
- **Statute versus Cowan**: *Cowan*'s "best financial interests" approach predates the modern regulatory regime. Reg 4 now speaks of "best interests" and "security, quality, liquidity and profitability", and reg 2 asks for policies on financially material ESG factors and non-financial matters. Has the statute softened *Cowan*, or merely put it into operation? (See esg-ethical-and-political-investment.)
- **DB versus DC risk bearer**: in DB the employer bears investment risk (with Part 3 funding and s75). Investment choices therefore affect the employer covenant. This is why the employer must be consulted on the SIP but cannot have a veto (s35(4)). In DC the member bears the risk, which strengthens the case for member choice and for trustee care in designing defaults.
- **Delegation shield**: s34(4) protects trustees who take reasonable steps. Combined with standard-form IMAs and FSMA limits, lay trustees depend heavily on managers. The Tontine problem tests whether accepting "non-negotiable" terms that remove compliance checks is consistent with reasonable steps.
- **Non-excludable liability**: s33 is unusual in trust law, where exoneration for negligence is generally permitted (*Armitage v Nurse*). Pensions policy treats investment care as too important to contract out of.

## Applying it to a problem question
1. **Power**: does the deed (with s34) permit this investment? Are there express prohibitions? Is the activity within s255 retirement-benefit activities? Is it an employer-related investment (s40)?
2. **Process**: a current SIP (s35; reg 2) consulted on with the employer; proper written advice (s36(3)) before investing and at intervals; consistency with the SIP (s36(5)).
3. **Substance**: reg 4 (best interests; sole interest where conflicted; security, quality, liquidity, profitability; diversification; regulated markets; derivatives); *Cowan* (put aside personal views; prudent man; take advice).
4. **Delegation**: a FSMA-compliant manager (s34(2)(a)); reasonable steps on appointment and monitoring (s34(4)); IMA terms that require compliance with the deed and s36.
5. **Liability**: breach, causation and loss; exclusion barred by s33; indemnities limited by s256 (fines) and, for companies, CA s232-235.
6. **Third parties**: dishonest assistance (*Royal Brunei*); claims against the manager and advisers (if asked).
7. Traps: applying Trustee Act 2000 ss3-7 (disapplied by s36); assuming an exoneration clause protects investment negligence; forgetting the FSMA limit on trustees' day-to-day decisions.
