# Protection of accrued rights - Pensions Act 1995 section 67 (subsisting rights)

## Overview
Even where a valid amendment power exists and is properly used, statute stops occupational pension schemes from cutting rights members have **already built up**, unless specified safeguards are met. The main override is **PA 1995 s67, with ss67A-67I** (the "subsisting rights provisions"). For contracted-out rights there is also PSA 1993 s37, which the module ignores. Section 67 has three versions, applying to modifications made in different periods. It does **not** create a power of amendment, and it does not save an amendment that is invalid under the trust deed. So the correct order is **validity first, section 67 second** (master notes). The hard questions are what counts as a "subsisting right" and when a change "would or might adversely affect" it. *Aon v KPMG*, *QinetiQ* and the Courage restriction show how the courts handle those questions.

## The law

### 1. Which version applies?
The slides give three periods for modifications ("modification" is defined in PSA 1993 s181):
1. **6 April 1997 to 5 April 2006**: original s67 (use Perspective's time travel). It protects only amendments to accrued rights made after 5 April 1997.
2. **6 April 2006 to 31 July 2022**: s67-67I as substituted by the Pensions Act 2004.
3. **From 1 August 2022**: as amended by the Pension Schemes Act 2021 to deal with collective money purchase (CMP) benefits.
The handbook also tells students to read **PA 1995 s124(2)**: accrued rights are the rights that have accrued at that time to future benefits. For a member still in pensionable service, they are determined **as if the member had opted to leave pensionable service immediately before that time**. Pension credit rights are excluded from this subsection.

### 2. Structure of the current s67
1. **Scope (s67(1))**: any power conferred by an occupational pension scheme on any person to modify the scheme, except public service schemes and prescribed schemes.
2. **Prohibited modification, which is void (s67(1A); s67A(1A))**: a modification that would or might turn a member's or survivor's subsisting **non-money purchase (DB) right into a CMP right**.
3. **Regulated modification, which is voidable unless conditions are met (s67(2))**: a protected modification, a detrimental modification, or both (s67A(2)). The conditions, for each affected member:
   - for a **protected modification** (or in a CMP scheme), the **consent requirements** (s67B);
   - otherwise, **either** the consent requirements **or** the **actuarial equivalence requirements** (s67C);
   - plus the **trustee approval requirement** (s67E) and the **reporting requirement** (s67F).
4. **Protected modification (s67A(3), (3A))**: a non-prohibited modification that would or might cause a **relevant transformation** (DB to money purchase; individual money purchase to CMP; CMP to individual money purchase, subject to Modification Regulations 2006 reg 3(l), "Continuity Option 1"), **or reduce the prevailing rate of a pension in payment** (other than a CMP pension), or is of a prescribed description.
5. **Detrimental modification (s67A(4))**: any other non-prohibited modification that would or might **adversely affect** a member's or survivor's subsisting right. **"Adversely affect"** (s67A(10)(b)) means it would alter the nature or extent of the right so that the benefits "would or might be less generous".
6. **Subsisting right (s67A(6), (7))**: (a) for a member, any right that has **accrued** to future benefits, or any **entitlement to present payment**; (b) for a survivor, any entitlement or right to future benefits in respect of the member. Pension credit rights are included. For active members, the rights are determined as if the member had left pensionable service immediately before the modification.
7. **Consent requirements (s67B)**: informed consent, meaning written information explaining the modification and its effect, notice of the right to make representations, a reasonable opportunity to make them, notice that consent applies, then the member's **written consent**; and timing, meaning the modification takes effect within a reasonable period after consent.
8. **Actuarial equivalence requirements (s67C-67D)**: only for non-protected modifications, and only where the trustees decide they apply. They require information (all reasonable steps to give the same kind of written explanation and notices); **actuarial value**, meaning arrangements adequate to secure that the actuarial value of subsisting rights immediately after the change is at least equal to that immediately before; and an **actuarial equivalence statement** from the scheme actuary within a reasonable period after the modification takes effect.
9. **Trustee approval (s67E)**: the trustees decide to make the modification, or consent if another person exercises the power, only after the consent or information and actuarial value requirements are met, and within a reasonable period after the first consent.
10. **Reporting (s67F)**: notify affected members of the determination within a reasonable period and before the modification takes effect.
11. **Consequence (s67G)**: TPR ("the Authority" in the text) may make an order declaring that a voidable modification is void as regards specified members. The order may extend to related modifications or grants of rights, and may require the trustees to take steps. Pollard notes the pre-2006 position was also voidable at the Regulator's discretion.
12. **CMP-specific detrimental modifications**: see the Modification of Schemes Regulations 2006 reg 3(k) (balancing the books after a valuation) and reg 3(l) (Continuity Option 1) (slides).

### 3. What is an "accrued right"? The case law
- ***Re Courage*** [1987] 1 WLR 495: the restriction (third proviso) barred amendments that would "vary or affect any benefits already secured by past contributions". Millett J read benefits acquired by past contributions as including benefits to which a member is **prospectively entitled if he continues in the same employment**, which brings in the **final salary link** for past service (para 53 is flagged; the slides also ask whether the words "and accrued" in the All England report were approved by the judge). This is a scheme-specific restriction, wider than the statutory "leaving service" test.
- ***Aon Trust Corporation Ltd v KPMG*** [2005] EWCA Civ 1004 (CA, 28 July 2005): new clause 8.5 let trustees, with employer consent and after actuarial advice, adjust "benefits secured or thereafter accruing" to restore solvency after a deficiency. The CA held that this power to reduce benefits to match assets at a valuation date was a **"power to modify" caught by s67** (in its then form).
- ***QinetiQ Trustees v QinetiQ Holdings*** [2012] EWHC 570 (Ch) (High Court, 14 March 2012): "Index" meant RPI "or any other suitable cost-of-living index selected by the Trustees". The trustees could move to CPI **without breaching s67**. For pensions in payment, a member becomes entitled to a specific increase only on each annual increase date, so future increases are not yet accrued. For deferred revaluation calculated at the end of deferment, CPI for future retirements likewise does not breach s67. Trustees may use different indices for different purposes. The Mayer Brown note adds that trustees must still be satisfied that the switch is in beneficiaries' interests.
- ***Barnardo's v Buckinghamshire*** [2018] UKSC 55 (contrast): the issue was construction, not s67. Where the rules allowed only an official "replacement" of RPI, the trustees had no power to switch while RPI continued. Section 67 never arose because there was no power.
- The handbook links these to the **Everlasting Life** problem. If the deed already defines NRD by a longevity adjustment factor, applying it to push NRD from 65 to 66 may be operating an existing rule rather than a "modification" (compare *QinetiQ*). Where instead a separate employer power allows NRD to be amended, using it would be a modification and engage s67 (and the question of what type of power it is).

### 4. Relationship with other controls
- Section 67 protects **past service** (the "leaving service" test). Future accrual changes fall outside it (see changing-future-accrual-and-consultation). But scheme provisos can protect more, as in *Courage* and *BBC* ("interests").
- Section 67 does not validate an amendment outside the power, and consultation does not cure invalidity (master notes).
- Statutory powers of modification exist separately: s68 and the 2006 Regulations.

## Debate and critique
- **Narrow statutory protection**: because accrued rights are measured as if the member left service, s67 does not protect the final salary link for past service, which a member would lose on leaving anyway. *Courage*-type provisos and the BBC "interests" wording can be more protective than statute. So members' protection depends on accidents of drafting.
- **Timing of entitlement**: *QinetiQ* treats annual increases as arising year by year, which allows prospective cuts in indexation without consent. Critics see this as a significant erosion of the value of accrued pensions (CPI usually runs below RPI). Supporters say it reflects the scheme's wording and that s67 was never meant to freeze discretionary or index-based mechanics.
- **Actuarial equivalence**: lets trustees change benefit shape without consent, provided overall actuarial value is kept. But actuarial value depends on assumptions, and members may value the benefit form (for example guaranteed increases) differently from an actuary.
- **Voidable, not void**: s67G gives TPR a discretion. That brings flexibility but also uncertainty for administrators and members, especially where breaches are found decades later (compare the contracted-out cases mentioned in the materials-folder summary).
- **Three versions**: practitioners must apply the version in force at the date of each historic modification, which is classic "pensions archaeology".

## Applying it to a problem question
1. **Validity first**: is there a power, used by the right party, in the right form, for a proper purpose, within the express provisos? (amendment-powers-and-employer-constraints)
2. **Date of the modification**: pick the s67 version (pre-6 April 2006; 2006-2022; post-1 August 2022). Before 6 April 1997, s67 does not apply.
3. **Is it a "modification" at all**, or the operation of an existing rule (*QinetiQ*; Everlasting Life Q1)? Is a benefit-adjustment power itself a power to modify (*Aon v KPMG*)?
4. **Identify subsisting rights**: accrued rights on the leaving-service basis (s67A(6)-(7); s124(2)); entitlements in payment; survivors' rights.
5. **Classify**: prohibited (DB to CMP: void); protected (relevant transformation, or a cut in pension in payment: consent needed); detrimental (consent or actuarial equivalence); or not adverse (outside s67).
6. **Check process**: s67B or s67C-67D steps, s67E trustee approval within a reasonable period, s67F reporting before the effective date.
7. **Consequence**: voidable, and TPR may order it void for specified members (s67G).
8. **Then** consider the scheme-specific provisos (for example *Courage*'s "benefits already secured by past contributions"), which may protect final salary linkage beyond s67.
9. Traps: treating s67 as protecting future accrual; forgetting the leaving-service deeming for active members; assuming any CPI switch breaches s67 (it depends on when entitlement arises); applying the current s67 to a 2003 amendment.
