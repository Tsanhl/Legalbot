# Member dispute resolution and the Pensions Ombudsman

## Overview
Accrued pension rights are often a member's most valuable or second most valuable asset. A £10,000 a year pension from 65 would cost about £127,242 to buy from an insurer in September 2025 (single life, level, no guarantee). Unlike a house, though, the member cannot see it. It rests on a long, often-amended trust deed and may have been transferred between schemes. Court litigation is beyond most members' means: civil legal aid generally requires gross income of no more than £31,884 and disposable capital of no more than £8,000, unless there is insurance, union support or a conditional fee. Parliament filled this access-to-justice gap with the **Pensions Ombudsman** (PSA 1993 Part X, ss145-152, and the 1996 Regulations). It is a free, investigative, legally binding forum for disputes of fact or law and complaints of maladministration, subject to internal dispute resolution first, jurisdictional exclusions, time limits and natural justice limits (*Edge*).

## The law

### 1. Functions and jurisdiction (PSA 1993 s146)
The Ombudsman may investigate and determine:
1. **Maladministration complaints** (s146(1)(a)) by or for an **actual or potential beneficiary** who alleges **injustice** caused by **maladministration** in an act or omission of a person responsible for managing the scheme. There are related complaints by managers or trustees about other managers or schemes, and by independent trustees.
2. **Disputes of fact or law** (s146(1)(c) and following) between a person responsible for management and an actual or potential beneficiary, and other listed disputes (for example involving employers and independent trustees). A dispute under (c)-(g) must be referred by the appropriate party, for example the beneficiary.
3. **Administrators**: under reg 1A of the 1996 Regulations, complaints of maladministration by administrators are also within jurisdiction.
- **Who is a member / beneficiary**: reg 1 deems anyone who is or has been entitled to benefits to be a member for s146(7). This matters for survivors and dependants, such as Beta in the the materials problem.
- **Matters before 1 October 1990** may still be investigated.
- The handbook asks: what is "maladministration", and how is it interpreted? The term is not defined in the sources provided.

### 2. Exclusions and preconditions
- **Internal dispute resolution first** (reg 2): where PA 1995 s50 (IDRP) applies, the Ombudsman will not investigate until the trustees have issued written notice of a decision under s50(5)(b). The exception is where there is no real prospect of a decision within a reasonable period and it is reasonable to proceed.
- **Existing court or tribunal proceedings** (s146): no investigation where proceedings on the same matters have begun and not been discontinued, or were discontinued on a settlement binding the complainants.
- **Excluded schemes** by regulations.
- **FOS overlap** (reg 3): matters within the Financial Ombudsman Service's compulsory jurisdiction (FSMA Part 16) are excluded, except complaints about managing a personal pension scheme.
- **No findings of fact on listed statutory breaches** (reg 3): surplus payments (PA 1995 ss37, 76), employer-related investment (s40), appointing advisers (s47(1)(a)-(b)), records (s49), money purchase payment schedules (ss87-88), **PA 2004 Part 3 scheme funding**, and member-nominated trustee rules (PA 2004 ss241-243). These are for TPR.
- **Time limit** (reg 5): **3 years** from the act or omission. If the complainant was unaware of it, the 3 years runs from when they knew or ought reasonably to have known. There is a discretion to extend where a delay was reasonable.

### 3. Procedure
- **Investigative, not adversarial**: the Ombudsman investigates for the member, free of charge. The member is not expected to get legal advice. Costs orders are generally not made, though there are some circumstances where the member can recover costs.
- Steps: the respondent is asked for information and views; the member comments; a **draft determination** is issued; the parties comment; then the **final determination**.
- **Natural justice**: everyone affected must be heard. *Edge*: third parties affected by an eventual determination must be able to state their position.

### 4. Determinations, enforcement, appeals (s151)
- A written statement with reasons goes to the complainant and the person responsible for management (s151(1)). The Ombudsman may direct that person to take or refrain from taking steps (s151(2)).
- **Final and binding** on the complainant, the person responsible for management, and **any person claiming under them** (s151(3)). *Edge*: that phrase covers dependants and personal representatives, not non-complainant members or employers whose interests are adverse.
- **Appeal on a point of law** to the High Court (s151(4)), by any person bound.
- **Enforceable** in the county court as if it were a judgment (s151(5)).
- Reports may be published. Publication is absolutely privileged for defamation.
- **Interest on late paid benefit** (s151A; reg 6: reference banks' base rate).

### 5. Allocation of jurisdiction between court and Ombudsman
- A member with a dispute of fact or law can (1) sue or (2) complain to the Ombudsman. For **discrimination** claims they may also (3) go to an employment tribunal, or bring County Court or High Court proceedings.
- **s148 stay**: where a complaint or reference has been made and a party then starts court proceedings, any party may apply (after acknowledging service and before taking any other step) to stay them. The court may stay if there is no sufficient reason why the Ombudsman should not investigate and the applicant is ready and willing to cooperate.
- On cost and effort the Ombudsman is "always" the member's preferred route, but **time limits** may favour going to court in some cases. Representative beneficiary proceedings funded from the trust are outside the module (the handbook refers back to *BA v APST*).

### 6. Limits from Edge v Pensions Ombudsman [2000] Ch 602 (CA, 29 July 1999)
1. The Ombudsman has **no greater powers than a court** to remedy breaches of trust.
2. The supposed **duty of impartiality** is simply the ordinary duty to use a discretionary power for its purpose, taking account of relevant matters only. Trustees may prefer one class (here active members over pensioners when distributing surplus) and the Ombudsman must not substitute his view of what is "fair".
3. Part X makes **no provision for representing class interests**. The Ombudsman cannot entertain complaints that can only be remedied by **adversely affecting people not bound** by his determination (here employers and active members whose contributions he purported to increase). He should have declined the investigation. This was not strictly for want of jurisdiction, but because no effective remedy could be given.
4. The Ombudsman may seek leave to appeal a costs order against him, but costs will be awarded only where a point of principle with conflicting High Court decisions makes his work difficult.
5. The member-trustee conflict argument was abandoned in the CA. PA 1995 s39 had settled it for the future.

### 7. Overpayments and restitution (the materials link)
- *Scottish Equitable plc v Derby* [2000] PLR 1: a mistaken overpayment is prima facie recoverable. **Change of position** operates pro tanto, needs a causal link, and early repayment of an existing debt is not detriment. **Estoppel** is not a complete defence where detriment is small. This framework is commonly used when the Ombudsman handles recovery of overpaid pensions.

- Personal and Occupational Pension Schemes (Pensions Ombudsman) Regulations 1996 (SI 1996/2475): reg 1 and 1A (members; administrators), reg 2 (IDRP first), reg 3 (exclusions), reg 5 (3-year limit), reg 6 (interest).

- *Harris v Lord Shuttleworth* [1995] OPLR 79 (CA): the test for reviewing trustee discretion.

## Debate and critique
- **Access to justice**: the Ombudsman is free, investigative and binding, a model of specialist ADR for vulnerable claimants. But it is excluded from some areas (scheme funding, statutory breaches that are TPR's business), bound by a 3-year limit, and unable to deal with class-wide remedies (*Edge*). Systemic problems may therefore need court proceedings with representative beneficiaries.
- **Maladministration versus law**: the Ombudsman can award for maladministration (for example distress) where a court might not. After *Edge* he cannot substitute his own idea of fairness for a trustee's lawful discretion. The boundary between finding a flawed process and second-guessing the outcome is contestable.
- **Natural justice and multi-party schemes**: pension decisions often redistribute between classes. *Edge* means the individual complaint model fits poorly with the collective nature of trust funds.
- **Forum shopping and time limits**: the overlap with employment tribunals for discrimination claims, and the different limitation regimes (compare *Hughes* on the 6-year limit for PPF arrears, and the Limitation Act s21(1)(b) for trust property, in pension-protection-fund), create strategic choices for members' advisers.

## Applying it to a problem question
1. **Standing**: is the claimant an actual or potential beneficiary (reg 1: anyone who is or has been entitled to benefits; dependants and survivors who may be within a discretionary class)? In the the materials Beta problem: is Beta an "Eligible Adult Dependant" (dependency or financial interdependence) or within the clause 18 "Named Class"? If not, is Beta a potential beneficiary at all?
2. **Type of claim**: dispute of fact or law, or maladministration with injustice, or both? Identify the respondent (trustees, administrator, employer).
3. **Preconditions**: IDRP completed (reg 2)? No existing court proceedings? Not excluded (FOS, reg 3 list)? Within 3 years (reg 5, knowledge, discretion)?
4. **Merits**: apply the trustee decision checklist (challenging-trustee-decisions). For discretionary death benefits: did the trustees identify the full class, make enquiries, avoid fettering themselves by treating an expression of wishes as binding, consider relevant changes (Gamma's new wealth; the Alpha-Beta relationship), avoid irrelevant factors and conflicts (employer-appointed trustees benefiting if assets go unallocated and employer contributions fall), and act within the 2-year window?
5. **Remedy**: usually the Ombudsman (or a court) directs the trustees to **retake** the decision properly rather than making it for them (*Edge*: no greater powers than a court; *Mihlenstedt*). Also consider interest (s151A) and distress or maladministration awards. Payments already made, such as the lump sum to Gamma, raise recovery issues (restitution, change of position).
6. **Defending trustees** (the materials Q2): a valid process (*Edge*/*Harris*); the class definitions; a proper weighing of the expression of wishes; Beta's lack of standing; time limits; no effective remedy without affecting third parties.
7. **Appeal**: on a point of law only, to the High Court (s151(4)).
8. Traps: going straight to the Ombudsman without IDRP; asking the Ombudsman to rule on funding (Part 3) breaches; assuming the Ombudsman can award the benefit outright; forgetting that adversely affected third parties are not bound.
