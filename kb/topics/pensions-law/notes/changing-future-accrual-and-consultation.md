# Changing future service benefits - amendment restrictions, contract, consultation and dismissal risk

## Overview
Employers facing rising DB costs often want to stop or reduce **future** accrual: freezing pensionable pay, closing to future accrual, or moving to DC for future service. PA 1995 s67 does not apply to rights not yet accrued. But that does not leave the employer free. It still needs (1) an adequate power to amend or to cut off accrual, (2) to check that the employment contract, later variations, collective agreements and communications have not given employees rights that block the change, (3) to comply with statutory consultation (PA 2004 ss259-261 and the 2006 Regulations), (4) to act consistently with the Imperial duty and the contractual duties of trust, confidence and good faith, and (5) if it goes down the "fire and re-hire" route, to manage unfair dismissal (ERA 1996) and collective redundancy (TULRCA ss188-194) risk. The discussion "Wessex Wind" problem tests all of these.

## The law

### 1. Is there power to change future accrual?
1. Section 67 is not engaged if accrued rights are untouched. Remember, though, that freezing final pensionable pay for past service *does* touch the final salary link that some provisos protect, even though statutory accrued rights are measured on a leaving-service basis.
2. **Construe the restriction** in the amendment power. Wording matters:
   - **Courage** (third proviso: no variation of "benefits already secured by past contributions"): read to protect benefits a member is prospectively entitled to if he stays in the same employment, so it covers the **final salary link for past service** (para 53).
   - **Lloyds Bank Pension Trust Corp v Lloyds Bank plc** [1996] PLR 263 / [1996] OPLR 181 (Rimer J): general rule 9 required written sanction from three-quarters (pre-1 November 1983 joiners) or three-fifths (later joiners) for any alteration that in the actuary's opinion "may" increase contributions or decrease "the pecuniary benefits secured". It barred adverse alteration of leavers' and pensioners' pecuniary rights and any change making the main purpose cease to be pensions. Pollard (17.188-17.193) treats this as a restriction **construed even more widely than Courage**, protecting benefits on the same rationale as the Australian *Gas & Fuel Corp of Victoria v Fitzmaurice* case (future service benefits covered). Rimer J rejected the employer's argument (Edward Nugee QC) that pension promises are inherently precarious because the employer can dismiss, re-hire or end participation. Those points were irrelevant to construing the amending power: "The message means what it says, no more and no less."
   - **BBC v BBC Pension Trust Ltd** [2023] EWHC 1965 (Ch), upheld [2024] EWCA Civ 767: rule 19 was a broad trustee power exercisable with BBC consent, but proviso 19.2(3) protected active members' "interests". These were held to include (a) past service rights, (b) the final salary link for those rights (subject to *Bradbury*: only such salary as the BBC determines to be pensionable Basic Salary), (c) the ability to accrue future service benefits on the same terms, and (d) the ability to accrue any future benefits at all. So changing or closing future accrual was outside the power without satisfying the proviso. The CA discussed *Barnardo's*, *Courage* and *Lloyds*.
3. Worked example: with a Lloyds-type power, a pay freeze plus a DC switch needs the member sanction thresholds. With a Courage-type power, freezing past-service pensionable pay breaches the proviso. The "point to think about" is whether a variant keeps the past-service salary link while switching future service to DC (or making future pay rises non-pensionable by contract), which would still achieve most of the savings.

### 2. Contractual and collective constraints
- Review the employment contracts issued over time, variations, booklets and communications, and any collectively bargained pay agreement with recognised unions. Any of these may restrict the power or give employees enforceable rights. "Reasonably substantial due diligence" is needed.
- **Contract overriding trust** (Pollard ch 15): employer and member can agree reduced accrual. Examples are non-pensionable pay rises (*Bradbury v BBC* [2012] EWHC 1369 (Ch), where Warren J rejected Mr Bradbury's claim) and union agreements (*South West Trains v Wightman* [1998] PLR 113; *Tibbals v Port of London Authority* [1937] 2 All ER 413, HL). The usual contract requirements must be met. Vague forms referring to booklets may fail (*Re IMG Pension Plan, HR Trustees v German* [2009] EWHC 2785 (Ch); *IBM* [2012]). In *IMG*, Arnold J said obiter that an extrinsic contract overriding contrary deed terms must amount to **informed consent** of the beneficiaries. Pollard thinks this is "odd", since the contract is with the employer, not a fiduciary. Warren J in *Bradbury* reviewed *IMG*.
- Pollard's practical checklist: check employment contracts; get express employee agreement; make all communications clear; have and document good reasons; involve trustees early; run a thorough consultation.
- *Tesco v USDAW* [2024] UKSC 28 (reversing the CA [2022] EWCA Civ 978, which had reversed the HC [2022] EWHC 201 (QB)): whether "retained pay" promises restricted Tesco's freedom to terminate on notice and re-engage on new terms.

### 3. Statutory consultation (PA 2004 ss259-261; SI 2006/349)
- Applies to an employer with **50 or more employees in Great Britain** (from 6 April 2008; higher thresholds earlier), whether or not most are scheme members, that proposes a **"listed change"**. Listed changes include closing to future accrual, increasing member contributions, reducing future accrual, and replacing DB with DC for future service. From 6 April 2010 they also include changes to which elements of pay are pensionable or limits on pensionable pay (Pollard 15.61-15.64).
- It requires at least **60 days' consultation** with employee representatives or employees directly, and provision of prescribed information.
- **Sanction**: limited. Pollard notes a TPR civil penalty can be charged, and the Act confirms the change is effective even if consultation was not followed (15.65). But **IBM (HC)** held that failing to comply was a breach of the **implied contractual duty of good faith** (not challenged on appeal; see amendment-powers-and-employer-constraints). Consultation must be genuine and take place while the proposal can still be influenced. It is a process duty, not a member veto.

### 4. Fire and re-hire: unfair dismissal risk (ERA 1996)
- If the employer gives **full contractual notice** and offers re-employment on identical terms except future pension, there is no damages claim for breach of the old contract. But there is exposure to an **unfair dismissal** claim (reinstatement, re-engagement or compensation).
- The view is that if the correct procedure is followed (including consultation) and there is a **genuine business need** (a substantive reason), the claim should fail. Its author knows of no successful employee claim.
- **Reform**: clause 28 of the Employment Rights Bill (version of 23 July 2025) would make fire and re-hire automatically unfair unless needed for business survival. The Bill was still before Parliament at 22 September 2025.

### 5. Collective redundancy consultation (TULRCA 1992 ss188-194)
- Read literally, these concern proposals to dismiss 20 or more employees as redundant. But they transpose **Directive 98/59/EC**, whose definition of "collective redundancies" covers dismissals "for one or more reasons not related to the individual workers concerned" above set thresholds (for example at least 20 over 90 days). So "redundancy" takes the broader Directive meaning, and dismissals for re-engagement on new pension terms can be caught. This interpretive rule survives the REUL Act 2023.
- **Sanction**: a protective award of up to **90 days' pay** per affected employee, plus possible criminal sanctions for failing to notify the Secretary of State. The solution is to comply with consultation and notification.

### 6. Imperial duty constraint
- Even a valid amendment or dismissal route must respect the Imperial duty (on employer powers under the deed) and the contractual duty of trust and confidence. After *IBM* (CA) the test is Wednesbury rationality. Employer cost-saving is a legitimate interest, and reasonable expectations are only a relevant factor. *Imperial* still bars collateral purposes such as coercing members to give up accrued rights. The discussion asks whether the Braganza duty applies to termination on notice at all, and whether *Tesco v USDAW* shows a "wobble".

## Key authorities
- *Re Courage* [1987] 1 WLR 495: "benefits already secured by past contributions" protects the final salary link.
- *Lloyds Bank Pension Trust Corp v Lloyds Bank* [1996] PLR 263 (Rimer J): a wide "pecuniary benefits secured" restriction with member sanction thresholds; employer's precariousness arguments irrelevant to construction.
- *BBC v BBC Pension Trust* [2023] EWHC 1965 (Ch); [2024] EWCA Civ 767: "interests" covers future accrual and final salary linkage.
- *Bradbury v BBC* [2012] EWHC 1369 (Ch): non-pensionable pay rises upheld.
- *South West Trains v Wightman* [1998] PLR 113; *Tibbals* [1937] 2 All ER 413 (HL); *HR Trustees v German (IMG)* [2009] EWHC 2785 (Ch).
- *IBM UK Holdings v Dalgleish* [2014] EWHC 980 (Ch); [2017] EWCA Civ 1212.
- *Tesco v USDAW* [2024] UKSC 28.
- PA 2004 ss259-261; Occupational and Personal Pension Schemes (Consultation by Employers and Miscellaneous Amendment) Regulations 2006 (SI 2006/349); ERA 1996; TULRCA 1992 ss188-194; Directive 98/59/EC.

## Debate and critique
- **Protection by drafting accident**: future accrual is protected only where an old proviso happens to be broad (*Lloyds*, *BBC*). Members in otherwise similar schemes get very different protection. *BBC* shows that 1949-era wording can block twenty-first-century reform.
- **Weak statutory consultation**: a 60-day duty with weak sanctions and no veto. *IBM*'s route (breach of the implied good faith term) gives it teeth, but only contractually, and the "over fine" contract/Imperial split can trap litigants.
- **Fire and re-hire policy**: it is presented as low risk if done properly. The Employment Rights Bill reflects a political view that it is abusive. Essay angle: should pension restructuring be treated differently from pay cuts?
- **Contract overriding trust**: Pollard backs *South West Trains* and is sceptical of *IMG*'s "informed consent" gloss. Others (cited by Pollard) doubt that external contracts should bind trustees at all. Informed consent protects vulnerable members against pressured sign-ups (*IMG*'s six reasons) but makes reform costlier.
- **Rationality as the outer limit**: *IBM* (CA) lets employers close DB to meet group financial targets. Critics argue this undervalues years of reassuring communications.

## Applying it to a problem question
1. **Separate past and future**. Is any accrued right or final salary link affected? If so, also run section-67 and proviso analysis.
2. **Find and construe the power and its provisos** (*Courage*, *Lloyds*, *BBC* wording). Check consent thresholds and actuarial opinions; joint trustee consent (proper purpose, genuine consideration); formalities and effective date.
3. **Due diligence** on contracts, variations, booklets, communications, collective agreements and retained pay promises.
4. **Consultation**: 50+ GB employees? A listed change? 60 days, information, genuine engagement. Note the IBM good faith consequence of non-compliance.
5. **Imperial / trust and confidence**: rational, not for a collateral purpose, expectations weighed, process documented.
6. **If the power is inadequate**: get member agreement (contract), with clear explanation and possibly advice (to avoid *IMG* problems). For holdouts, consider fire and re-hire, assessing ERA unfair dismissal (fair procedure plus substantive business reason), TULRCA s188 thresholds on the Directive meaning (90 days' pay protective award), and pending reform.
7. **Other risks**: equality (indirect age or sex discrimination in transitional terms), communications and misrepresentation, tax, TPR reporting, and employee relations (strikes, as in USS).
8. Traps: assuming s67 is the only barrier; reading TULRCA "redundancy" literally; ignoring collective agreements; treating consultation as optional because the change is effective regardless.
