# The EU, UK regulatory and tax framework for occupational pensions

## Overview
UK occupational pensions sit under several layers of rules. These are retained (now "assimilated") EU law, chiefly the IORP II Directive and the equality, insolvency and TUPE Directives; a large body of UK pensions statutes and regulations; general trust statutes, partly disapplied; and a separate tax code in the Finance Act 2004. Brexit, through the European Union (Withdrawal) Act 2018 and the Retained EU Law (Revocation and Reform) Act 2023, changed how the EU-derived parts can be enforced, but it did not remove them. This note maps the framework and the interpretive rules a pensions lawyer needs. It also covers the tax regime at the level the discussion requires, which is awareness only (the discussion says there are "no essay questions on it").

## The law

### 1. Retained EU requirements
1. The **IORP II Directive (2016/2341)**, which replaced IORP I, is the main EU measure on funded occupational schemes. Key articles: Art 2 (scope), Art 6 (definitions), Art 7 (activities), Art 9 (home member state supervision, based on registration and main administration), Art 12 (cross-border activity), Art 13 (technical provisions, meaning the value of liabilities), Art 14 (funding of technical provisions), Art 19 (investment rules).
2. Other EU sources covered elsewhere:
   - non-discrimination (sex, sexual orientation, age);
   - protection of employment rights on a transfer of an undertaking;
   - protection of pension rights on employer insolvency.
3. The **Pensions Act 2004 is the main Act transposing IORP I**. The cross-border provisions (PA 2004 ss252-255, 287-295) no longer apply after Brexit.
4. **Interpretive obligation**: UK legislation that transposed a Directive by 11pm on 31 December 2020 (the "IP completion date") must, in general, be read so as to give effect to that Directive.

### 2. Principal UK pensions legislation
- Pension Schemes Act 1993; Pensions Act 1995; Pensions Act 2004; Pension Schemes Act 2021. Other Acts are listed in the table, which did not survive extraction. All of them have "Pension(s)" in the title. There are also many regulations made under them.
- Practical tip: amending Acts are usually cited by reference to the original Act. Use Perspective's "time travel" to see the text in force at a particular date. This matters for section 67 and for PSA 1993 s1 before and after 6 April 2006.
- **General trust statutes**: Trustee Act 1925; Perpetuities and Accumulations Act 2009 (which exempts relevant pension schemes); **Trustee Act 2000, of which s36 disapplies most of the Act for occupational pension schemes**; Trustee Investments Act 1961 and Trusts (Capital and Income) Act 2013, whose default rules are usually excluded expressly or by necessary implication in a well-drafted pension deed.
- **PA 2004 s252**: a UK-based OPS receiving payments must be established under irrevocable trust with effective rules (subject to the Trust and Retirement Benefits Exemption Regulations 2005).
- **PA 1995 s33**: overrides trust-deed exclusion clauses so far as they would exclude or limit liability for breach of duty in performing investment functions. Read it with **s117**, the general override.

### 3. General law areas carried into pensions
| Area | Pensions application |
|---|---|
| Contract | Offer, acceptance and consideration for promises to deferred or pensioner members; privity for survivors; Contracts (Rights of Third Parties) Act 1999 s6(3); whether UCTA applies to trust deed exoneration clauses; deed polls. |
| Tort / restitution | Negligent misstatement (economic loss, reliance), as in the *Corsham* case on tax treatment; mistaken overpayments (*Scottish Equitable v Derby*). |
| Employment | Unfair dismissal (ERA 1996) on changes to future service; implied duties of trust and confidence and of good faith (*Imperial*, *Braganza*); TUPE. |
| Trusts | Irrevocable trust protects assets from employer insolvency; general rules (no conflicts, no profit) apply "unless otherwise disapplied or authorised by an express provision of the trust deed" (for example NAPS clause 27); exoneration clauses, but subject to PA 1995 s33. |
| EU law | Interpretation, direct effect, state/emanation, Charter, general principles, Francovich, all as modified after Brexit. |

### 4. EU law doctrines (pre-Brexit) and how they apply now
Pre-Brexit, before a *Francovich* damages claim arose, the following steps applied in order:
1. **Vertical effect**: the State or an emanation of the State cannot rely on its own failure to transpose a Directive against a non-State claimant.
2. **Consistent interpretation**: courts must use the interpretive tools available to read UK law consistently with the Treaty or Directive obligation.
3. **Disapplication between private parties**: where consistent interpretation failed, courts had to set aside UK law that conflicted with a general principle of EU law referenced in the Treaties, or with a Charter right (Charter given Treaty status by TEU Art 6; the UK's Protocol 30 opt-out has been called "a political fig leaf").
4. **Treaty articles with horizontal direct effect** (for example TFEU Art 157) create rights between private persons.
5. Only after all of these failed would a **Francovich** claim against the State arise.

**European Union (Withdrawal) Act 2018** (as amended by the EU (Withdrawal Agreement) Act 2020), ss2-7 and Sch 1:
- EU law forming part of UK law by the IP completion date is kept until Parliament changes it.
- s4(1): directly effective Treaty rights (other than the Charter) continued.
- s5(4): the Charter is no longer part of domestic law.
- Sch 1 para 4: *Francovich* damages abolished.
- Sch 1 paras 2-3: general principles can no longer be used to disapply legislation or as a freestanding cause of action. They kept interpretive and judicial review effect.
- All subject to limited savings.
- Pre-IP-completion ECJ case law stays binding but can be departed from by the Court of Appeal and Supreme Court.

**Retained EU Law (Revocation and Reform) Act 2023** (from the end of 31 December 2023):
- "Retained EU law" is renamed **"assimilated law"** (s5). The old term is kept here for ease.
- Unless an exception applies: no *Francovich* claim; a claim of the type in step 3 against the State or an emanation is available only where the right had been recognised by an EU or UK court before the IP completion date; no freestanding claim on general principles or the Charter.
- **Exception**: the act or omission occurred, and proceedings were started, before the IP completion date (or within 3 years after it; 2 years for *Francovich*). This is subject to a proviso against certain disapplication challenges under EUWA Sch 5 para 39(5)(b).
- Rights from retained EU law for acts before the IP completion date stay enforceable unless and until Parliament changes them.
- **For acts on or after the IP completion date**: general principles can no longer be used as an interpretive aid; retained direct EU legislation must be read compatibly with domestic legislation; courts can no longer set aside incompatible domestic law where no interpretive solution exists; departing from retained case law is easier; **claims based on directly effective Treaty articles such as Art 157 TFEU are no longer available**.
- **The interpretive rule for UK legislation that transposed pre-IP-completion Directives continues.** This is relied on when reading TULRCA s188 in line with Directive 98/59/EC.

### 5. Jurisdiction
- The question is how far UK pensions legislation reaches: employees abroad, members who retire overseas, and foreign schemes covering UK-based staff. Under IORP II Art 9 supervision belongs to the member state of registration and main administration.
- The discussion goes no further than this.

### 6. The tax regime (Finance Act 2004)
Reliefs for a **registered pension scheme** (FA 2004 s153):
1. **Employer contributions** are generally deductible against profits (the discussion notes that spreading rules may apply).
2. **Employee contributions** are deductible up to the amount of employment income, subject to the annual allowance.
3. **No benefit-in-kind charge** on the value accrued. Instead an **annual allowance charge** applies to accrual above the allowance across all registered schemes, generally **£60,000**. The allowance tapers down to as little as £10,000 for taxable income over £200,000. DB annual allowance calculations are outside scope.
4. **Investment income and gains** of a registered OPS trust are free of UK tax.
5. **Tax-free lump sum**: 25% of the capital value of benefits (25% of the DC pot), capped at **£268,275** from 6 April 2023, aggregated across schemes. The rest is taxed as income.
6. The **lifetime allowance** was abolished from 6 April 2024 (detail outside scope).
- Definitions: FA 2004 s150 ("pension scheme", which includes death; "occupational pension scheme"; "sponsoring employer"), s151 (member categories, folding survivors into pensioners), s152, s153 (registration), s154 (who may establish a PPS), s165 and s279 (pension rules; minimum pension age).
- Scheme administrators can be liable for **negligent misstatement** about tax treatment. The *Corsham* case is cited as an example, with no further detail in the sources.

## Debate and critique
- **How much EU law is left?** The Government's Charter analysis said the Charter added no new rights, so removing it lost nothing. The Joint Committee on Human Rights (report) took the opposite view. Both are useful essay sources on whether Brexit weakened pension members' protection.
- **Assimilated law after 2023**: losing direct claims under Art 157 for acts after the IP completion date is significant for sex equality in pensions, where Art 157 was historically the engine of reform (*Barber*). Directive-derived domestic law still has to be read consistently with the Directive, so the result is a split regime that depends on when the relevant acts took place.
- **The Charter opt-out**: the "fig leaf" remark reflects the CJEU's approach that Protocol 30 did not exempt the UK, a point to cite critically.
- **Default trust law versus pensions statute**: Trustee Act 2000 s36 and PA 1995 s33 show Parliament treating pension trusts as a distinct kind of trust, with stricter rules than family trusts in some respects (no exclusion of liability for investment negligence) and looser ones in others (investment powers come from the PA 1995 regime instead).
- **Tax as a design driver**: pension scheme drafting has historically followed Revenue practice. This is why schemes are construed "against their fiscal backgrounds" (*Barnardo's*, see amendment-powers-and-employer-constraints) and why tax-driven limits (earnings cap, minimum pension age) shape benefit design.

## Applying it to a problem question
1. **Is the rule EU-derived?** If so, identify the Directive or Treaty article and the date of the relevant acts (before or after 31 December 2020, and before or after the end of 2023).
2. **Which enforcement route?** Consistent interpretation (still available for transposing legislation); direct effect of a Treaty article (only for acts before the IP completion date, subject to time limits); a claim against the State or an emanation; *Francovich* (gone, subject to savings).
3. **Which UK statute governs?** Check classification first (OPS or PPS; DB or DC). Then check overriding provisions (PA 1995 s117, s33, s67; PA 2004 s252).
4. **Does general trust law apply, or has the deed or statute displaced it?** Trustee Act 2000 s36; express conflict authorisations; exoneration clauses.
5. **Tax angle?** Is the scheme registered (s153)? Do the annual allowance, lump sum cap or minimum pension age affect the proposal? Is there any misstatement risk?
6. **Jurisdiction?** Flag any cross-border element. The discussion does not expect detailed analysis.
7. Traps: using "retained EU law" without dating the facts; assuming Art 157 claims survive for post-2020 conduct; assuming the Trustee Act 2000 investment rules govern pension trusts.
