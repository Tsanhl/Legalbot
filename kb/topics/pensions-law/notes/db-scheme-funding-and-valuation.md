# Funding defined benefit schemes - valuation methods, assumptions and Part 3 PA 2004

## Overview
A DB scheme promises benefits whose cost will only be known decades from now. The law therefore requires periodic actuarial valuations to measure liabilities ("technical provisions") against assets, with recovery plans for deficits. EU law sets the framework through IORP II Arts 13-14, transposed by **PA 2004 Part 3 (ss221-233)** and the **Scheme Funding Regulations 2005**. The discussion is concept-based. The maths is not needed, but it is essential to understand **valuation method versus assumptions**, **discounting**, the different **valuation bases** (accounting, Part 3 prudent, PPF s143, buy-out/s75), and why the answers differ so much. These concepts matter for funding negotiations, for the PPF, for s75 debts and for corporate deals.

## The law

### 1. Scope of Part 3
- Part 3 applies to occupational schemes described in **PA 2004 s221**. Money purchase schemes are outside it, and so are, under Scheme Funding Regs **reg 17(1)(j)**, schemes whose only non-money-purchase benefits are **death benefits secured by insurance policies or annuity contracts** (the SPM scheme point). Collective money purchase benefits now fall within "money purchase benefits" (PSA 2021 s6 amending PSA 1993 s181).
- Each employer must pay contributions under the **schedule of contributions (s227)**. If an employer stops being an employer while others continue to employ active DB members, a **s75 debt** arises (see corporate-transactions-share-and-asset-sales).
- PSA 2021 s123 and Sch 10 added **ss221A-221B** (funding and investment strategy, and a statement of strategy). These are in force from 6 April 2024 for valuation dates on or after 22 September 2024.

### 2. The Part 3 machinery 
1. **s222 statutory funding objective**: the scheme must have sufficient and appropriate assets to cover its **technical provisions** (the amount needed, on an actuarial calculation, to make provision for its liabilities).
2. **s223**: statement of funding principles.
3. **s224**: actuarial valuations at least every 3 years (annual reports in between). The valuation must be completed within **15 months** of its effective date (s224 with reg 7).
4. **s226**: a **recovery plan** if the SFO is not met.
5. **s227**: schedule of contributions certified by the actuary.
6. **s229**: matters requiring **employer agreement** (method and assumptions, recovery plan, schedule), or consultation in some cases.
7. **s231**: TPR's powers if agreement cannot be reached or requirements are not met. TPR can modify future accrual, give directions on method and assumptions, or impose a schedule of contributions (the "tie-breaker"). The Q4 and Q5 ask how this interacts with TPR's **s5(1)(cza)** objective to minimise adverse impact on employers' sustainable growth, and whether that makes assumptions less prudent.
8. TPR's **DB Funding Code** (November 2024 version) is relevant evidence (s90; see pensions-regulator-powers).

### 3. Scheme Funding Regulations 2005, regs 3-5
- **Reg 3**: assets are those in the relevant accounts, **excluding** employer-related investments made in breach of s40, s75 or s228(3) debts unlikely to be recovered without disproportionate cost or within a reasonable time, and (at the actuary's discretion) insurance policy rights. **Liabilities** are members' accrued rights and entitlements in payment, and survivors' rights. Pension credit rights are included. Assets and rights in transit on a transfer are treated as still with the transferring scheme until the receiving scheme has the full consideration.
- **Reg 4**: assets at their value in the relevant accounts, less external liabilities. Assets and liabilities are valued at the same date.
- **Reg 5**: **trustees determine** the method and assumptions (subject to the employer agreement in s229). The method must be an **accrued benefits funding method**. Assumptions must be **prudent**, with an appropriate margin for adverse deviation where applicable. **Discount rates** must be chosen prudently, taking account of the yield on scheme assets and anticipated returns and/or market redemption yields on government or other high-quality bonds. **Mortality** must be based on prudent principles reflecting the membership. **Any change** from the last valuation must be justified by a change of legal, demographic or economic circumstances.
- **IORP II Art 13**: technical provisions must be adequate, calculated annually (or triennially with interim reports), and certified by an actuary using a sufficiently prudent valuation covering benefits in payment and accrued rights. It sets the same prudent discount rate sources (asset yields, high-quality or government bond yields, or a combination), prudent biometric tables, and consistency of method unless circumstances change. **Art 14** requires sufficient and appropriate assets, with a concrete and realisable recovery plan if underfunded. Arts 15-18 do not apply to UK trust schemes because of s75.

### 4. Valuation concepts
1. **Method versus assumptions**: different methods and assumptions give different answers for the same promise.
2. **Methods**:
   - **Projected unit (credit) method**: allows for projected future pay increases for active members up to the assumed exit. This is almost universal for Part 3 and is required for IAS 19.
   - **Current unit method**: assumes all actives left at the valuation date, so there is no salary projection, but statutory revaluation to NPA is still included (PSA 1993 Part IV Chapters II and III).
   - **Buy-out method**: the cost an insurer would charge to take over accrued rights on a leaving-service basis. This is in effect the **s75 debt** basis.
   - **s143 method**: leaving-service basis, capped at **PPF compensation** levels.
3. **Assumption sets**:
   - **accounting** (FRS 102 / IAS 19): best estimate, no prudence margin, discounted at **high-quality corporate bond** yields;
   - **Part 3**: prudent, discounted at gilt or high-quality bond yields, or expected returns on scheme assets, or a mix;
   - **buy-out**: more conservative still;
   - **s143**: broadly like buy-out, but only PPF-covered liabilities.
   - An indicative ranking is of values, with buy-out highest. The CFCF facts illustrate it: assets £100m; ongoing liabilities £110m; PPF basis £120m; buy-out £130m.
4. **Process**: choose the effective ("as at") date; take the market value of assets from the audited statement; choose the method; set the assumptions ("often an iterative process under which the assumptions are reverse engineered from the desired result within the latitude available"); discount the projected payments to present value; compare with assets to find a surplus, deficit or balance.

### 5. Discounting: the "piggy bank" thought experiment
- Jo is 45 at 31 March 2025 with 20 years' service and £30,000 final pay, in a 1/60th scheme with NPA 65 and no increases, revaluation or death benefits. Using the current unit method her pension is 20/60 × £30,000 = **£10,000 a year**, paid annually in advance for 20 instalments from age 65 (she dies just before the 21st).
- Each instalment needs a "piggy bank" today: £A = £10,000 × 1/(1.05)^n, where n runs from 20 to 39 years. At a 5% discount rate, the 20 piggy banks total **£49,317**, against £200,000 of payments. The remaining £150,683 is assumed investment return.
- Arithmetic points: £100 at 5% becomes £105 in a year and £110.25 in two. £100 due in two years is worth £90.70 today. **Rule of 72**: money doubles in about 72 ÷ rate years (14.4 years at 5%, 7.2 at 10%).
- **Sensitivity**: a lower discount rate means a higher present value of liabilities. BA's accounts show a 0.25% fall in the discount rate adding about £369m to the obligation (summary of the BA accounts).
- **Longevity**: life tables (English Life Tables No 17) are backward-looking with trend lines. The future may differ (medical advances, climate effects, war, pandemics). Unisex ("blended") tables are needed where sex differences must be ignored. "An actuary does not have a 'crystal ball'."

### 6. Context
- The Times headline of 29 September 2022, "Bank spends £65 bn to avert pensions collapse". This is the gilt market crisis linked to pension schemes' investment strategies, which shows how far funding and investment interact. The sources give no further detail.
- The PPF levy is in effect an employer cost, because £1 of levy paid from the scheme becomes £1 of extra Part 3 funding.

## Debate and critique
- **Prudence versus affordability**: prudent assumptions protect members and the PPF, but can push employers toward closing DB or toward insolvency. s5(1)(cza) explicitly adds employer growth to TPR's objectives for Part 3. Does that dilute prudence? (the Q5.)
- **Trustee control versus employer agreement**: reg 5 gives trustees the choice of method and assumptions, but s229 requires employer agreement, and TPR breaks deadlocks (s231). That produces negotiation power struggles, including "reverse engineering" assumptions within the permitted latitude.
- **Valuation uncertainty**: the limits of forecasting are stressed, echoing King and Kay's *Radical Uncertainty*. The same promise can show a surplus on one basis and a deficit on another. The USS dispute over a deficit of between £14.9bn and £17.9bn shows how contested valuations can become, including litigation (*McGaughey*) and strikes.
- **Discount rate choice**: asset-based (expected return) discounting lowers liabilities but carries investment risk. Gilt-based discounting is "safer" but costlier. Accounting standards use corporate bond yields, so pension figures in the accounts differ from funding figures.
- **Intergenerational fairness**: IORP II Art 7 refers to an equitable spread of risks and benefits between generations. Recovery plan length shifts costs between current and future employers and members.

## Applying it to a problem question
1. **Is the scheme in Part 3?** DB or hybrid, not exempt (reg 17(1)(j) insured death benefits; money purchase).
2. **Valuation timetable**: effective date; 15-month completion; triennial cycle; interim reports.
3. **Method and assumptions**: who sets them (trustees, reg 5), with employer agreement (s229). Are they prudent, using permitted discount rate sources? Is any change from last time justified?
4. **Outcome**: SFO met? If not, a recovery plan (s226) and schedule (s227). If there is no agreement, TPR (s231) and the Funding Code.
5. **Which basis is relevant to the question?** Funding (Part 3), accounting (IAS 19 / FRS 102), PPF (s143) or s75 (buy-out). Explain why the numbers differ.
6. **Use discounting intuition**: a lower discount rate means higher liabilities; longer life expectancy means higher liabilities; salary projection (projected unit) means higher liabilities than the current unit method.
7. Traps: confusing the accounting deficit with the funding deficit or the s75 debt; forgetting that assets exclude irrecoverable debts and unlawful employer-related investments; treating assumptions as facts.
