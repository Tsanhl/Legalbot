# Powers of amendment and constraints on employer powers

## Overview
Long-lived pension trusts need wide powers of amendment. Pollard (ch 17) gives the reasons: schemes outlast perpetuity limits (PAA 2009 s2(4) exempts them), legislation and tax keep changing, commercial needs shift (for example from DB to DC), and equality law forces equalisation. A power of amendment probably cannot be implied (Pollard, citing the Canadian case *Re Reevie and Montreal Trust Co of Canada*). Each power must be found, construed and exercised validly. The discussion identifies the controls: the express terms and provisos of the power; construction principles (*Barnardo's*); the classification of employer powers (own-interest, Imperial-constrained or fiduciary); the proper purpose rule (*Courage*, *British Airways*); the implied duty of good faith and rationality (*Imperial*, *IBM*, *Braganza*); contractual limits (*South West Trains*; *Tesco v USDAW*); joint "keyholder" powers; and statutory overrides, mainly PA 1995 s67 (see the separate note).

## The law

### 1. Is there a power, and who holds it?
1. Amendment is only possible under a reserved power in the Trust Deed and Rules, or under a limited statutory power such as **PA 1995 s68** and the Occupational Pension Schemes (Modification of Schemes) Regulations 2006. Beneficiaries might also act under *Saunders v Vautier*, a further point to consider.
2. Powers are commonly **joint**: the employer (principal employer) and the trustee are "two keyholders". When the trustee turns its key, all the trustee decision rules apply (see challenging-trustee-decisions). In *British Airways* the power was held by the trustees alone.
3. In *Courage*, a clause saying the committee "shall concur" in executing the employer's deed was read as leaving the committee a **discretion**. "Concur" denotes voluntary agreement, and reading out the discretion would have denied members protection. Validity is tested at the date the last necessary party executes.

### 2. Construction
- **Barnardo's v Buckinghamshire** [2018] UKSC 55 (Lord Hodge; 7 November 2018). The approach to pension scheme documents:
  1. General contract principles apply (*Rainy Sky*, *Arnold v Britton*, *Wood v Capita*).
  2. A pension scheme is a formal document by specialist drafters. It is not the product of commercial negotiation. It confers rights on non-parties (members, often without access to advice or background), and it is long-term. So **textual analysis carries significant weight**, and less weight goes to the factual matrix.
  3. The context is "inherently antipathetic" to private common understandings between employer and trustee that depart from the plain language.
  4. No literalism: give "reasonable and practical effect to the scheme".
  5. Construe "against the fiscal background" (*National Grid v Mayes*). But if the drafter chose wording different from Revenue models, that wording governs.
  6. Obvious drafting mistakes can be corrected by construction (*Chartbrook*).
  - **Held**: "RPI ... or any replacement adopted by the Trustees" did **not** let the trustees switch to CPI while RPI was still published. A replacement must first be made by the official body. Only then may the trustees adopt it.
- Pollard collects earlier principles. *Mettoy* (Warner J) called for a "practical and purposive" rather than "detached and literal" approach, with special background factors (members are not volunteers; Revenue practice; common practice). *Courage* (Millett J): give "reasonable and practical effect". *Stevens v Bell* (British Airways, Arden LJ, 2002): members are not volunteers; test competing constructions against their practical consequences; schemes are "patchworks" read clause by clause at the date of adoption; background includes Revenue practice; read as a whole. Briggs J in *Stena Line* favoured a "broad interpretation" of amendment powers. Pollard disagrees and says the power should be interpreted precisely. Professor Dal Pont criticises "practical and purposive" as adding little.

### 3. Classifying employer powers
1. **Own-interest power, unconstrained**: for example termination of employment on notice, or starting a winding up. The discussion asks whether *Tesco v USDAW* [2024] UKSC 28 "wobbles" on whether the Braganza duty reaches termination on notice.
2. **Own-interest power subject to the implied duty of mutual trust and confidence / good faith** (the Imperial duty).
3. **Fiduciary power**: must be used for its purpose and not in the employer's own interest, for example the surplus power in *Mettoy*.
- Classification is "a matter of construction". A question is whether an employer could draft every power as an unconstrained own-interest power. The discussion cites deed clauses saying the principal company "shall owe no duty" when exercising powers, and ask whether express exclusion of the Imperial and Braganza duties would work.

### 4. The Imperial duty (good faith / trust and confidence)
- *Imperial Group Pension Trust Ltd v Imperial Tobacco Ltd* [1991] 1 WLR 589 (Browne-Wilkinson V-C).
  - **Facts**: after Hanson's takeover, the company would consent to benefit increases above the 5% RPI-linked guarantee (r64A) only in its new open scheme (the RBS). That scheme offered guaranteed increases (the lesser of 15% and RPI) and returned any ultimate surplus to the company. Members had to transfer to it to get them.
  - **Held**: the employer's powers under the deed are subject to the implied obligation of good faith, the employment law duty not, without reasonable and proper cause, to act in a way calculated or likely to destroy or seriously damage trust and confidence (*Woods v WM Car Services*). The test is **not reasonableness**. The company may look after its own financial interests. But it must use its rights (a) with a view to the efficient running of the scheme and (b) **not for the collateral purpose** of forcing members to give up accrued rights, including their rights in the surplus. A **blanket refusal** ever to consider amendments that have been accepted for 20 years would breach the duty. Each proposal must be considered when made.
- *IBM United Kingdom Holdings Ltd v Dalgleish* [2017] EWCA Civ 1212 (CA; on appeal from Warren J [2014] EWHC 980 (Ch)). "Project Waltz" (2009) closed DB accrual, removed early retirement enhancements, and required non-pensionability agreements for future pay rises. Earlier "Ocean" and "Soto" communications had created "Reasonable Expectations".
  - Warren J found Holdings in breach of its Imperial duty and UKL in breach of the contractual duty of trust and confidence. He treated the expectations as constraining IBM unless it had no other course.
  - **CA**: the test for both duties is **Wednesbury irrationality or perversity, including both limbs**, applied in the employment context. Reasonable Expectations are a **relevant factor**, not an overriding one. Giving them paramount weight was an error of law. The employer may look after its own interests. Meeting the group earnings-per-share roadmap gave a rational reason. The trial judge also wrongly treated the IBM group as one entity, ignoring separate personality.
  - Further: at first instance, failure to comply with statutory consultation obligations (PA 2004 ss259-261) was held to breach the implied contractual duty of good faith. That was not challenged on appeal. The members had not argued that it also breached the Imperial duty and were not allowed to raise this in the CA. The commentary notes a "perhaps over fine" distinction between the contractual good faith duty and the trust-deed Imperial duty.
- *Braganza v BP Shipping* [2015] UKSC 17 supplies the rationality standard for contractual discretions in relational contracts (see challenging-trustee-decisions).

### 5. Proper purpose
- *Re Courage Group's Pension Schemes* [1987] 1 WLR 495 (Millett J, 10 December 1986). Hanson bought Imperial and was selling Courage (I.B.L.) to Elders. It proposed amendments substituting itself as principal company so that it could keep about £70m of the £80m surplus.
  - **Held** ultra vires and for a foreign purpose. A power "can be exercised only for the purpose for which it is conferred". The amending power exists to **promote** the scheme's purposes, not to alter them. A substitute principal employer must be recognisably the successor to the business and workforce, and substitution must be necessary or expedient to preserve the scheme.
  - Obiter: members have no legal right to surplus, but are entitled to have it dealt with by negotiation between employers with continuing responsibility and a committee with discretion, and not lose it to "a take-over raider with only a transitory interest". Millett J also cautioned against unduly fettering amendment powers (Pollard 17.4).
- *British Airways v Airways Pension Scheme Trustee* [2018] EWCA Civ 1533 (CA, 5 July 2018). The APS's main object was to provide pensions on retirement, with a subsidiary object of injury and death benefits, and clause 2 said it was "not in any sense a benevolent scheme". Clause 18 let trustees amend "in any way", subject to provisos including not changing the purposes. In 2011 the trustees confirmed a decision to take a power to grant discretionary pension increases. The CA concluded that the power of amendment was being **exercised for an improper purpose**.
- *Mettoy Pension Trustees Ltd v Evans* [1990] 1 WLR 1587: the employer's discretion to use surplus on winding up was fiduciary, so a liquidator could not exercise it for creditors. The court would decide how to exercise it.

### 6. Express restrictions in amendment powers
Different wording produces different protection. Pollard 17.7 sets out the sequence of validity requirements: employer implied duties; trustee duties; proper purpose; formalities (for example by deed); express restrictions; statutory limits. The **effect of failure varies**:
- breach of a mandatory formality: void;
- a past-service restriction breached: the amendment may still be effective for the future but invalid for the past;
- failure to notify: no effect on validity;
- breach of s67: voidable at TPR's discretion under the pre-2006 wording (Pollard) and now under s67G.
Examples from the reading: Courage's third proviso (not to vary benefits "already secured by past contributions"); the Lloyds Bank 1996 clause 9 (consent thresholds; no alteration making the main purpose cease to be pensions); BBC r19.2(3) ("interests" of active members). These are developed in changing-future-accrual-and-consultation.

### 7. Contractual restrictions
The power holder may be limited by contract with members: retained-pay promises (*Tesco v USDAW*), collective agreements, or earlier communications. Heavy due diligence is essential on employment contracts, booklets and communications before relying on any power.

## Key authorities
- *Barnardo's v Buckinghamshire* [2018] UKSC 55: construction; no CPI switch while RPI is still published.
- *Re Courage Group's Pension Schemes* [1987] 1 WLR 495: proper purpose; substitution of principal employer; surplus.
- *Imperial Group Pension Trust v Imperial Tobacco* [1991] 1 WLR 589: the implied duty of good faith on employer powers; collateral purpose; no blanket refusal.
- *IBM UK Holdings v Dalgleish* [2017] EWCA Civ 1212: rationality test; Reasonable Expectations only a relevant factor; corporate separateness.
- *Mettoy v Evans* [1990] 1 WLR 1587: fiduciary employer power; construction.
- *British Airways v APS Trustee* [2018] EWCA Civ 1533: improper purpose in a trustee amendment.

## Debate and critique
- **Broad or precise reading of amendment powers?** Briggs J (*Stena Line*) favoured broad. Pollard insists on precise. *Barnardo's* emphasises text over matrix, which suits members, who cannot know the drafting background, but may block sensible change (the CPI switch). The CA dissent in *Barnardo's* favoured flexibility, and the SC rejected the use of hindsight about RPI's fall from favour.
- **How strong is the Imperial duty?** After *IBM* (CA) the duty is a low-intensity rationality review that allows employer self-interest. Critics, including "high bar" framing, say members' expectations built through years of communications get little protection. Supporters stress that judges should not substitute their business judgment. The "over fine" distinction between contractual good faith and the Imperial duty matters procedurally, as the IBM members found.
- **Can drafting exclude the duties?** If classification is construction, express "no duty" clauses may work. But the Imperial duty comes from the employment relationship, and there are policy objections to letting the stronger party write out good faith.
- **Purpose analysis and surplus**: *Courage* and *Imperial* came out of the 1980s takeover battles for surplus. With many DB schemes again in surplus and statutory surplus-release reforms in progress (mentioned in the summaries), purpose analysis is newly relevant.
- **Trustee-held amendment powers**: *BA v APST* shows trustees, not just employers, can misuse amendment powers, here by pursuing what the scheme's own purposes ruled out.

## Applying it to a problem question
1. **Find the operative power** at the proposed effective date: who holds it (employer, trustee or both), what form it requires (deed), and any consents or actuarial certificates.
2. **Construe the provisos** using *Barnardo's*: text first, the whole document, the fiscal background, no literalism. Distinguish "accrued rights", "benefits secured by past contributions", "interests", "pecuniary benefits", "main purpose".
3. **Classify each employer power** (own-interest, Imperial-constrained or fiduciary) and apply the matching standard. For Imperial: is there a collateral purpose (for example coercing transfers or capturing surplus)? A blanket refusal? Irrationality on both limbs? Expectations weighed as a factor?
4. **Trustee consent**: is it genuine? Proper purpose, relevant considerations, conflicts.
5. **Proper purpose**: does the amendment promote the scheme's purposes (*Courage*; *BA*)? Is a principal employer substitution justified by succession to the business and workforce?
6. **Contract**: do employment contracts, collective agreements or communications restrict the power? Have members agreed a variation?
7. **Statute**: apply s67 to subsisting rights only after the power is valid (validity first, s67 second). Also consider contracted-out rights (PSA 1993 s37, ignored) and consultation (ss259-261).
8. **Effect**: void, partly effective, or voidable?
9. The exercises: NAPS clause 16 (amendment) and clauses 6(a), 6(d) and 6A (appointing and removing employer-representative trustees). Which category is each? The Everlasting Life longevity-adjusted NRD power: what type of employer power is a unilateral power exercised by deed addressed to the trustee?
