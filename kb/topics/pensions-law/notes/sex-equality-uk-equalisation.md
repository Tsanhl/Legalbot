# Sex equality in UK pension schemes - Equality Act 2010, the Barber window and Safeway v Newton

## Overview
UK law gives effect to the EU equal pay principle in pensions through an implied **sex equality rule**. This was originally the "equal treatment rule" in PA 1995 s62 (in force 1 January 1996) and is now in the **Equality Act 2010 ss61-71 and Sch 7 Pt 2**, with exceptions in the **Sex Equality Rule (Exceptions) Regulations 2010**. The practical problem for schemes was **equalising normal pension ages (NPAs)** that had been 60 for women and 65 for men. The "Barber window" runs from 17 May 1990 until equalisation became legally effective. Service in that window must be levelled up. **Safeway v Newton** (CA 2017, CJEU 2019, CA 2020) decided that a scheme cannot equalise retroactively to the date of an announcement, and that PA 1995 s62 closed the window on 1 January 1996. The Travis/Taylor problem tests all of this. The Human Rights Act 1998 (*Brewster*, *RR*) is a further route for discrimination challenges.

## The law

### 1. Equality Act 2010 framework
1. **s13**: direct discrimination, meaning less favourable treatment because of a protected characteristic. **s39**: employees.
2. **s61-62**: non-discrimination rule for occupational pension schemes and non-discrimination alterations (other protected characteristics).
3. **s67, sex equality rule**: if an OPS lacks one, it is **treated as including one**. A **relevant term** (on which people become members, or on which members, including as regards dependants, are treated) that is less favourable to A than to a comparator B of the opposite sex is **modified so as not to be less favourable**. A **relevant discretion** capable of being exercised less favourably is modified to prevent that. Special provisions apply to GMPs (s67(2A)-(2B)). Comparators must be in the same marital or family position.
   - **Temporal limits**: no effect on terms of *membership* for pensionable service **before 8 April 1976**, or on terms of *treatment* for pensionable service **before 17 May 1990**.
4. **s68**: if trustees lack power to make **sex equality alterations**, or the procedure would be unduly complex or protracted or needs consents that cannot readily be obtained, they may make them **by resolution**. Such alterations **may be retrospective**.
5. **s69, material factor defence**: the trustees show the difference is because of a material factor that is not the difference of sex. For indirect disadvantage, the factor must be a proportionate means of achieving a legitimate aim.
6. **Sch 7 Pt 2 exceptions**:
   - para 4: State retirement pension differences (bridging pensions; indexation in salary-related contracted-out schemes; Exceptions Regs regs 2-3);
   - para 5: **actuarial factors**. Under Exceptions Regs reg 4, factors that differ for men and women because of differences in average life expectancy, set with a view to providing equal periodical pensions, are permitted for: a lump sum consisting of a **commuted** periodical pension; a pension granted in exchange for a lump sum; a pension derived from money purchase benefits; and a pension derived from cash balance benefits.
7. The Act's coverage of the Framework and Equal Treatment Directives is "far from straightforward", with overlapping provisions.

### 2. Historic statutory route
- **PA 1995 s62** (from 1 January 1996): every OPS is treated as including an **equal treatment rule**. Where a woman is employed on like work or work of equal value with a man and a scheme term is or becomes less favourable to her, it is treated as modified so as not to be less favourable, and vice versa (via s63(4), construed with the Equal Pay Act). These rules are now in the Equality Act 2010.
- **PA 1995 s65(3)**: a power to amend schemes easily to bring the paperwork into line with equal treatment (discussed in *Safeway* 2020).
- **Normal pension age** for PSA 1993 purposes: s180 (see pension-rights-key-concepts). State pensionable age is in PA 1995 Sch 4.

### 3. The three periods (Coloroll as applied in the UK)
1. **Service before 17 May 1990**: scheme rules apply, including different NPAs (no EU obligation; Equality Act s67 temporal limit).
2. **Service from 17 May 1990 until the "legally effective measure" equalising benefits**: **levelling up**. Men get the more favourable treatment, for example NPA 60 for that service.
3. **Service after the effective measure**: equality may be achieved by **levelling down**, for example NPA 65 for both.

### 4. Safeway Ltd v Newton
- **Facts**: Safeway announced in 1991 that NPAs would be equalised at 65 from **1 December 1991**. The formal amendment was a deed of **2 May 1996**, expressed to be retrospective. The amendment power required a deed but allowed retrospective changes.
- **High Court** (Warren J, 29 February 2016): equalisation took effect only from the deed, 2 May 1996. Until then the NPA was 60 for both sexes.
- **CA [2017] EWCA Civ 1482** (5 October 2017; Lord Briggs, Longmore and Floyd LJJ): the power had to be exercised **by deed**, so the announcement alone did not amend the scheme. The CA **referred to the CJEU** whether EU law allowed retrospective levelling down to the announcement date where domestic law and the deed permitted it.
- **AG Opinion** (28 March 2018), then **CJEU C-171/18** (Grand Chamber, 7 October 2019): Art 119 EC **precludes**, absent objective justification, a scheme from adopting a measure that equalises NPA **retroactively** at the level of the previously disadvantaged category for the period between the announcement and the adoption, **even where national law and the trust deed allow it**. The summary adds that only an overriding reason in the public interest, such as a proven risk of seriously undermining the scheme's financial balance, could justify it, and the threshold is very high.
- **CA [2020] EWCA Civ 869** (14 July 2020; Floyd LJ, Arnold LJ agreeing): the Barber window closes when domestic law gives members **legally enforceable and certain rights**. **PA 1995 s62 did that from 1 January 1996.** It gave a full, immediate and unconditional right, and the "like work" requirement is no more than Art 119 itself requires. So the window closed on **1 January 1996**, and the 2 May 1996 deed could level down for service from that date (in effect, from 1 January 1996 onwards). *Appeal allowed* on that issue.

### 5. Other UK cases referred to
- **Lloyds Banking Group Pensions Trustees v Lloyds Bank plc** [2020] EWHC 3135 (Ch) (GMP equalisation and past transfers, Morgan J). According to the summary: trustees must top up past CETVs that were underpaid because GMPs were not equalised for 1990-1997 service; claims are not time-barred (Limitation Act 1980 s21(1)(b)); top-ups carry interest at 1% above base rate. The summary's quotations are not verified against the judgment text in raw/case_law, so check before quoting.
- **Brewster** [2017] UKSC 8 (Northern Ireland local government scheme): an unmarried survivor had a "legitimate expectation" amounting to a "possession". A requirement that cohabitants (not spouses) lodge a nomination form was a disproportionate, unjustified interference, contrary to ECHR Art 14 with A1P1 under the HRA. Strictly, this was judicial review of a requirement in public scheme regulations.
- **RR v SSWP** [2019] UKSC 52 (bedroom tax): courts may **disapply secondary legislation** incompatible with the HRA 1998. This developed the *Brewster* line.
- **HRA 1998** s3 (interpret compatibly where possible), s4 (declaration of incompatibility), Sch 1 Pt I Art 14, and Sch 1 Pt II Art 1 (A1P1).

### 6. The problem (Travis and Taylor), worked framework
Facts: both joined on 17 May 1986, aged 20, in a 1/60th final salary scheme (not contracted out) with NPA 60 for women and 65 for men. A deed of 17 May 1996 equalised NPA at 65 retrospectively from 17 May 1994 (an announced date) under a unilateral employer power permitting retrospective amendment. Both retire at 60 on 17 May 2026 with final pay of £60,000 and 40 years' service. The early retirement reduction is 5% per year early.
1. **Tranches**:
   - 17 May 1986 to 16 May 1990 (4 years): pre-Barber, so Taylor's NPA is 60 and Travis's is 65.
   - 17 May 1990 to 31 December 1995 (Barber window): levelled up to NPA 60 for both.
   - From 1 January 1996 (s62, per *Safeway* 2020): levelling down to 65 is permitted, and the 1996 deed achieves that from its effective date for future service.
   - The purported retrospective equalisation to 17 May 1994 **is ineffective for 17 May 1994 to 31 December 1995** (*Safeway* CJEU).
2. **Q1 calculation approach**: each tranche's pension is 1/60 × £60,000 × years in tranche. Reduce early-payment tranches with NPA 65 by 25% (5 years early), and leave tranches with NPA 60 unreduced. Taylor's pre-1990 slice is unreduced, while Travis's pre-1990 slice is reduced. Window slices are unreduced for both. Post-1996 slices are reduced for both. (The legal analysis is supplied here; the arithmetic is left to the reader.)
3. **Q3 (deed executed 17 May 1998)**: the window still closed on 1 January 1996 by s62, so the analysis is the same unless the deed's own timing affects the levelling down of post-1996 service (consider when the deed took effect for that service).
4. **Q4 (deed in 2008)**: in addition, consider PA 1995 s67, which applies to modifications after 5 April 1997. Retrospectively raising NPA for service already accrued after 1996 would adversely affect subsisting rights.
5. **Q2 (employer consent not assumed)**: for tranches with NPA 60, the member may retire without consent. Elsewhere, an early retirement consent is a discretion subject to the sex equality rule (s67(2)(b)) and to the Imperial duty, which the question tells you to set aside.
6. **Q5 (commutation factors 18:1 for women, 16:1 for men)**: prima facie a less favourable term because of sex, but Sch 7 para 5 and Exceptions Regs reg 4 permit sex-differing actuarial factors for commuting pension into a lump sum, consistent with *Coloroll* ruling 10 and Directive 2006/54 Art 9(1). Counter-arguments: *Lindorfer*, *Test-Achats* reasoning, the Charter (interpretive only after Brexit), and whether Art 157 can still be relied on for events after 31 December 2023 (no, under the REUL Act 2023).

## Debate and critique
- **EU supremacy over trust deeds**: the CJEU in *Safeway* put the effectiveness of EU law above a valid retrospective amendment power. This conflicts with the domestic idea that "defeasible" rights can be amended. *Safeway* shows the hierarchy of legal rules in action.
- **When does the window close?** The 2020 CA put closure at s62 (1 January 1996), not each scheme's formal amendment. That is good for schemes and certainty, but means members lose the benefit of trustee and employer delay in amending the paperwork. Mr Newton's policy argument that textual modification should be encouraged was rejected.
- **Equality versus generosity**: levelling down is lawful after the window. Equal treatment law therefore produced widespread *reductions* for women, raising NPA to 65, rather than improvements for men.
- **Actuarial factors**: the UK's exceptions for sex-differing commutation factors rely on *Coloroll*. Post-*Test-Achats* thinking and Charter Art 23 suggest these may be vulnerable, but after Brexit there is a much weaker route to challenge them.
- **Human rights as the new route**: with EU-based claims shrinking, *Brewster* and *RR* show the HRA (A1P1 with Art 14) as an alternative, though it mainly bites on public schemes and legislation.
- **GMP equalisation**: the Lloyds litigation shows how decades-old inequality, through contracted-out GMPs, still creates large administrative and financial burdens today.

## Applying it to a problem question
1. **Facts**: joining dates, scheme type (contracted-out or not), the NPA for each sex, the dates of announcement and deed, and the terms of the amendment power (form, retrospectivity, consent).
2. **Validity of the amendment** under domestic law (form such as a deed: *Safeway* 2017; power holder; purpose).
3. **Split service** into pre-17 May 1990 / Barber window / post-1 January 1996, unless a scheme-specific effective measure closed the window earlier. Apply levelling up in the window and allow levelling down afterwards.
4. **Retrospective equalisation** back to an announcement date: ineffective for the window period (*Safeway* CJEU), absent overriding public interest justification.
5. **s67 PA 1995** for modifications after 5 April 1997 affecting subsisting rights.
6. **Other differences**: early retirement discretions (sex equality rule on discretions), survivors' benefits (comparator rules; temporal limits), GMPs (s67(2A)), actuarial factors (Sch 7 para 5; Exceptions Regs reg 4), bridging pensions (reg 2).
7. **Defences**: material factor (s69); statutory exceptions.
8. **Remedies and route**: Equality Act claim (employment tribunal, court or Ombudsman); Art 157 claims only for events before the end of 2023; HRA for public schemes or legislation.
9. Traps: treating the announcement date as effective; forgetting that the window closed on 1 January 1996 by statute; overlooking reg 4's commutation exception; ignoring tranches when calculating early retirement reductions.
