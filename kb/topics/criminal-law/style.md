# Criminal Law: corrections from the student's marked work
- Problem answers: no introduction or conclusion; go straight to the offences, party by party; in-text citations, not
  footnotes; no "I/we"; do not hide behind the jury; avoid "likely", "clearly".
- Pick the right offence and analyse it fully rather than surveying many offences superficially.
- Address or rule out **every** element: e.g. direct vs oblique intention; *Woollin*'s two-part test (virtual certainty
  objectively, and D's appreciation); GBH requires "really serious harm"; s 18 needs intent to cause GBH.
- Assault requires V to apprehend immediate unlawful violence: if V did not see it, there is no assault.
- Argue the level of harm with case law and the CPS charging standards (is a broken nose ABH or GBH?).
- Spot **transferred malice** (*Latimer*): same offence only.
- Consent: valid for assault/battery if express or implied and effective; for ABH and above only within a recognised
  exception (*Brown*); sport: *Barnes* factors (type and level of sport, nature of act, degree of force, risk, state of
  mind). Surgery: D intends to wound, so s 18 is in play; the question is consent (*Tabassum* on deception as to nature
  or quality).
- Theft: AR and MR with dishonesty per *Ivey*; consider duress even if it fails.
- Murder/LOSC: apply the facts clearly to each element.
- **Out of syllabus** (do not discuss): inchoate offences; secondary/accessorial liability.
- Fitness to plead essays: discuss current and proposed law and medical advances.
