# R v Smith (Thomas Joseph) [1959] 2 QB 35 (case note)

> Case note written from two consistent secondary accounts; the judgment itself is not in the knowledge base.
> Check pinpoints against the report (also [1959] 2 All ER 193) before citing them.

## Overview
Court of Appeal sitting as the Courts-Martial Appeal Court; Lord Parker CJ gave the judgment. Confirms that medical
negligence and other later events do not break the chain of causation while the original wound remains an
**operating and substantial cause** of death.

## Facts
During a fight at an army barracks, Smith stabbed a fellow soldier. On the way to the medical centre the victim was
dropped twice. The medical officer failed to detect a punctured lung and the treatment given was described as
"palpably wrong"; evidence suggested proper treatment might have saved him. The victim died and Smith was convicted of
murder.

## Held
- Conviction **upheld**: neither the dropping nor the poor treatment broke the chain.
- If at the time of death the original wound is still an operating and substantial cause, death can be attributed to
  it even though another cause also operates. It is superseded only where a later cause is so overwhelming that the
  wound becomes merely part of the history.

## Why it matters
- Core authority for the **operating and substantial cause** test and for the point that medical negligence does not
  readily break the chain. See *R v Cheshire* [1991] 1 WLR 844 for the later formulation.

## Applying it to a problem question
1. Was the defendant's wound still operating at the time of death?
2. Was the later event (treatment, accident, delay) so overwhelming that the wound is just background?
3. Ordinary negligence in treatment will not usually suffice; only palpably abnormal treatment will.
