# R v Savage; DPP v Parmenter [1992] 1 AC 699 (case note)

> Case note from a solicitor-written summary, checked against *Mowatt* and *Cunningham* (both in the knowledge base).
> The House of Lords judgment is not held; check page pinpoints against the report before citing them.
> An AI-generated "summary" of this case found online gave invented facts and was deliberately excluded.

## Overview
House of Lords, conjoined appeals; leading speech of **Lord Ackner**. Settles the **mens rea of s 20 and s 47 OAPA
1861**.

## Facts
- **Savage:** intending to throw beer over her ex-boyfriend's new partner, she threw the contents of her glass; the
  victim was injured. She was charged under s 20; the issue on appeal was the mental element for s 47.
- **Parmenter:** injured his baby son by handling him roughly while playing, saying he did not realise this would
  cause injury. He was convicted under s 20.

## Held
1. **Section 20 ("maliciously")** requires that the defendant **intended or actually foresaw that some physical harm**
   might result, **subjective (Cunningham) recklessness**. The harm foreseen need only be of a minor character; it need
   **not be wounding or grievous bodily harm**. This approves *R v Mowatt* [1968] 1 QB 421.
2. **Section 47** requires **only the mens rea of assault or battery**: no intention or foresight of any bodily harm
   is needed for the "occasioning ABH" element.
3. Results: Savage's appeal was dismissed (s 47 conviction stands); Parmenter's appeal was allowed: without foresight of
   some harm s 20 fails, and a **s 47 conviction was substituted**.

## Why it matters
- The constructive structure of s 47 (no mens rea for the extra harm) and the "some harm" rule for s 20 are criticised
  for the mismatch between fault and harm (correspondence principle); the Law Commission's reform proposals target this.
- Confirms that recklessness in the OAPA is **subjective** (Cunningham), unlike the objective recklessness then applied
  in criminal damage.

## Applying it to a problem question
- s 47: battery or assault (intent or subjective recklessness as to force or apprehension) + ABH caused. No need to prove
  D foresaw the injury.
- s 20: wound or GBH inflicted + D foresaw **some** harm (not necessarily serious). If D foresaw no harm at all, fall
  back to s 47 (as in Parmenter).
