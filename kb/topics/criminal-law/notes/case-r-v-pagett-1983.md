# R v Pagett (1983) 76 Cr App R 279 (case note)

> Case note written from two consistent secondary accounts; the judgment itself is not in the knowledge base.
> Check pinpoints against the report before citing them.

## Overview
Court of Appeal (Criminal Division), 1983. A defendant's act can remain a cause of death even where a third party's
intervening act is the immediate cause, if the intervention is a **reasonable act of self-preservation or in
performance of a legal duty**.

## Facts
Pagett shot at armed police who came to arrest him and used his pregnant teenage girlfriend as a human shield. The
police returned fire and she was killed by a police bullet. He was acquitted of murder and convicted of manslaughter (among other offences) and appealed on causation.

## Held
- Appeal **dismissed**. The police officers acted in self-defence in returning fire, and Pagett's use of the girl as a
  shield caused her death.
- A reasonable act of self-preservation, or an act done in the execution of a legal duty, does not break the chain of
  causation (*novus actus interveniens*).
- In homicide cases a simple direction that the defendant's act need not be the sole cause of death is usually
  enough. Pagett's two acts, firing at the police and using the girl as a shield, were dangerous acts that
  significantly contributed to her death.

## Why it matters
- Establishes that the foreseeable, reasonable reaction of others (including police) to the defendant's wrongful
  conduct does not exonerate the defendant.
- Example of a third party's act that is not "free, deliberate and informed" (not voluntary in the relevant sense),
  so the chain stays intact.

## Applying it to a problem question
1. Was the intervening act a reasonable response to the danger D created (self-defence, rescue, duty)?
2. If so, D remains a cause of the result.
3. Also check that the original act was an unlawful and dangerous one capable of founding manslaughter.
