# Copyright subsistence - works, originality, fixation, duration

## Overview
Copyright is automatic: it subsists in an eligible category of work, if the work is original and (for literary, dramatic and musical works) recorded, and if it qualifies by connection to the UK or a convention country. UK copyright law is a closed list of categories, but the originality standard is the EU-derived test, whether the work is the "author's own intellectual creation", which the Court of Appeal applied in *THJ Systems v Sheridan* [2023] EWCA Civ 1354. Ideas, facts and information are not protected, only their expression. Duration is generally life plus 70 years.

## The law
**Categories (CDPA 1988 s 1(1)).** Copyright subsists in: (a) original literary, dramatic, musical or artistic works; (b) sound recordings, films and broadcasts; (c) the typographical arrangement of published editions. Section 3: literary works include tables and compilations (other than databases), computer programs, preparatory design material for a computer program, and databases; "original" databases are literary works if they are the author's own intellectual creation by reason of the selection or arrangement of the contents (s 3A). Dramatic works include works of dance or mime; musical works exclude words or action intended to be sung, spoken or performed with the music. Artistic works (s 4) include graphic works, photographs, sculptures and collages irrespective of artistic quality, works of architecture, and works of artistic craftsmanship. There is no general category for "any original creation": a work that falls outside the list is unprotected by copyright (see *Lucasfilm* and *Nova v Mazooma* below on the boundaries). Names, titles and slogans are usually too short to qualify as literary works.

**Originality.** Under the older domestic approach, a work was original if it originated from the author and was the result of their skill, labour and judgment (for example *Ladbroke (Football) v William Hill* [1964] 1 WLR 273 (HL)). The EU harmonised test for some works and then for all works is that a work is original if it is the author's own intellectual creation, reflecting their free and creative choices and stamping the work with their personal touch: *Infopaq International A/S v Danske Dagblades Forening* (C-5/08) [2009] ECR I-6569 (extending the test to literary works generally; the Court held that an extract of 11 consecutive words could be protected if it expressed the author's intellectual creation, and left it to the national court to decide), *Football Dataco v Yahoo! UK* (C-604/10) (the test applies to databases: creativity in selection or arrangement, not mere labour or skill in data compilation), *Painer* (C-145/10) (portrait photograph: free and creative choices such as lighting, framing, angle). In *THJ Systems Ltd v Sheridan* [2023] EWCA Civ 1354 (Court of Appeal, Arnold LJ giving the leading judgment), the court held that the deputy judge had applied the wrong test ("skill and labour"); the correct test is the author's own intellectual creation, which is more demanding. The test is objective; there is no requirement of artistic merit (s 4(1)(a): "irrespective of artistic quality"); the burden is on the claimant; and the functionality of the software is irrelevant to the originality of its visual output. Applying that test, the risk and price charts displayed by the claimant's software were original graphic works, because their author had made choices about layout, the arrangement of components, fonts and colours. The degree of creativity was low, so the scope of protection is correspondingly narrow and only a close copy would infringe. Authorship under s 9(3) was not in issue on appeal (it was common ground that, if the charts were original, the software's author was their author), so the case does not decide how s 9(3) works; see the AI note.

**Fixation.** Literary, dramatic and musical works: copyright does not subsist until the work is recorded, in writing or otherwise, by or with the authority of the author (s 3(2)); it is irrelevant whether the recording is the author's, and the work can be recorded after creation. Artistic works, sound recordings, films and broadcasts need no fixation beyond their nature (a broadcast is by nature transient). *Levola Hengelo v Smilde Foods* (C-310/17) (a food flavour is not a "work") requires that a work be identifiable with sufficient precision and objectivity.

**Authorship categories and qualifying works.** Copyright subsists in a work only if it qualifies by reference to author (British citizen, domiciled or resident in the UK or a Berne/WTO country at the time) or country of first publication (ss 153-156). In practice nearly all works qualify.

**Duration.**
- Literary, dramatic, musical and artistic works: life of the author plus 70 years from the end of the calendar year of death (s 12(2)); if authorship is unknown, 70 years from the end of the calendar year of creation, or of first making available to the public if that happens in the period (s 12(3)); joint authorship: death of last known author (s 12(8)).
- Computer-generated works: 50 years from the end of the calendar year in which the work was made (s 12(7)).
- Sound recordings: s 13A: 50 years from the end of the calendar year of making, or, if the recording is published (or, failing publication, communicated to the public) within that period, 70 years from the end of the calendar year of that first event.
- Films: s 13B: 70 years from the end of the calendar year of death of the last of the principal director, screenplay author, dialogue author and composer of the music created for the film.
- Typographical arrangement of published editions: 25 years from the end of the calendar year of first publication (s 15).
- Broadcasts: 50 years from the end of the calendar year in which the broadcast was made (s 14(2)); a repeat broadcast gets no fresh term (s 14(5)).
Copyright runs to the end of the calendar year.

**Photographs of existing works.** A photograph is an artistic work (s 4(1)(a)) and may be original on the author's choices of lighting, angle, framing, exposure and composition (*Painer*; *Temple Island Collections v New English Teas* [2012] EWPCC 1, where a deliberately composed London photograph with a red bus was held original and infringed). Whether a faithful photograph of a two-dimensional artwork, or other mechanical copy of a work in the public domain, is original is disputed: in *Interlego AG v Tyco Industries* [1989] AC 217 (PC) Lord Oliver said that to qualify as an original artistic work a copy must have some element of material alteration or embellishment, not merely copying skill and labour, and the copying of technical drawings with modifications made without visual significance did not make them original; for photographs of paintings the US case *Bridgeman Art Library v Corel* (SDNY 1999) found no originality, and the English view has often been that a slavish photographic reproduction of a 2D work is unlikely to be original, though the intellectual-creation standard leaves a space for creative choices in lighting or photography of 3D objects. *Painer* suggests that creative choices matter even in realistic photographs. No appellate court in England and Wales has decided whether a faithful photograph of a 2D artwork meets the intellectual-creation test, so the point remains open.

## Key authorities
- *Infopaq International v Danske Dagblades Forening* (C-5/08) [2009] ECR I-6569 (CJEU): intellectual creation test for literary works; 11 words may be protected.
- *Football Dataco v Yahoo! UK* (C-604/10) (CJEU, 2012): original databases; the creative stage.
- *THJ Systems Ltd v Sheridan* [2023] EWCA Civ 1354 (Court of Appeal): UK originality test is the author's own intellectual creation, not skill and labour; no artistic merit requirement; low creativity gives narrow protection.
- *Nova Productions Ltd v Mazooma Games Ltd* [2007] EWCA Civ 219 (CA): each screen frame of a video game is a graphic work, but a series of frames is not itself a graphic work; the defendants took only general ideas, not a substantial part, so the claim failed; the player is not an author of the frames (see the AI note).
- *Lucasfilm Ltd v Ainsworth* [2011] UKSC 39 (Supreme Court): Stormtrooper helmet: not a sculpture under s 4 (primarily utilitarian in a prop for film; a work of sculpture needs an artistic purpose); no copyright in the helmets; the court confirmed that a US copyright could be enforced in the UK.
- *Designers Guild v Russell Williams* [2000] 1 WLR 2416 (HL): existing case note, on substantial part and idea/expression.
- *Interlego AG v Tyco* [1989] AC 217 (PC): original requires more than copying skill.
- *Temple Island Collections v New English Teas* [2012] EWPCC 1 (PCC): original photograph; infringement of composition.
- *Levola Hengelo v Smilde Foods* (C-310/17) (CJEU): a flavour is not a "work": a work must be identifiable with sufficient precision and objectivity.

## Applying it
Steps: (1) Which category is the work (s 1)? If none, no copyright (consider design right or confidence). (2) Is it original (author's own intellectual creation)? Look for free and creative choices (selection, arrangement, expression): not merely routine technical choices dictated by function or rules (*Football Dataco*), and not simply labour. (3) Is it fixed (literary, dramatic, musical)? (4) Who is the author and does it qualify? (5) Is the copyright still in term? (6) If a case turns on idea versus expression, reference *Designers Guild* (existing note). For photographs of artworks, ask what creative choices the photographer made. Common errors: stating that copyright protects ideas; applying "skill and labour" in a modern answer; forgetting that short phrases and titles are rarely protected; ignoring the closed-list problem. Everyday: "Copyright arises automatically when you create an original work and fix it; you don't register it. It lasts 70 years after death of the author for most creative works."

## Debates and reform
- Whether the UK should return to the domestic "skill, labour and judgment" standard post-Brexit, now that retained EU case law can be departed from by the higher courts. The argument that the EU standard raises the bar is set against the argument that it brings clarity.
- The closed list of works (versus open fair-use style protection), and whether new forms (video-game output, user interfaces, flavours, AI output) fit.
- Copyright in photographs of public domain art: lack of clarity, and the digital heritage sector; the EU Directive 2019/790 art 14 provides that reproductions of public domain visual art are not original (not implemented in the UK).
- Duration: the length of life plus 70.

## Recent changes and points to check
- No statutory change to subsistence was made in 2024-26. The Government's March 2026 report on copyright and AI proposed removing protection for wholly computer-generated works (s 9(3)), but no legislation had been introduced by November 2026; see the AI note.
- Since 1 January 2024 pre-2021 CJEU case law is "assimilated case law" which the Court of Appeal and Supreme Court may depart from (Retained EU Law (Revocation and Reform) Act 2023). No appellate decision departing from the intellectual-creation test was identified as at November 2026.
