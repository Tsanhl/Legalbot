# Constitutional Law: answer rules

## Learned from testing
- When relying on a judicial statement, say whether it is ratio or obiter and how that affects its weight. If the materials do not establish which it is, say so rather than guessing. [2026-11-24 · ref 2026-11-22-1301/q13]
- Ouster clauses and parliamentary sovereignty: cover both (a) how the courts construe the clause and (b) whether they would give effect to a clause that is clearly drafted and fully effective on its construction (the rule-of-law limits debate). Say what material is missing for either part. [2026-11-24 · ref 2026-11-24-1510/q13]
- Human rights: where the facts engage a qualified Convention right (Arts 8–11 ECHR), apply the full test to the client's facts: interference; in accordance with / prescribed by law (accessibility and foreseeability, including how precise the statutory or other legal wording is); a legitimate aim listed in the Article's second paragraph; and necessity in a democratic society, using the four-stage proportionality test in Bank Mellat v HM Treasury (No 2) [2013] UKSC 39. In strict / limited-material modes, cite Bank Mellat only if it is in the materials; otherwise say it is missing. For everyday questions, walk through the same steps in plain English. [2026-11-24 · ref 2026-11-24-1746/q13]
