# Land Law: answer rules
- **Express legal easement over registered land**: its grant is a registrable disposition (LRA 2002, s 27(2)(d)). If not
  completed by registration it takes effect in equity only, and an equitable easement can **never** override under
  Sch 3 para 3 (legal easements only). It binds only if protected by a notice. (A common and costly error.)
- **Lease over 7 years**: registrable disposition (s 27(2)(b)(i)); unregistered = at best equitable lease (*Walsh v
  Lonsdale*); may override only via Sch 3 para 2 (actual occupation), not para 1 (short legal leases only). Always deal
  with the **exceptions in para 2** (inquiry not disclosed; not obvious on a reasonably careful inspection and purchaser
  without actual knowledge).
- **Option to purchase / consent agreements / restrictions** are separate interests: discuss each. An option binds only
  if protected by a notice; a restriction (s 40) protects a beneficial interest/consent requirement.
- **Overreaching** (LPA 1925, ss 2, 27): capital money paid to two trustees → beneficial interest overreached; with one
  trustee it is not. Always test it before actual occupation (*Boland*, *Flegg*).
- Apply the **whole** of s 29 LRA 2002, not part of it.
- Spend words on the hard issue (how to determine actual occupation: *Boland*, *Link Lending*, *Chhokar*, *Stockholm
  Finance*), not on the easy one.
- Proprietary estoppel: cover every element, including the **presumption of reliance**, and weigh benefits received
  (e.g. living rent-free) against detriment; remedy per *Guest v Guest* (expectation starting point) and
  proportionality (*Jennings*). Skip routes that clearly fail (common intention constructive trust) in a sentence.
- Severance of joint tenancy essays: statutory notice (s 36(2) LPA; *Kinch v Bullard*), mutual agreement, course of
  dealing, *Williams v Hensman*; evaluate certainty vs fairness, gaps (e.g. email notice), reform.
- Problem answers: OSCOLA footnotes, italicised case names, a bibliography listing only cited sources.

## Everyday questions (learned from testing)
- For adverse possession, first say whether the land is registered: for unregistered land, 12 years' adverse possession bars the owner (Limitation Act 1980 ss 15, 17). For registered land, set out LRA 2002 Sch 6: the squatter may apply after 10 years (para 1); the owner is notified (para 2) and can object and require the application to be dealt with under para 5 (para 3); the squatter then succeeds only on one of the three para 5 conditions (estoppel, some other entitlement, or a reasonable belief for 10 years that the adjoining land was theirs). [2026-11-22 · ref 2026-11-22-1017/q12]
- If the application is rejected and the squatter stays in adverse possession for two more years, they can reapply and be registered (paras 6–7), so the owner must recover possession or start possession proceedings within those two years. [2026-11-22 · ref 2026-11-22-1017/q12]
- Before applying English tenancy rules, confirm the property is in England: Wales uses occupation contracts under the Renting Homes (Wales) Act 2016, and Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own regimes. [2026-11-24 · ref 2026-11-22-1301/q22]
- For residential tenancy questions about property in England dated on or after 1 May 2026, state that the Renters' Rights Act 2025 abolished s 21 'no-fault' eviction and converted existing assured shorthold tenancies into assured periodic tenancies from that date, so a landlord now needs a statutory possession ground under Housing Act 1988 s 8; never present s 21 or s 21-linked restrictions as current law, except to flag transitional rules for notices served before that date. [2026-11-24 · ref 2026-11-22-1301/q22]
