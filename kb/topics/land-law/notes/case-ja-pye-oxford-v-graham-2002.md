# J A Pye (Oxford) Ltd v Graham [2002] UKHL 30 (case note)

> The National Archives does not hold this judgment and no other official copy could be obtained. The House of
> Lords speeches were not read for this note. The facts come from the European Court of Human Rights' account of the
> domestic proceedings, and the holdings from secondary sources. No paragraph pinpoints are given. Check against
> [2003] 1 AC 419 before citing.

## Overview
House of Lords, 4 July 2002 (Lord Bingham, Lord Mackay of Clashfern, Lord Browne-Wilkinson, Lord Hope, Lord Hutton).
The leading speech was given by Lord Browne-Wilkinson. The House unanimously restored Neuberger J's order that the
squatters had acquired title. It is the leading modern statement of what "possession" means in adverse possession,
and it still governs that question under the Land Registration Act 2002.

## Facts
- Pye owned about 23 hectares of farmland in Berkshire. The Grahams, neighbouring farmers, grazed it under an
  agreement that ended on 31 December 1983. In January 1984 Pye refused a new agreement because it planned to seek
  planning permission.
- In June 1984 Pye sold the Grahams the standing grass, to be cut by 31 August 1984. The Grahams' later requests for
  a further cut or grazing (December 1984, May 1985) went unanswered. From September 1984 they kept using the whole
  land as part of their farm without permission.
- Mr Graham registered cautions in 1997. Pye began proceedings in 1998 and sought possession in January 1999.
- Neuberger J ([2000] Ch 676) found for the Grahams. The Court of Appeal ([2001] Ch 804) reversed, holding that they
  lacked the intention to possess. The House of Lords allowed the Grahams' appeal.

## Held
- "Adverse possession" means ordinary possession by the squatter without the owner's consent. It needs (1) a
  sufficient degree of **factual possession** and (2) an **intention to possess**, approving Slade J in *Powell v
  McFarlane* (1977) 38 P & CR 452.
- The intention required is to **possess**, not to **own** or to acquire title. *Buckinghamshire CC v Moran* [1990]
  Ch 623 was approved.
- The Grahams farmed the land as their own. It mattered not that they knew of Pye's plans for the land, or that they
  would have paid for a licence had one been offered.
- The "implied licence" idea from *Leigh v Jack* (1879), that acts consistent with the owner's intended future use
  are not adverse, was rejected (see also Limitation Act 1980 Sch 1 para 8(4)).
- Twelve years ran from September 1984 (Limitation Act 1980 s 15). Under Land Registration Act 1925 s 75 the
  registered owner then held on trust for the squatters.
- Lord Bingham agreed with "no enthusiasm" and found it hard to justify, for registered land, a rule compelling such
  a result (as quoted by the ECtHR).

## Later treatment
- **ECtHR.** A Chamber held by four votes to three on 15 November 2005 that the law violated Article 1 of Protocol
  No 1. The Grand Chamber reversed this on 30 August 2007 by ten votes to seven ((2008) 46 EHRR 45). It held that
  the interference was proportionate and within the UK's margin of appreciation.
- **Land Registration Act 2002** (in force 13 October 2003). For registered land, a squatter applies after 10
  years. The registered owner is notified and, unless one of the Sch 6 para 5 conditions applies, can object and
  defeat the claim. *Pye*'s meaning of possession still applies.

## Applying it to a problem question
1. Factual possession: acts suited to the land (fencing, locked gates, farming) showing exclusive physical control.
2. Intention to possess: present intention to exclude everyone, including the owner. An intention to own is not
   needed, and willingness to pay for a licence does not defeat it.
3. Without consent: possession under a licence is not adverse. Time runs once the licence ends.
4. Period and regime: unregistered land, or registered land where the 12 years ran before 13 October 2003, falls
   under LA 1980. Otherwise apply LRA 2002 Sch 6.
