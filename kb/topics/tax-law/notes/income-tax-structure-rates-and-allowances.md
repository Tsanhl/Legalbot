# Income tax structure, allowances, bands and rates (2025-26 to 2027-28)

## Overview
Income tax is charged annually on a person's total income after reliefs, in layers (non-savings, savings, dividends; and from April 2027 a separate property income layer), each with its own rates. The personal allowance (£12,570) is lost progressively once adjusted net income exceeds £100,000, producing an effective 60% rate on the slice from £100,000 to £125,140. Thresholds are frozen to April 2031; dividend rates rose by two percentage points from April 2026, and savings and property income rates rise from April 2027.

## The law

### Charge and computation
- Income tax is charged for each tax year (6 April to 5 April) by the annual Finance Act, with the machinery in the Income Tax Act 2007 (ITA 2007) and the charging provisions for each source in ITEPA 2003 (employment, pensions, social security) and ITTOIA 2005 (trading, property, savings, dividends and miscellaneous income).
- Computation follows ITA 2007 s 23 in steps: identify income by source and the amount taxable; deduct reliefs (for example trading losses, qualifying interest) to arrive at net income; deduct the personal allowance (ITA 2007 s 35) to find taxable income; apply the rates in the order non-savings, savings, then dividends; then apply tax reductions. Gift Aid and personal pension contributions are not deductions: they extend the basic-rate (and higher-rate) band.
- The tax-free layers: personal allowance; savings starting rate band (up to £5,000, reduced £1 for £1 by non-savings income above the personal allowance; fixed at £5,000 to 2030-31); personal savings allowance (£1,000 for basic-rate taxpayers, £500 for higher-rate, nil for additional-rate); dividend allowance (£500); trading allowance and property allowance (£1,000 each, as an alternative to deducting actual expenses; Budget 2026 proposes raising the trading allowance to £3,000 from 6 April 2027).

### Personal allowance and taper
- Personal allowance: £12,570. Finance Act 2026 s 10 extended the freeze on the personal allowance and the basic-rate limit to 2030-31 (that is, to 5 April 2031).
- Taper (ITA 2007 s 35): reduced by £1 for every £2 of adjusted net income over £100,000. It is nil where adjusted net income is £125,140 or more. "Adjusted net income" is net income less grossed-up personal pension contributions and Gift Aid donations, which can pull income back under £100,000.
- Marriage allowance: a spouse or civil partner can transfer £1,260 of personal allowance to the other if neither pays tax above the basic rate (in practice the transferor has income below the personal allowance); value up to £252.

### Bands and rates: England and Northern Ireland, non-savings non-dividend income (2025-26 to 2027-28)
| Band | Taxable income | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Basic | £0 to £37,700 | 20% |
| Higher | £37,701 to £125,140 | 40% |
| Additional | over £125,140 | 45% |
With the full personal allowance, the higher-rate threshold on total income is therefore £50,270. Scotland sets its own bands and rates for non-savings non-dividend income (starter, basic, intermediate, higher, advanced and top rates), so the same salary can produce a different bill; savings and dividend income is taxed at UK-wide rates everywhere. Wales sets Welsh rates (UK rates less 10p plus a Welsh rate), which have so far produced the same rates as England.

### Savings, dividends and property income
- Savings income (interest): 20% basic, 40% higher, 45% additional (2025-26 and 2026-27). The 0% starting rate and the personal savings allowance sit within the bands as above.
- Dividends: 2025-26: 8.75% ordinary, 33.75% upper, 39.35% additional. From 6 April 2026 (FA 2026 s 4): 10.75% ordinary and 35.75% upper; additional rate unchanged at 39.35%.
- From 6 April 2027 Finance Act 2026 (ss 5-8) creates separate savings rates and property income rates (property rates for England and Northern Ireland; Scotland and Wales may set their own property rates) and enacts them at 22%, 42% and 47%. At Budget 2026 (28 October 2026) the government announced a further two-point rise, to 24% (basic), 44% (higher) and 49% (additional), for both savings and property income from 6 April 2027; this is to be legislated in Finance Bill 2026-27 and was not yet enacted at November 2026. Property finance cost relief is to be given at the property basic rate, and from April 2027 allowances and reliefs are set against other income before property, savings and dividend income.

### Employment income and National Insurance
Employment income is taxed under ITEPA 2003 through PAYE, with employee Class 1 NICs (8% between the primary threshold and upper earnings limit, 2% above) running separately; NICs are a distinct charge and not part of income tax. Employer NICs are 15% above a secondary threshold of £5,000 a year (from April 2025). Self-employed Class 4 NICs are 6% between £12,570 and £50,270 and 2% above.

### Other core points
- Residence matters to the scope: UK residents are taxed on worldwide income; non-residents on UK-source income only (see the residence note).
- The High Income Child Benefit Charge applies between adjusted net incomes of £60,000 and £80,000 (from April 2024); mention only if relevant.

## Key authorities
- *Edwards v Bairstow* [1956] AC 14 (HL): whether a transaction is an "adventure in the nature of trade" is a question of fact and degree; an appellate court may interfere with the tribunal's conclusion only if no person acting judicially and properly instructed as to the law could have reached it.
- *Ramsay* line (see tax avoidance note) governs the reading of reliefs.
- For the personal allowance and bands, the primary authority is statute, not case law.

## Applying it
1. Identify each source of income and the charging provision (employment, trading, property, savings, dividends).
2. Compute net income; deduct reliefs; find adjusted net income for the £100,000 taper.
3. Allocate the personal allowance first against non-savings income, then savings, then dividends (from April 2027, property, savings and dividend income come last).
4. Apply bands in that order; check whether the savings starting rate or PSA is available.
5. Check the date: 2025-26 dividend rates differ from 2026-27; savings and property rates change in 2027-28.
6. Common traps: forgetting the taper's 60% effective rate; forgetting that income covered by the dividend allowance or PSA, though taxed at 0%, still uses up the basic-rate band; using Scottish rates for savings income; treating NICs as income tax.
7. Everyday question: "How much tax on £60,000 salary?" works through personal allowance (£12,570), £37,700 at 20% (£7,540), the remaining £9,730 at 40% (£3,892): £11,432 income tax, plus NICs.

## Debates and reform
- Fiscal drag: freezing thresholds to 2031 takes more people into higher bands without a rate rise.
- The 60% effective rate between £100,000 and £125,140 is criticised as irrational and as discouraging work and encouraging pension contributions.
- The couple-versus-individual basis of taxation (the UK taxes individuals, marriage allowance is marginal) is a recurring subject.
- The movement towards higher taxation of "unearned" income (dividends, savings, property) narrows the gap with earned income (which also bears NICs); critics fear effects on saving and on the rental market.

## Recent changes and points to check
- 6 April 2026: dividend ordinary and upper rates 10.75% / 35.75% (FA 2026).
- 6 April 2027: separate savings and property rates. Enacted at 22/42/47% (FA 2026); Budget 2026 proposes 24/44/49% via Finance Bill 2026-27, not yet enacted at November 2026.
- Threshold freeze to 5 April 2031 (FA 2026 s 10).
- Scottish and Welsh rates are set annually and should be checked for the year in question.
