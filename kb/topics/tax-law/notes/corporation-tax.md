# Corporation tax: rates, marginal relief, losses and close companies

## Overview
Companies pay corporation tax (CT) on profits (income and chargeable gains) for each accounting period. The main rate is 25%, the small profits rate 19% (profits up to £50,000), with marginal relief between £50,000 and £250,000. Trading losses can be carried back, forward and across a group within rules. Close companies face extra charges, notably on loans to participators.

## The law

### Charge and residence
- Corporation Tax Act 2009 (CTA 2009) ss 2 and 5: UK-resident companies are chargeable to CT on profits wherever arising; non-resident companies are chargeable on profits of a UK permanent establishment and on profits from UK land (property income and gains on UK land, since April 2019/2020).
- A company incorporated in the UK is UK-resident (CTA 2009 s 14). A foreign-incorporated company is resident if its central management and control are in the UK (*De Beers Consolidated Mines Ltd v Howe* [1906] AC 455), subject to treaty tie-breakers.
- Profits for CT: income (trading, property, loan relationships, miscellaneous) computed under the usual income rules (CTA 2009), plus chargeable gains computed under TCGA 1992 (no annual exempt amount or BADR; gains are taxed at the company's rate). Capital allowances are deducted as trading expenses.
- Accounting periods: up to 12 months; return (CT600) due 12 months after the period end; tax due nine months and one day after the period end (large and very large companies pay in quarterly instalments).

### Rates and marginal relief (from 1 April 2023; unchanged for financial years 2025 to 2027, and Budget 2026 proposes the same for FY2028)
- Main rate: 25% on profits over £250,000.
- Small profits rate: 19% on profits up to £50,000.
- Marginal relief for profits between £50,000 and £250,000: the main rate applies and tax is reduced by (£250,000 minus profits) x (basic profits / profits) x 3/200. The effective marginal rate is 26.5% in that band.
- The limits are divided by the number of associated companies plus one (for control by the same person or group), and proportionately reduced for short accounting periods. "Profits" for this purpose include exempt distributions (augmented profits).
- The multinational and domestic top-up taxes (Pillar Two, F(No 2)A 2023, for accounting periods beginning on or after 31 December 2023) add a layer for groups with consolidated revenues of at least EUR 750 million.

### Loss relief (CTA 2010 Pt 4 and Pt 7ZA, with CTA 2009 for loan relationships)
- Trading loss: set against total profits of the same period (s 37), and carried back to the previous 12 months (s 37(3)(b)); the terminal loss relief extends carry-back to three years for losses of the last 12 months of trading (s 39). Temporary extended carry-back (three years) applied for periods ending 1 April 2020 to 31 March 2022 only.
- Carry-forward: pre-April 2017 losses (s 45) only against future profits of the same trade; losses arising from 1 April 2017 can generally be set against total profits of the company (s 45A), and can be group relieved (Pt 5A). All carried-forward losses are subject to the loss restriction: a £5 million deductions allowance per group, with only 50% of profits above that relievable (CTA 2010 Pt 7ZA).
- Non-trading loan relationship deficits, property business losses and capital losses (against chargeable gains only) have their own rules.
- Group relief (CTA 2010 Pt 5): current-year losses, and some other amounts, can be surrendered between 75% group companies; consortium relief is also available.
- Change of ownership (CTA 2010 Pt 14, ss 673 onwards): carried-forward trading losses are forfeited if there is a change in ownership and a major change in the nature or conduct of the trade within a five-year window (or the trade has become small or negligible).

### Close companies (CTA 2010 Pt 10)
- s 439: a close company is a UK-resident company under the control of five or fewer participators, or of any number of participators who are directors. Participators are those who have a share or interest in the capital or income, including loan creditors; associates are attributed (relatives, partners, trustees).
- Exceptions: non-close if it is controlled by a non-close company, or if it is listed with at least 35% of voting power held by the public.
- s 455: where a close company makes a loan or advances credit to a participator (or associate), and the loan is still outstanding nine months after the end of the accounting period, the company pays tax on the outstanding amount, due nine months and one day after the period end. The rate matches the dividend upper rate: 33.75% for loans made before 6 April 2026 and 35.75% for loans made on or after that date. The tax is refunded (s 458) when the loan is repaid, released or written off, but only nine months after the end of the period in which that happens. Loans not exceeding £15,000 to a borrower who works full time for the company and has no material interest (more than 5%) are outside the charge (s 456(4)). Release or writing off of the loan is taxed on the participator as a distribution (ITTOIA 2005 s 415), not as a further s 455 charge. Anti-"bed and breakfasting" rules (s 464C) stop repayments followed by re-borrowing from counting.
- Benefits to participators who are not employees may be treated as distributions (CTA 2010 s 1064).
- Close investment-holding companies pay the main rate regardless of profit level (they cannot use the small profits rate).

## Key authorities
- *De Beers Consolidated Mines Ltd v Howe* [1906] AC 455 (HL): a company resides where its real business is carried on; that is where central management and control actually abide.
- Disputes on close-company status and loans are mostly decided at tribunal level and are fact specific; use the statutory text.

## Applying it
1. Establish the company's residence and accounting period.
2. Compute profits: income under the heads; chargeable gains; deduct capital allowances and reliefs (including R&D relief).
3. Determine the rate: the number of associated companies and the length of period, then marginal relief.
4. Apply losses in the order: current year, carry-back, carry-forward; check restrictions.
5. For a company owned by its director: consider close company status, s 455 charges on director's loan accounts, and the interaction with dividends and salary.
6. Common traps: forgetting associated company divisors; confusing marginal relief with the effective 26.5% rate; forgetting that the s 455 charge is refundable; treating directors' loans as tax-free.
7. Everyday question: "My limited company made £60,000 profit. What do I pay?" With no associated companies and a 12-month period: 25% x £60,000 = £15,000, less marginal relief (£250,000 - £60,000) x 3/200 = £2,850, so £12,150 (the same as 19% of £50,000 plus 26.5% of the extra £10,000); payable nine months and one day after year end.

## Debates and reform
- The rate rose from 19% to 25% in April 2023 after eight years of cuts; whether lower rates drive investment is contested.
- The small profits band creates a marginal rate spike; some argue for a smooth schedule.
- Full expensing for companies' new main-pool plant and machinery (100% first-year allowance from April 2023, made permanent by Finance Act 2024) is credited as an investment incentive. The main-pool writing-down allowance fell from 18% to 14% from April 2026 and a 40% first-year allowance (including for leased assets and unincorporated businesses) applies from 1 January 2026. Budget 2026 proposes raising that first-year allowance to 50% from 1 January 2027 and cutting the WDA to 12% from April 2027 (not yet enacted).
- Tax treatment of owner-managed businesses (dividends vs salary) remains disputed.

## Recent changes and points to check
- CT rates 19%/25% since 1 April 2023; Budget 2026 confirms no change.
- s 455 rate 35.75% for loans made on or after 6 April 2026 (HMRC's online CT service applies the new rate only from April 2027, so some returns may need amending).
- Capital allowance changes proposed at Budget 2026 await Finance Bill 2026-27.
