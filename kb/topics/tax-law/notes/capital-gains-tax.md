# Capital gains tax: disposals, computation, reliefs and reporting

## Overview
CGT is charged on gains made on disposals of chargeable assets by individuals, trustees and personal representatives (companies pay corporation tax on chargeable gains instead). The gain is proceeds less allowable costs; the annual exempt amount (£3,000) is deducted, and the net gain is taxed at 18% or 24%. Private residence relief and Business Asset Disposal Relief are the key reliefs. UK residential property gains must be reported and paid within 60 days of completion.

## The law

### Charge and disposals (TCGA 1992)
- s 1 and s 2: CGT is charged on chargeable gains accruing on disposal of assets by persons resident (or ordinarily resident, historically) in the UK; non-residents are within the charge for UK land and certain UK property-rich interests.
- s 21: "assets" are all forms of property, including debts, options and incorporeal property, with listed exceptions. s 22: a "disposal" includes capital sums derived from assets (compensation, insurance proceeds), even where no asset is disposed of.
- s 17: where the disposal is a gift or not at arm's length, the disposal is treated as for market value. s 18: connected persons are treated as dealing at market value, and losses on such transactions are ring-fenced.
- s 58: transfers between spouses or civil partners living together are on a no gain/no loss basis. s 62: no gain or loss on death; the personal representatives acquire at probate value (the "uplift"), and legatees acquire at that value.
- s 38: allowable expenditure is limited to acquisition cost, enhancement expenditure reflected in the asset, and the costs of establishing title or disposal.
- Shares: share pooling and matching rules (ss 104, 105, 106A): same-day, then the following 30 days ("bed and breakfasting"), then the s 104 pool.

### Rates and allowance
- Annual exempt amount (s 1K): £3,000 for 2024-25 onwards for individuals (£1,500 for most trusts).
- Rates (s 1H, as amended by FA 2025 s 7): from 30 October 2024, 18% on gains falling within the unused basic-rate band and 24% above it, for all assets (residential property gains had already been taxed at 18%/24% since 6 April 2024, when the 28% higher rate was cut). Trustees and personal representatives pay 24%.
- Business Asset Disposal Relief (BADR, ss 169H to 169S): applies to disposals of all or part of a trading business, assets used in a business that has ceased within three years, and shares in a trading "personal company" (at least 5% of ordinary share capital and voting rights, and the person is an officer or employee) held for two years. Lifetime limit of £1 million. Rate: 10% to 5 April 2025; 14% for disposals from 6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026; 18% from 6 April 2026 (FA 2025 s 8). Budget 2026 proposes cutting the lifetime limit to £500,000 for disposals on or after 6 April 2027 (Finance Bill 2026-27, not yet enacted).
- Investors' relief (ss 169VA onwards): for newly issued shares in unlisted trading companies subscribed for and held for three years from 6 April 2016, by a person not an employee or officer. Lifetime limit cut from £10 million to £1 million for disposals from 30 October 2024. The rate follows BADR (14% in 2025-26, 18% from 2026-27).
- Founders' Relief (proposed): Budget 2026 announced a new 10% rate on up to £10 million of lifetime gains for qualifying founders disposing of shares on or after 6 April 2027, with BADR and investors' relief claims counting against the limit. Consultation closes 30 November 2026; it is not yet law.
- Hold-over relief for gifts of business assets (s 165) and for gifts that are immediately chargeable to IHT (s 260). Hold-over is not available where the donee is not UK resident (ss 166, 261).

### Losses and set-off order
- Allowable losses of the current year are set against gains of the same year first, in full, even if that brings the gains below the annual exempt amount (wasting the AEA). The AEA is then deducted, and brought-forward losses are set only against any gains that remain (TCGA s 1K(4)(b)), so the AEA is not wasted. Do not also say the losses are used 'down to the AEA': that counts the AEA twice. A loss must be notified (claimed) within four years from the end of the tax year in which it arose.
- Where gains attract different rates (for example BADR gains and other gains), losses and the AEA may be allocated in the way most favourable to the taxpayer (ss 1K(5) and 4B), normally against the gains taxed at the highest rate.
- Losses on disposals to connected persons can be set only against gains on other disposals to the same person.

### Private residence relief (ss 222 to 226)
- s 222: relief applies to a gain on a dwelling-house (with up to 0.5 hectare of grounds, or more if the land is required for reasonable enjoyment) that has been the individual's only or main residence.
- s 223(1): the gain is wholly exempt if the dwelling was the only or main residence throughout ownership. s 223(2): the last nine months of ownership are always treated as occupation (this is 36 months for disabled people and residents of care homes where the property was previously the main residence).
- s 223(3): periods of absence count: any period of up to three years in total, any period of working abroad, and up to four years where work is elsewhere in the UK; these require actual occupation as the main residence before and after the absence (the "after" condition is relaxed where work prevents a return).
- Letting relief survives only where the owner shares occupation with the tenant (s 223(4)), from April 2020.
- An election can be made within two years of acquiring a second residence to nominate which is the main residence (s 222(5)).
- Occupation need not be long, but it must have "some degree of permanence, some degree of continuity or some expectation of continuity" (*Goodwin v Curtis*).

### Reporting and payment
- Self Assessment: gains for a tax year are reported by 31 January after the year end and the tax paid by that date.
- UK residential property (Finance Act 2019 Sch 2): for disposals completed on or after 27 October 2021, a UK resident must file a UK property return and pay the estimated tax within 60 days of completion (30 days for completions from 6 April 2020 to 26 October 2021). A UK resident need not file one where no CGT is payable (for example the gain is fully covered by private residence relief, the AEA or losses, or the disposal is to a spouse). The payment on account is credited in the Self Assessment return.
- Non-residents must file a 60-day return for every direct or indirect disposal of UK land (residential or not), even where there is no gain or tax due.

## Key authorities
- *Goodwin v Curtis* [1998] STC 475 (CA): for private residence relief, the occupier must have settled in the property with some degree of permanence; a short stay and then selling did not qualify as a "residence" on the facts.
- *WT Ramsay Ltd v IRC* [1982] AC 300 (HL): the court looks at composite transactions in the light of the statute's purpose; see the tax avoidance note.

## Applying it
1. Is there an "asset" and a "disposal" (s 21, s 22, including deemed disposals)? Is it a gift or connected-person transaction (market value)?
2. Compute: proceeds (or market value) less allowable costs; apply relief (PRR, BADR, hold-over).
3. Order of set-off (TCGA s 1K(4)): deduct current-year losses in full first; then the AEA; then brought-forward losses against what remains. So brought-forward losses are used only on gains above the AEA, and the AEA is never wasted. Losses and the AEA may be set against the gains taxed at the highest rate first (most beneficial use; for the AEA, TCGA s 1K(5)).
4. Apply 18% or 24% depending on remaining basic-rate band; use BADR rate on qualifying gains.
5. Check reporting: the 60-day return for UK residential property; otherwise Self Assessment by 31 January after the year.
6. Common traps: forgetting the 60-day deadline; wasting the AEA by setting brought-forward losses first; assuming letting relief applies to ordinary lets; ignoring the final nine months; forgetting that death produces an uplift not a gain.
7. Everyday question: "I sold my buy-to-let. How much CGT and by when?" gain less costs and AEA, at 18% or 24%, reported and paid within 60 days of completion if tax is due.

## Debates and reform
- The Office of Tax Simplification (2020) recommended aligning CGT rates with income tax and cutting the AEA; the government has cut the AEA but kept lower rates.
- BADR's cost and effectiveness are questioned; the staged increase in the rate, and the proposed cut in the lifetime limit alongside a targeted Founders' Relief, reflect a view that it did not drive investment.
- The uplift on death is criticised as an incentive to hold assets until death.

## Recent changes and points to check
- 30 October 2024: rates 18%/24%; investors' relief lifetime limit £1m.
- BADR and investors' relief rate: 14% (6 April 2025), 18% (6 April 2026).
- AEA £3,000 from 2024-25 (unchanged for 2026-27 and 2027-28 at Budget 2026).
- Proposed from 6 April 2027 (Finance Bill 2026-27, not enacted at November 2026): BADR lifetime limit £500,000; Founders' Relief at 10% on up to £10 million.

## Common errors to avoid

- Setting brought-forward losses before the annual exempt amount: TCGA s 1K(4) deducts current-year losses, then the AEA, then brought-forward losses. [2026-11-24 · ref 2026-11-24-1510/q33]
- Ruling out BADR on the 5% personal-company test without asking whether the shares came from EMI options: EMI shares need no 5% holding. [2026-11-24 · ref 2026-11-24-1510/q33]
- Assuming the BADR employee/officer and holding conditions are met: check they were satisfied throughout the 2 years ending with the disposal. [2026-11-24 · ref 2026-11-24-1510/q33#3]
- Do not present 'Founders' Relief' as available or use it in a computation: it is only a proposal for disposals from 6 April 2027, not enacted at November 2026. If mentioned at all, state its exact source and unlegislated status; otherwise omit it. [2026-11-24 · ref 2026-11-24-1510/q33#2]
