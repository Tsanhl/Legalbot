# Inheritance tax: transfers, reliefs, nil-rate bands and the 2026-27 changes

## Overview
IHT (Inheritance Tax Act 1984, IHTA) charges 40% on the value of a chargeable transfer above the nil-rate band, on death and on some lifetime transfers. Gifts to individuals are potentially exempt and fall out of charge after seven years. The nil-rate band is £325,000 and the residence nil-rate band £175,000, both frozen. From 6 April 2026 the 100% relief for business and agricultural property is capped at £2.5 million per person, and from 6 April 2027 unused pension funds will fall into the estate.

## The law

### The charge
- s 1: IHT is charged on the value transferred by a chargeable transfer. s 2: a chargeable transfer is a transfer of value made by an individual that is not exempt. s 3: a transfer of value is a disposition that reduces the transferor's estate; s 3A: potentially exempt transfers (PETs) are gifts to individuals (or to certain trusts for disabled people or bereaved minors), which become chargeable only if the donor dies within seven years. s 4: a transfer is deemed made immediately before death of the whole estate.
- Lifetime transfers into most trusts are chargeable lifetime transfers (CLTs): 20% above the nil-rate band (cumulated over seven years), with a further charge up to 40% if the donor dies within seven years. Relevant property trusts bear periodic (10-yearly, up to 6%) and exit charges.
- Rates (s 7 and Sch 1): 0% up to the nil-rate band (£325,000), 40% above; 36% if at least 10% of the net estate is left to charity (Finance Act 2012 Sch 33).
- Seven-year rule and taper (s 7(4)): for gifts made more than three but less than seven years before death, tax is reduced by taper relief: 20% (3-4 years), 40% (4-5), 60% (5-6), 80% (6-7). Taper relief reduces the tax, not the value, and matters only if the gift exceeded the nil-rate band.
- Nil-rate band used first by earlier transfers in the seven years before the later transfer ("cumulation"); unused nil-rate band of a deceased spouse or civil partner transferred on second death (s 8A to 8C), up to 100%.
- Residence nil-rate band (RNRB, ss 8D to 8M): up to £175,000 where a home (or its proceeds under the downsizing rules, s 8FA) passes to direct descendants. Tapered by £1 for each £2 of estate above £2 million (the taper threshold applies to the net estate before reliefs). Transferable to a surviving spouse. Frozen to 5 April 2031 (FA 2026 s 72).
- Exemptions: spouse/civil partner (s 18; unlimited, but since 6 April 2025 limited to £325,000 where a long-term UK resident transferor gives to a spouse who is not a long-term UK resident); annual exemption £3,000 (s 19, one year's carry forward); small gifts £250 per donee (s 20); normal expenditure out of income (s 21); gifts in consideration of marriage (s 22: £5,000 parent, £2,500 grandparent, £1,000 other); charities (s 23); political parties (s 24).
- Gifts with reservation (Finance Act 1986 s 102): a gift where the donor keeps a benefit is still treated as part of the estate on death; pre-owned assets income tax charge (FA 2004 Sch 15) can apply. 
- Valuation: open market value (s 160), with related property rules (s 161) and the "loss to donor" principle.
- Payment: tax on death is due six months after the end of the month of death (interest runs from then); instalments are available for land and certain business assets over ten years. Personal representatives pay the tax from the estate before probate is granted where possible; the grant is usually needed to access assets, which creates a cash-flow problem.

### Business property relief (BPR, ss 103 to 114) and agricultural property relief (APR, ss 115 to 124C)
- Relevant business property: a business or interest in a business (100%), unquoted shares including AIM (100% until 2026), controlling holdings of quoted shares (50%), land, buildings and machinery used by a controlled company or the partnership (50%). Excepted: businesses that consist wholly or mainly of dealing in securities, stocks or shares, land or buildings, or making or holding investments (s 105(3)); the "investment" test turns on the whole business.
- Minimum ownership period: two years (s 106).
- APR: relief on the agricultural value of agricultural property (land or pasture, growing crops, farmhouses of appropriate character) occupied for agriculture by the owner for two years or owned by the owner for seven years and occupied by another for agriculture; rate 100% (vacant possession or tenanted under certain tenancies) or 50%.

### 6 April 2026 changes (Finance Act 2026 s 65)
- 100% BPR/APR is limited to the first £2.5 million of combined qualifying property for each individual (the allowance was first announced as £1 million at Autumn Budget 2024 and raised to £2.5 million on 23 December 2025). Qualifying property above that attracts 50% relief, giving an effective 20% rate on the excess.
- The allowance is transferable to a surviving spouse or civil partner, so a couple can pass up to £5 million at 100%. Where the first death was before 6 April 2026, the full £2.5 million is treated as available.
- AIM and similar unlisted-market shares receive 50% only, regardless of the allowance (not 100%).
- Lifetime transfers: the new rules apply to lifetime transfers made on or after 30 October 2024 where the donor dies on or after 6 April 2026, so such a gift can use up the allowance.
- Trusts: relevant property trusts settled before 30 October 2024 each have their own £2.5 million allowance; trusts made by the same settlor on or after that date share one allowance.
- Instalment option: IHT on all property qualifying for APR or BPR can be paid in ten equal annual instalments interest-free.

### Pensions in the estate from 6 April 2027
Finance Act 2026 (ss 66-71) brings most unused pension funds and death benefits into the estate for IHT for deaths on or after 6 April 2027. Death-in-service benefits from registered schemes and dependants' scheme pensions are excluded, and the spouse and charity exemptions still apply. Personal representatives are responsible for reporting and paying, and may direct the scheme administrator to pay the IHT due on the pension (with the beneficiary also able to ask the scheme to pay). Budget 2026 proposed further technical changes, including how pension property counts towards the 36% charity rate test.

## Key authorities
- *Ingram v IRC* [2000] 1 AC 293 (HL): where a donor carved a lease out of freehold land and gave away the freehold, the gift was not subject to the reservation of benefit provisions; the carve-out was a gift of a lesser interest and the donor was not retaining a benefit in the gifted property. Legislation (FA 1986 s 102A, inserted in 1999) later closed that planning route for gifts of land.
- *IRC v George* [2003] EWCA Civ 1763 (CA): for the s 105(3) "wholly or mainly holding investments" test the business is looked at in the round; a residential caravan park with substantial services was not mainly an investment business. *HMRC v Pawson* [2013] UKUT 50 (TCC): a furnished holiday let was mainly the holding of an investment, so BPR was refused. The question is one of fact and degree.

## Applying it
1. Identify the transfer: death estate, PET, CLT or a trust charge.
2. Value the transfer; apply exemptions in the order the Act sets (spouse, charity, annual, small gifts, normal expenditure).
3. For lifetime gifts, check the seven-year period: PET becomes chargeable on death within seven years; apply nil-rate band to the earliest gifts first; taper relief from year three.
4. Death estate: add the value of gifts with reservation, shares and property; deduct debts and funeral costs; apply spouse/charity exemptions; reliefs (BPR/APR within the £2.5m allowance); nil-rate band and RNRB (check taper and who inherits).
5. Common traps: assuming a gift to a trust is a PET; forgetting the RNRB taper; assuming APR attaches to the whole value (it attaches to agricultural value); treating the BPR/APR cap as per asset rather than combined.
6. Everyday question: "Can I give my house to my children?" Possible, but a gift with reservation if the parent stays rent-free; the gift becomes exempt only after seven years.

## Debates and reform
- The nil-rate band has been frozen since 2009, with fiscal drag pulling more estates into IHT.
- Reliefs for agricultural and family businesses were criticised as tax-planning vehicles by the government, and the cap as threatening family farms by farming groups; the cap was raised from £1m to £2.5m and made transferable before enactment.
- Pensions in the IHT net remove a long-used planning route.
- Simplification: the Office of Tax Simplification's 2018 and 2019 reports on IHT recommended simplifying lifetime exemptions and the seven-year rules.

## Recent changes and points to check
- 6 April 2025: residence-based IHT (long-term UK residence).
- 6 April 2026: APR/BPR £2.5m allowance and 50% relief above; AIM at 50% (FA 2026 s 65).
- 6 April 2027: pensions in the estate (FA 2026 ss 66-71).
- The nil-rate band, RNRB and RNRB taper threshold are frozen to 5 April 2031 (FA 2026 s 72); Budget 2026 made no change.
