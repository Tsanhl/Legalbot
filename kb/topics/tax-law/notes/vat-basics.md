# VAT basics: supplies, registration, rates, exemption and input tax

## Overview
VAT (Value Added Tax Act 1994, VATA) is a tax on taxable supplies of goods and services made in the UK by a taxable person in the course of business. The standard rate is 20%. Zero-rated supplies are taxable at 0% (so the supplier recovers input tax); exempt supplies are outside the charge and the supplier generally cannot recover input tax. The registration threshold is £90,000 of taxable turnover.

## The law

### Charge and key concepts
- s 1: VAT is charged on the supply of goods and services in the UK, acquisitions and imports. s 4: VAT is charged on a taxable supply made in the UK, by a taxable person, in the course or furtherance of business. s 5 and Sch 4: what counts as a supply of goods or services; s 5(2): all that is not goods is services; the provision of services includes a "supply" even without payment where specified (such as private use of business goods).
- "Supply" requires consideration in general: a link between payment and what is supplied (a direct link between the supply and the payment; see *Apple and Pear Development Council v Customs and Excise* (Case 102/86), 1988).
- Time of supply (tax point), s 6: for goods, when they are removed or made available; for services, when performed; an earlier invoice or payment can fix an earlier tax point.
- Place of supply: s 7 (goods) and ss 7A and Sch 4A (services); broadly, goods are supplied where they are; services are supplied B2B where the customer belongs, and B2C where the supplier belongs, with exceptions (land, events, transport). Since the end of the Brexit transition the UK operates its own import VAT and reverse charge rules and is outside the EU one-stop shop (Northern Ireland stays within EU VAT rules for goods under the Windsor Framework).
- Taxable person (s 3): a person who is registered or required to be registered. Group registration (s 43) allows UK-established bodies under common control to be treated as one for VAT.
- Mixed and composite supplies: where a transaction contains several elements, the question is whether there is a single principal supply with ancillary elements, or several independent supplies, judged from the viewpoint of the typical consumer: *Card Protection Plan Ltd v Customs and Excise* (Case C-349/96) [1999] STC 270 (ECJ), applied by the House of Lords at [2001] UKHL 4; pre-2021 CJEU case law continues to be followed by UK courts unless departed from.

### Registration
- Compulsory (VATA Sch 1): a person must register if taxable turnover over the past 12 months (rolling) exceeds £90,000, or is expected to exceed £90,000 in the next 30 days alone. The deregistration threshold is £88,000. These figures have applied since 1 April 2024 and remain unchanged for 2026-27 and 2027-28. Non-UK-established businesses making UK supplies must register whatever their turnover.
- Voluntary registration is possible below the threshold (often useful for businesses with mostly zero-rated or business customers who can recover input tax).
- Making Tax Digital for VAT requires digital records and returns for VAT-registered businesses.
- Returns are normally quarterly; schemes include flat rate, cash accounting, annual accounting and the margin scheme.

### Rates (VATA ss 2, 29A, 30, 31)
- Standard rate: 20% (s 2).
- Reduced rate: 5% (s 29A, Sch 7A): domestic fuel and power, children's car seats, contraceptives, and some other items. Installation of energy-saving materials in residential accommodation is zero-rated until 31 March 2027. Budget 2026 announced that a temporary 0% rate on domestic electricity is extended to 30 June 2027.
- Zero rate (s 30, Sch 8): most food (excluding catering, confectionery and certain items), books and newspapers, children's clothes and shoes, passenger transport (vehicles with ten or more seats), new dwellings (Group 5), exports of goods, and prescribed drugs and certain aids for disabled people.
- Exempt (s 31, Sch 9): insurance, financial services, education provided by eligible bodies, health services, postal services by the universal service provider, burial and cremation, most land transactions (the supplier may "opt to tax" non-residential land under Sch 10, then charges VAT). Private school fees (and boarding) ceased to be exempt from 1 January 2025 (Finance Act 2025 ss 47-49), and fees paid from 29 July 2024 for terms starting in January 2025 or later are also caught.

### Input tax
- Input tax is VAT on supplies to the business (and on imports). A registered person may deduct input tax to the extent that it is attributable to taxable supplies (including zero-rated): ss 24 to 26. Output tax minus input tax gives net VAT due or repayable.
- Exempt supplies: input tax cannot be recovered where attributable to exempt supplies; "partial exemption" rules (Value Added Tax Regulations 1995, SI 1995/2518, regs 99 to 111) apportion residual input tax and apply a de minimis limit (a £625 per month and half of all input tax test).
- Blocked: input tax on business entertainment and on most motor cars (Value Added Tax (Input Tax) Order 1992, SI 1992/3222); input tax on supplies used wholly for private purposes.
- Evidence: a valid VAT invoice is required to claim input tax.

### Penalties, interest and appeals
Penalties for VAT use the same general scheme as other taxes for errors (FA 2007 Sch 24), but for VAT periods starting on or after 1 January 2023 late returns and late payments are governed by Finance Act 2021 Schs 24-26: a points-based system for late submission (a £200 penalty once the points threshold is reached), and late payment penalties of 3% of tax unpaid at day 15, a further 3% at day 30 and 10% a year after that (rates from April 2025). Late payment interest runs at Bank of England base rate plus 4%. Appeals go to the First-tier Tribunal, after an optional HMRC review.

## Key authorities
- *Card Protection Plan Ltd v Customs and Excise* (Case C-349/96) [1999] STC 270 (ECJ): single supply versus multiple supplies; ancillary elements follow the principal supply's tax treatment; applied for credit-card protection and insurance.
- *Apple and Pear Development Council v Customs and Excise* (Case 102/86) [1988] ECR 1443 (ECJ): a taxable supply requires a direct link between the service and the payment; a compulsory annual levy on growers funding generic promotion was not consideration for a supply.
- Disputes over borderline categories (for example the classification of food items) are typically tribunal decisions on the statutory schedules.

## Applying it
1. Is there a supply of goods or services for consideration, made in the UK in the course of business by a taxable person?
2. What is the tax point and place of supply?
3. Classification: standard, reduced, zero, exempt or outside the scope? Treat mixed supplies using the CPP test.
4. Registration: is turnover over £90,000 (or voluntary registration)?
5. Input tax: is it attributable to taxable supplies? Is it blocked? Is there a valid invoice? Do partial exemption rules apply?
6. Common traps: confusing zero-rated with exempt; assuming registration is based on profit; applying the threshold to a single year rather than rolling 12 months; forgetting that deregistration (and voluntary registration) have their own rules; overlooking that a supplier of exempt supplies cannot recover input tax.
7. Everyday question: "Do I charge VAT on my side business?" Only if taxable turnover exceeds the threshold (or you choose to register); once registered you charge VAT on taxable supplies and file returns.

## Debates and reform
- The £90,000 threshold is among the highest in the OECD and is argued to discourage small businesses from growing (the "cliff edge"); proposals have included tapered thresholds, but none has been adopted.
- The distinction between zero-rating and exemption yields anomalies and litigation (the long-running classification disputes on food).
- Extending VAT to private education was challenged on human rights grounds. The Divisional Court dismissed the claims (*R (ALR) v Chancellor of the Exchequer* [2025] EWHC 1467 (Admin)) and the Court of Appeal dismissed the appeal (*BYL v Chancellor of the Exchequer* [2026] EWCA Civ 170). The Supreme Court granted permission to appeal; the appeal had not been decided by November 2026.

## Recent changes and points to check
- 1 January 2025: private school fees subject to VAT (Supreme Court appeal pending).
- 1 April 2024: registration threshold £90,000, deregistration £88,000; unchanged to at least March 2028.
- Budget 2026 measures (not yet enacted unless stated): online marketplace liability from October 2027; changes to the reverse charge for overseas services from April 2027; mandatory e-invoicing from April 2029.
