# Scope of the Sale of Goods Act 1979 and the Consumer Rights Act 2015

## Overview
The SGA 1979 applies only to "contracts of sale of goods" (s 1), defined in s 2(1). Whether a transaction
falls inside that definition matters because the SGA brings with it strict-liability implied terms
(ss 12-15), the property and risk rules (ss 16-20B), the nemo dat rules and the statutory remedies. If the
SGA does not apply, other statutes (e.g. the Supply of Goods and Services Act 1982) or the common law
(often fault-based) govern. Since 1 October 2015 trader-to-consumer supplies of goods are governed by
Chapter 2 of Part 1 of the CRA 2015, which runs in parallel with the SGA and borrows its property rules.
Law here is England & Wales (the SGA also extends to Scotland and Northern Ireland).

## The law

### 1. Sources and interpretation
1. The SGA 1979 consolidates the SGA 1893 (a codifying Act) and its amendments; it has itself been amended
   by the Sale and Supply of Goods Act 1994, the Sale of Goods (Amendment) Act 1994, the Sale of Goods
   (Amendment) Act 1995, EU-derived consumer measures and the CRA 2015. Other relevant statutes: Bills of
   Sale Acts 1878 and 1882, Factors Act 1889, Hire Purchase Act 1964, Consumer Credit Act 1974, UCTA 1977.
2. Read the words first: *Bank of England v Vagliano Bros* [1891] AC 107. But implied terms should not be
   read so narrowly as to force on parties promises they could not reasonably have intended (*Ashington
   Piggeries v Christopher Hill* [1972] AC 441, Lord Diplock), and for the satisfactory quality test only
   the words of the Act need be considered (*Rogers v Parish* [1987] QB 933).
3. The SGA does not codify general contract law: s 62(2) preserves the common law, including the law
   merchant, so far as not inconsistent with the Act, in particular agency, fraud, misrepresentation,
   duress, mistake and other invalidating causes.

### 2. The s 2(1) definition
"A contract of sale of goods is a contract by which the seller transfers or agrees to transfer the property
in goods to the buyer for a money consideration, called the price." Break it into four elements:

**(a) A contract.** Not defined in the SGA; it bears its common law meaning (offer, acceptance, intention),
but consideration must be money. Contracting is voluntary, so where supply is a statutory duty there is no
sale:
- *Pfizer v Minister of Health* [1965] AC 512: medicine dispensed to a hospital patient under a statutory
  right/obligation was not a sale.
- *NORWEB v Dixon* [1995] 3 All ER 952: electricity supply almost wholly regulated (creation and terms) by
  the Electricity Act 1989 was not a contract; the sum owed was not a contractual debt.
- A contract of sale may be absolute or conditional (s 2(3)); it may be between one part owner and another
  (s 2(2)).

**(b) Transfers or agrees to transfer the property.**
- *Sale* v *agreement to sell*: in a sale, property passes on the making of the contract (s 2(4)); in an
  agreement to sell it passes later or on a condition (s 2(5)); an agreement to sell becomes a sale when the
  time passes or the condition is fulfilled (s 2(6)). "Contract of sale" includes both (s 61).
- "Property" means general property (ownership), not a special property (s 61); this distinction recurs
  under CRA s 4(1). Battersby & Preston (1972) 35 MLR 269 identify three elements in any transfer of
  property: the estate or interest transferred, the title to it, and the evidence of that title (see also
  Ho [1997] CLJ 371). the materials stresses that the SGA's concern with transferring general property must
  not be confused with a need for a perfect title: a seller with a lesser title can still make a sale
  (relevant to s 12(3) and nemo dat).
- **Conditional sale** (title retained until a condition, usually full payment, is met) *is* a contract of
  sale. **Hire-purchase** is *not*: it is bailment plus an option, and the hirer has not agreed to buy
  (*Helby v Matthews* [1895] AC 471 - hirer of a piano pledged it; no agreement to sell, SGA did not
  apply). **Credit sale** (property passes outright, price in instalments) *is* a sale. A transaction
  in the form of a sale but intended to operate as a mortgage, pledge, charge or other security is outside
  the Act's provisions on contracts of sale (s 62(4); the handout and slides label this "s 2(4)"); the
  slides note a chattel mortgage must be made by bill of sale under the Bills of Sale Act 1878.
- *PST Energy 7 Shipping v OW Bunker Malta (The Res Cogitans)* (2016) (revision guide): a bunker supply
  contract with a retention of title clause and a liberty to consume the fuel during the credit period was
  held by the Supreme Court not to be a contract of sale under s 2(1); Lord Mance rejected the idea that
  property passed a "nanosecond" before consumption. *Wood v TUI Travel* (2017) refused to extend this to
  all-inclusive holiday food: property in a meal passes when it is served. *Gregor Fisken Ltd v Carl* (2021):
  a contract for a Ferrari 250 GTO including the separated original gearbox was a sale of goods including
  the gearbox, not a services arrangement for finding it.

**(c) In goods.** s 61(1): all personal chattels other than things in action and money; includes emblements,
industrial growing crops and things attached to or forming part of the land agreed to be severed before
sale or under the contract. Goods must be property (not services), tangible (debts, shares, IP are things in
action) and non-monetary. The revision guide adds that cryptoassets are things in action, not goods, for
SGA purposes. Problem cases:
- *Land.* *Morgan v Russell* [1909] 1 KB 357: sale of as much slag and cinders on land as the buyer wanted,
  with no definite quantity, was a sale of an interest in land, not goods.
- *Coins.* *Moss v Hancock* [1899] 2 QB 111: a coin held as a curiosity worth more than its face value was
  goods, not money. CRA s 3(3)(a) excludes supplies of coins or notes for use as currency.
- *Software.* *St Albans City and DC v International Computers* [1996] 4 All ER 481 (obiter): the disk is
  goods but the program is not. *ASX Operation v Pont Data* (1990, Federal Court of Australia): software
  supplied electronically is not goods. *Beta Computers v Adobe* 1996 SLT 604 (Scotland) recognised the
  difficulties with *St Albans*. *Southwark LBC v IBM UK* [2011] EWHC 549 (TCC): a licence to use software,
  with title and copyright retained by the manufacturer, involved no transfer of property so the SGA did not
  apply; Akenhead J said obiter software on a CD could in principle be goods. *Computer Associates v The
  Software Incubator* [2018] EWCA Civ 518: CA held "goods" (in the Commercial Agents Directive 86/653/EEC)
  means tangible property so electronically supplied software is not goods; the Supreme Court referred
  questions to the CJEU (the revision guide refers to a 2021 decision in the case). The CRA's Chapter 3
  creates a separate regime for digital content supplied to consumers.
- *Body parts.* No clear answer. *Williams v Williams* (1882) 20 Ch D 659: no property in a dead body.
  *Dobson v North Tyneside HA* [1996] 4 All ER 474: next of kin could not recover body parts retained after
  a post-mortem; autopsy did not make them property. Human Tissue Act 2004 s 32 regulates supply of
  "controlled material". *A v National Blood Authority* [2001] 3 All ER 289: it was conceded contaminated
  blood was a "product" under the Consumer Protection Act 1987 s 45(1).
- *Work and materials v sale.* The test is the essence of the contract (*Robinson v Graves* [1935] 1 KB 579:
  portrait painting is a contract for the artist's skill, not goods). *Clay v Yates* (1856) 1 H & N 73:
  printing a book was work and materials. *Lee v Griffin* (1861) 1 B & S 272: making dentures was a sale of
  goods - the materials asks whether that is right. Work and materials contracts fall under SGSA 1982
  ss 2-5 and 12-16.
- *Existing and future goods* (s 5(1), s 61): a contract for future goods operates as an agreement to sell
  (s 5(3)). CRA s 5 is similar.
- *Specific and unascertained goods*: specific goods are identified and agreed on when the contract is made
  (s 61); unascertained goods are undefined but are all others. Under s 16 no property passes in
  unascertained goods until ascertained (subject to s 20A). "Ascertained" means identified in accordance with
  the agreement after the contract is made (Atkin LJ, *Re Wait* [1927]).

**(d) Money consideration (the price).** No money, no sale: a gift or barter/exchange is outside s 2(1)
(barter falls under SGSA 1982 ss 2-5).
- *Esso Petroleum v Customs & Excise Commissioners* [1976] 1 WLR 1: a "free" medallion given to buyers of four
  gallons of petrol was not sold (the majority differed on why; the revision guide puts it as the
  consideration being entry into the linked petrol contract, not money).
- *Aldridge v Johnson* (1857) 7 E & B 885: bullocks exchanged for barley, each lot priced and the balance
  paid in cash: two reciprocal contracts of sale. Valuing both lots was key.
- Payment by charge/credit card is still a money price: *Re Charge Card Services* [1989] Ch 497 (see
  Tiplady [1989] LMCLQ 22; Jones [1988] JBL 457).
- **Fixing the price (ss 8-9).** The price may be fixed by the contract, left to be fixed in a manner
  agreed by the contract, or determined by the course of dealing (s 8(1)); otherwise the buyer must pay a
  reasonable price, a question of fact (s 8(2)-(3)); s 9 covers agreements to sell at a third party's
  valuation. *May and Butcher v R* [1934] 2 KB 17 (HL): price
  "to be agreed from time to time" - no concluded contract. Contrast *Foley v Classique Coaches* [1934] 2 KB
  1 (CA): petrol to be bought at a price agreed from time to time - held a concluded contract. the materials asks whether reliance/performance explains the difference.
  See also *Sudbrook Trading Estate v Eggleton* [1983] 1 AC 444, *Mamidoil-Jetoil v Okta* [2003] EWCA Civ
  1031, *Gillatt v Sky Television* [2000] 1 All ER (Comm) 461.

### 3. Other transactions (revision guide Table 1.1)
| Transaction | Implied terms | Why not SGA |
|---|---|---|
| Exchange/barter | SGSA 1982 ss 2-5 | consideration not money |
| Hire | SGSA 1982 ss 6-11 | no transfer of property |
| Hire-purchase | Supply of Goods (Implied Terms) Act 1973 ss 8-11; CCA 1974 | no obligation to buy |
| Work and materials | SGSA 1982 ss 2-5, 12-16 | substance is the service |
For trader-to-consumer contracts these implied-term provisions have been replaced by the CRA.

### 4. The Consumer Rights Act 2015 (roadmap)
1. **Application.** Part 1 applies where there is an agreement between a trader and a consumer for the
   trader to supply goods, digital content or services, if the agreement is a contract (s 1).
2. **Definitions (s 2).** *Trader*: a person acting for purposes relating to their trade, business, craft
   or profession, personally or through someone acting in their name (s 2(2)). *Consumer*: an individual
   acting for purposes "wholly or mainly outside" their trade, business, craft or profession (s 2(3)); the
   trader must prove otherwise (s 2(4)). A buyer of second-hand goods at a public auction which individuals
   can attend in person is not a consumer for Chapter 2, save for certain sections (ss 11(4)-(5), 12, 28,
   29) (s 2(5)-(6)). *Goods*: tangible moveable items, including water, gas and electricity only if put up
   for supply in a limited volume or set quantity (s 2(8)). *Digital content*: data produced and supplied
   in digital form (s 2(9)).
3. **Chapter 2 contracts (s 3).** Sales contracts, hire, hire-purchase and contracts for transfer of goods
   (s 3(2)). Excluded: coins/notes as currency; sales by execution or authority of law; contracts intended
   as a mortgage, pledge, charge or other security; in England & Wales, deeds whose only consideration is
   that presumed by the deed (s 3(3)). Contracts may be between part owners, for an undivided share, and
   absolute or conditional (s 3(5)).
4. **Ownership (s 4).** Ownership means general property, not a special property (s 4(1)). The time of
   transfer is governed by SGA ss 16, 17, 18, 19, 20A and 20B (s 4(2)) - so the SGA property rules apply
   equally to consumer sales.
5. **Sales contract (s 5)**: includes existing and future goods and conditional sales; the consumer pays or
   agrees to pay the price (money consideration again).
6. **Implied terms** ss 9-18; **remedies** ss 19-26.
7. **Delivery (s 28)**: unless agreed otherwise, the trader must deliver; with no agreed time, without
   undue delay and not more than 30 days after the contract.
8. **Risk (s 29)**: goods stay at the trader's risk until they come into the physical possession of the
   consumer or a person identified by the consumer; carrier exceptions in s 29(3)-(4).
9. Schedule 1 makes consequential amendments to the SGA.

## Key authorities
- *Pfizer v Minister of Health* [1965] AC 512 - statutory supply is not a contract of sale.
- *NORWEB v Dixon* [1995] 3 All ER 952 - statutorily regulated electricity supply is not a contract.
- *Helby v Matthews* [1895] AC 471 - hire-purchase is not an agreement to sell.
- *Morgan v Russell* [1909] 1 KB 357 - indefinite quantity of slag: interest in land.
- *Moss v Hancock* [1899] 2 QB 111 - curio coin is goods.
- *St Albans v ICL* [1996] 4 All ER 481; *Southwark LBC v IBM* [2011] EWHC 549 (TCC); *Computer Associates
  v Software Incubator* [2018] EWCA Civ 518 - software.
- *Robinson v Graves* [1935] 1 KB 579; *Lee v Griffin* (1861); *Clay v Yates* (1856) - work and materials.
- *Esso v Customs & Excise* [1976] 1 WLR 1; *Aldridge v Johnson* (1857) - money consideration.
- *Re Charge Card Services* [1989] Ch 497 - card payment is money.
- *May and Butcher v R* [1934]; *Foley v Classique Coaches* [1934] - price uncertainty.
- *The Res Cogitans* (2016, UKSC) - fuel supply with ROT and liberty to consume not a sale.
- SGA ss 1, 2, 5, 8, 9, 16, 61, 62(2); CRA ss 1-5, 28, 29.

## Debate and critique
- **Fitness of a 19th-century definition for modern goods.** The software cases show the strain of a
  tangible-goods definition. The split approaches (disk v program in *St Albans*; licence v transfer in
  *Southwark*) produce arbitrary results depending on the delivery medium or the drafting. Parliament's
  answer for consumers was a separate digital content regime (CRA Ch 3) rather than extending "goods",
  leaving business software contracts uncertain.
- **The Res Cogitans** shows that commercial drafting (ROT plus liberty to consume) can take a transaction
  that looks like a sale outside the SGA altogether, with consequences for the seller's remedies (e.g. the
  action for the price). The revision guide notes the courts confined it in *Wood v TUI*.
- **Work and materials.** The "essence of the contract" test is unpredictable (*Lee v Griffin* dentures as
  goods v *Robinson v Graves* portrait as services); the materials invites you to question *Lee v Griffin*.
- **Price uncertainty.** *May and Butcher* v *Foley*: tension between certainty and upholding commercial
  bargains the parties have acted on; the materials suggests reliance may be the key to reconciling them.
- **Two regimes.** the materials asks why the CRA reuses the SGA property rules (s 4(2)) while creating its
  own implied terms and remedies. One reason is that property rules affect third parties (creditors,
  sub-buyers), so a single rule is needed, whereas quality and remedies are about protecting the weaker
  party. Consumer status ("wholly or mainly") and the reverse burden in s 2(4) favour the individual.
- Rodger (1992) 108 LQR 570 is set reading on Victorian codification.

## Applying it to a problem question
1. Is there a contract (voluntary agreement) or a statutory supply (*Pfizer*, *NORWEB*)?
2. Does the deal transfer, or bind the seller to transfer, general property? Check for hire, hire-purchase
   (option only), security transactions, licences (software), or ROT with liberty to consume (*Res
   Cogitans*).
3. Are the items "goods" (s 61)? Check land-related items (severance), coins, software, body parts.
4. Is the essence goods or work/services (*Robinson v Graves*)? If services, SGSA 1982.
5. Is the consideration money, wholly or partly (*Aldridge v Johnson*; *Esso*)? Barter is SGSA.
6. Is the price fixed, fixable or left to agreement (ss 8-9; *May and Butcher* v *Foley*)?
7. Is the seller a trader and the buyer a consumer (CRA s 2)? If yes, CRA Chapter 2 for implied terms,
   remedies, delivery (s 28) and risk (s 29); SGA ss 16-20B for property. If B2B (or consumer to consumer),
   SGA throughout.
8. Classify goods as specific/unascertained and existing/future now - you will need it for property, risk,
   s 6 and s 7.
Traps: assuming every supply is a sale; forgetting that a business person can be a consumer; ignoring the
auction exclusion in CRA s 2(5).
