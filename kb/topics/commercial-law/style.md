# Commercial Law: answer rules
- Nemo dat: cover the Factors Act 1889 provisions as well as the SGA exceptions.
- Passing of property: use the specific rule — e.g. **s 18 r 2** SGA (specific goods to be put into a deliverable state).
- Consumer buyers: apply the **Consumer Rights Act 2015** (e.g. **s 29**, risk passes on delivery to the consumer), not
  the SGA.
- Implied terms and remedies: also apply **s 28** SGA (payment and delivery concurrent conditions); time of delivery
  (s 10(2); *Bunge v Tradax*; *Rickards v Oppenheim*); rejection (s 11(3)).
- Risk and frustration essays: analyse the **difference** between risk (s 20) and frustration/perishing (s 7), use
  scholars in the main body, and limit headings in a short essay.

## Learned from testing
- Test whether a person or their company is 'connected' separately under each statute in play, because CA 2006 ss 252–254 and IA 1986 ss 249 and 435 define it differently (e.g. a sibling is an associate under IA 1986 s 435 but not connected under CA 2006 s 253); never carry the conclusion across regimes. [2026-11-24 · ref 2026-11-24-1510/q05]
- Where the counterparty is connected under IA 1986 s 249, apply the 2-year look-back for preferences (s 240(1)(a)), the presumed desire to prefer (s 239(6)) and, for transactions at an undervalue, the presumed insolvency (s 240(2)). [2026-11-24 · ref 2026-11-24-1510/q05]
- When advising a director about a co-director's wrongdoing, first test whether his own failure to monitor and supervise breaches the duty of care in s 174 CA 2006 before treating his ignorance as a defence. [2026-11-24 · ref 2026-11-24-1510/q05]
